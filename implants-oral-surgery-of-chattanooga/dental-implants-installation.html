Dental Implants Installation

Dental implants have been around for over 40 years, and over the years they become the most successful method of replacing missing and lost teeth. Dental implants offer a ton of advantages, from a long lifetime and high comfort to the proven strengthening of the bone into which it was placed. For years, there has been one primary thing that discourages people from making implants their teeth replacement choice. The dental implants installation procedure.

You’d be surprised to know that dental implants installation is actually a quite simple process. In addition, other dental procedures such as root canals may be more difficult and more time-consuming. So, let’s explore the procedure to put your mind at ease.

Steps of Dental Implants Installation

It is good to know that completing the following steps takes around 45 minutes on average. Here is how it usually proceeds:

  • Anesthesia The area receiving the implant is numbed with local anesthesia. General anesthesia is sometimes used if multiple implants are placed at once.
  • Gaining Access to the Bone This is done with a small incision – usually a few millimeters – so that the bone is ready for the procedure.
  • Drilling A series of drills are used to create a small opening in the bone, about the same size as the chosen implant.
  • Implant Insertion The implant is inserted very slowly so that it adapts into the created hole.
  • Wound Cleaning The incision is irrigated with warm saline.
  • Wound Closure You’ll receive a few stitches, which are removed 7 to 10 days later.

Dental Implant Procedure Modifications

In some cases, the bone is not ready to directly receive the implant, and some modifications are needed. These are usually very minor and done at the same time as the implant procedure such as:

  • Extraction of problematic teeth.
  • Bone augmentation in cases when the bone is insufficient.
  • Alveoplasty, or bone modifications in cases of protrusions in the bone or abnormal bone shape.

The Prosthetic Phase

A few months later, the implant is again exposed and we take a series of steps to make a mold of the inserted implant. A crown is then fitted over the implant, and you’re good to go.

In most cases, dental implants installation is quite a simple process with hardly any complications or aftereffects. If you like to learn more, give us a call and schedule your consultation with one of our experts.
