Dental Implants in Bethlehem, PA

Because the post fuses with your jaw, an implant does something no bridge or denture can: it keeps the bone alive. When a tooth is lost, the bone that held it starts to shrink from disuse. An implant loads that bone the way a natural root does, which is why implants are the only replacement that prevents the sunken look that can follow long-term tooth loss.

  • The abutment. A small connector that links the post to the visible tooth.
  • The crown. A custom porcelain tooth, matched to your smile, attached on top. This is the only part anyone ever sees.

Why Patients Choose Implants

  • They stay put. Eat corn on the cob, steak, apples. Nothing shifts, nothing comes out at night.
  • They protect your other teeth. A bridge requires grinding down two healthy neighbors. An implant stands on its own.
  • They preserve your jawbone and face shape. No other option does this.
  • They last. With good hygiene, the implant post itself can last decades, often a lifetime. Crowns may be replaced after many years of wear, but the foundation stays.
  • They are easy to live with. Brush and floss like a normal tooth. No adhesives, no soaking, no special routine.

One Tooth, Several Teeth, or a Full Arch

Replacing one tooth. One implant plus one crown. The gold standard for a single missing tooth, and the option that leaves every neighboring tooth untouched.

Replacing several teeth. Implants can anchor a bridge, so three or four missing teeth might need only two implants. No healthy teeth get ground down to carry the load.

Securing a full arch. A handful of implants can lock a full denture firmly in place. For patients who hate a loose denture, implant-supported dentures are usually the life-changing option: stronger chewing, no slipping, and no denture covering the roof of your mouth. The Implant Process, Step by Step

Implant treatment is a journey measured in months, not days, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something. Here is the honest timeline:

Throughout the process, your care is coordinated in one familiar office by a dentist who knows your whole mouth, with any specialist involvement planned and explained upfront rather than discovered along the way.

  • Consultation and 3D planning. Dr. Tunney examines your mouth, takes a CBCT scan (a quick, in-office 3D image of your jaw), and reviews your health history. The scan shows your bone, nerves, and sinuses in three dimensions, so you get a definite answer about whether an implant will work, not a maybe. You see everything on the chairside screens, and you leave with a clear plan and a written estimate.
  • Any groundwork first. If a failing tooth needs extraction , or gum disease needs treatment, that happens before implant placement. Some patients need bone grafting if the jaw has thinned; you will know upfront if that applies to you.
  • Guided implant placement. Using your CBCT scan, Dr. Tunney plans the exact position, angle, and depth of your implant digitally before surgery. A custom surgical guide made from that plan directs the placement, so the implant goes precisely where it was planned. Guided surgery typically means smaller incisions, a shorter procedure, and an easier recovery. Most patients describe recovery as easier than a tooth extraction, managed with over-the-counter pain relievers for a few days.
  • Healing and fusion. Over roughly three to six months, the bone fuses to the post (the technical term is osseointegration). You are never left with a visible gap; temporary options cover the site while you heal.
  • The new tooth. Once the post is solid, Dr. Tunney attaches the abutment and your custom porcelain crown, shaped and shaded to disappear among your natural teeth. Then you go eat something you have been avoiding.

What About Bone Grafting?

You may need a bone graft sounds alarming, so here is the plain-English version. An implant post needs enough bone to hold it, the way a fence post needs enough ground. If a tooth has been missing for years, or gum disease thinned the bone, the site may need rebuilding first. A graft adds bone material to the area, your body integrates it over a few months, and then the implant proceeds normally.

It is a common, routine step, not a complication, and plenty of patients need no grafting at all. Your CBCT scan answers the question before anything is scheduled, and if grafting applies to you, it appears in your written plan with its own timeline and cost. Nothing about it gets discovered halfway through.

Implant vs. Bridge vs. Denture: The Honest Comparison

There is no universally right answer; there is a right answer for your mouth, your health, and your budget. Dr. Tunney will lay the options side by side with real numbers and let you decide without pressure. Sometimes he will tell you the bridge or the denture is the smarter choice for you; that honesty is exactly why you can trust the recommendation when it is the implant.

  • Dental implant. Highest upfront cost, longest lifespan, only option that preserves jawbone, and the closest feel to a natural tooth. No impact on neighboring teeth. Needs surgery and months of healing.
  • Fixed bridge. Moderate cost, no surgery, finished in weeks. Requires grinding down the two neighboring teeth to anchor it, does nothing for the bone underneath, and typically needs replacement every 10 to 15 years.
  • Removable denture (full or partial). Lowest upfront cost and no surgery, but it can shift, needs relines as the jaw changes, comes out at night, and does not stop bone loss.

Caring for Your Implant (It Is Easy)

Implants cannot get cavities, but the gum and bone around them still need defending: an inflamed implant site (the technical term is peri-implantitis) is the main threat to a healthy implant. The defense is boring and familiar. Brush twice a day, floss around the implant daily, and keep your six-month cleanings, where we check the implant, the crown, and the gum around it every visit. Do that, and decades is a realistic expectation.

Am I a Candidate for Dental Implants?

Most healthy adults are. The real requirements are:

Age alone almost never disqualifies anyone; we evaluate patients well into their 80s. The only way to know for sure is an exam and a CBCT scan, and Dr. Tunney will tell you plainly if an implant is NOT your best option. That honesty is the reason patients trust the recommendation when it is.

  • Enough healthy jawbone to hold the post. If bone has thinned, grafting can often rebuild it.
  • Healthy gums. Active gum disease has to be treated first, because implants need the same healthy foundation teeth do.
  • Good general health. Conditions like uncontrolled diabetes or heavy smoking slow healing and lower success rates. They do not automatically rule implants out, but they need honest discussion.
