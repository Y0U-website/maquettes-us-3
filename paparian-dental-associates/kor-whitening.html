What makes KöR results durable — and what “permanent” really means

When clinicians describe KöR results as long-lasting, they’re referring to the way the system removes or alters stain molecules deep within the tooth structure. By addressing intrinsic discoloration (stains inside the tooth), KöR reduces the tooth’s capacity to recollect pigments that cause visible darkening. That foundational change is what distinguishes lasting improvement from temporary brightening.

“Permanent” in this context does not mean teeth will never darken again, but rather that the teeth have been effectively restored to a cleaner baseline that resists re-staining. Lifestyle factors — like coffee, tea, red wine, and tobacco — can gradually tint teeth over time. The benefit of KöR is that with minimal, occasional touch-up care at home, many patients maintain their improved shade for years rather than months.

Routine maintenance is straightforward and noninvasive: intermittent overnight wear of the custom trays with a maintenance gel can preserve results. Because the primary whitening action has already altered staining within the enamel and dentin, these periodic refreshes are typically effective and simple to incorporate into an oral-care routine.

Comfort and safety: what research and experience tell us

Safety and patient comfort are central to KöR’s design. The whitening gels used in the system are clinically studied and formulated to minimize common side effects such as sensitivity. One contributing factor is the way the gels are handled — refrigerated storage and careful preparation reduce the need for chemical stabilizers that can irritate tooth nerves and soft tissues.

Most patients report mild or no sensitivity with KöR compared with traditional high-concentration treatments. When sensitivity does occur, it is generally short-lived and manageable with standard at-home strategies recommended by dental professionals. Additionally, the system’s emphasis on custom-fit trays helps limit gel contact with the gums, further reducing the chance of irritation.

Clinically, KöR is considered safe for a broad range of patients when used as directed. Your dental provider will review your dental health and treatment goals to confirm suitability. Those with extensive restorations or specific dental conditions receive a tailored recommendation so whitening is performed thoughtfully and safely.

How the KöR process fits into your life: what to expect in office and at home

The KöR program typically combines a short, comfortable in-office component with a home-based phase that fits into everyday life. The in-office visit(s) prepare and optimize the teeth for the at-home therapy, while custom impressions are taken to create ultra-comfortable KöR-Seal™ trays. These thin, form-fitting trays hold the whitening gel against the teeth throughout the overnight wear period.

Most patients wear their KöR-Seal trays while they sleep for approximately two weeks, although specific protocols can vary depending on initial staining and the selected KöR system. The trays are engineered to remain secure during sleep and to trap the gel inside so it does not pool or leak onto the gums, supporting both effectiveness and comfort.

Because the regimen happens largely overnight, it is easy to maintain with a busy schedule. Brief office appointments for monitoring or single-session boosts are available when recommended, but the core work occurs in a series of nightly applications that are typically discreet and low hassle.

How does KöR penetrate stains inside the tooth?

KöR works by delivering active oxygen molecules that diffuse into enamel and dentin where intrinsic stains reside, altering or removing stain compounds at their source. The gels are formulated and stored to maintain maximum activity so that peroxide molecules remain effective when applied in the custom trays. This molecular-level action contrasts with treatments that primarily affect only surface discoloration.

Custom impressions produce ultra-thin, well-sealed trays that optimize gel contact and prevent pooling on the gums, which enhances penetration into the tooth. The overnight wear protocol used with many KöR systems provides extended contact time, increasing the opportunity for active oxygen to reach deeper stain sites. As a result, patients often see lightening that appears more integrated and even across treated teeth.

Refrigerated storage and careful handling of the gels limit the need for chemical stabilizers that can irritate tooth nerves and soft tissues. Custom trays also reduce gum exposure to gel, lowering the chance of soft-tissue irritation.

Many patients report minimal or no sensitivity compared with some traditional in-office high-concentration treatments, and any transient sensitivity is generally manageable with standard at-home measures recommended by dental professionals. Your clinician will assess tooth and gum health before treatment and recommend desensitizing strategies if you have a history of sensitivity. Thoughtful monitoring during the treatment sequence helps keep the procedure comfortable and safe for each patient.

Who is a good candidate for KöR Whitening and who should avoid it?

Good candidates are adults with natural teeth who have extrinsic or intrinsic discoloration and who maintain generally healthy gums and enamel. KöR can be effective for a variety of stains, including some deeper intrinsic discolorations that do not respond well to surface-only treatments. A dental exam is important to confirm there are no untreated cavities, gum disease or other conditions that should be addressed before whitening.

Certain patients, such as those with extensive restorations on front teeth or untreated dental disease, may require an alternative or a carefully staged plan that includes restorative work. Pregnant or breastfeeding patients and some individuals with specific dental conditions should consult their clinician before beginning whitening. Your provider will review your medical and dental history and recommend the safest, most appropriate approach for your smile.

How does KöR address intrinsic stains such as medication-related discoloration?

Intrinsic stains, including those related to certain medications, form within enamel and dentin and are often more resistant to surface-level treatments. KöR’s approach is designed to reach deeper stain molecules by preserving peroxide potency and using prolonged contact under custom trays, which increases the potential for change within the tooth structure. While outcomes vary by the type and age of the stain, KöR often achieves improvement where other methods have been less effective.

For some medication-related stains, a carefully managed treatment plan with realistic expectations is essential; multiple cycles or combined restorative options may be discussed if needed. Your clinician will evaluate the nature of the discoloration and explain likely results, possible timelines and any supplemental procedures that could enhance aesthetics. Thoughtful planning helps ensure a balanced, natural-looking outcome tailored to each patient’s situation.

Will crowns, veneers or fillings change color with KöR Whitening?

No, dental restorations such as crowns, veneers and composite fillings do not whiten with bleaching gels because they are made of materials that do not respond to peroxide the way natural tooth structure does. This means restorations that are visible when you smile may need to be evaluated to match the new shade of natural teeth after whitening is complete. Your clinician will discuss sequencing options to achieve a harmonious appearance across both natural teeth and restorations.

If you have existing restorations on your front teeth, your provider may recommend completing whitening first and then assessing whether any restorations require replacement or modification to match the improved tooth shade. Planning restorations after whitening helps avoid color mismatch and creates a more uniform smile. The practice will outline restorative considerations as part of your personalized treatment plan.
