Types of Interceptive Treatments We May Use

At McNeal Orthodontics, we lean toward a “wait and watch” approach when it comes to interceptive treatment, stepping in only when the timing is right. When needed, we use gentle, effective options like braces and 3D printed holding appliances to guide healthy growth while avoiding unnecessary appliances or active treatments unless they’re truly required. This helps ensure your child gets the right care at the right time, without overdoing it.

Growth & Development Monitoring

Our primary focus is on closely watching your child’s growth over time to determine the best moment for treatment, if it’s needed at all. This careful approach helps avoid unnecessary procedures and ensures we act only when it will be most effective.

Dental Monitoring With Monthly Scans

Using Dental Monitoring technology, we can check your child’s progress remotely with simple monthly scans from home. This allows us to track changes in real time, reduce in-office visits, and make timely decisions without interrupting your family’s routine.

Selective Primary Tooth Extractions

In some cases, removing certain baby teeth at the right time can help guide permanent teeth into better positions. This can prevent future issues, like impacted teeth, and may reduce the need for more complex treatment later on.

Fixed Holding Appliances

These small, comfortable appliances help hold space for incoming adult teeth after baby teeth are lost. By keeping everything properly aligned as your child grows, they can help prevent crowding and reduce the need for more involved treatment down the road.

Limited Interceptive Braces

In specific cases, we may recommend a short phase of braces to address a developing issue early. This targeted approach focuses only on the area of concern, helping guide proper development without committing to full treatment too soon.

Occasional Use of Expanders

Expanders are used only in select cases where there is a true need for jaw development assistance, such as significant upper jaw narrowing. We reserve this option for when it’s truly necessary to support proper function and long-term oral health.

What To Expect During Interceptive Treatment

Interceptive care at McNeal Orthodontics is designed to be simple, supportive, and carefully timed around your child’s development. Not every child needs immediate treatment, and our goal is to guide growth and step in only when it will make the greatest impact.

Your child’s first visit includes a complimentary, in-depth evaluation using advanced 3D imaging. Dr. McNeal assesses growth patterns and identifies any developing concerns, providing clear guidance on whether treatment is needed now or if monitoring is the best next step.

If your child isn’t ready for treatment, we’ll monitor their development over time. This allows us to track growth and begin treatment at the ideal stage, rather than too early or too late. We typically start monitoring every 6 months and then may transition to monthly remote monitoring via the Dental Monitoring app.

If active treatment is recommended, a customized plan is created based on your child’s unique development. Every step is designed to support healthy growth and long-term success. During active treatment, progress is monitored closely, with adjustments made as needed to keep everything on track.

Frequently Asked Questions About Interceptive Treatment

What age should my child see an orthodontist?

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends an evaluation around age 7. At this stage, Dr. McNeal can evaluate your child’s development and determine whether active treatment or monitoring is the best next step.

Does every child need interceptive treatment?

No, not every child requires interceptive treatment. Some may simply need monitoring until the right time for care. A professional evaluation with Dr. McNeal can help determine the best approach for your child’s unique development.

How long does interceptive treatment take?

Treatment length varies depending on your child’s growth and development stage, but many Phase 1 treatments last between 9 and 12 months. Afterward, your child may enter a monitoring phase before any additional treatment is considered, allowing us to determine the ideal timing for future care if needed.

Will my child still need braces later?

Yes, in most cases, children will still need future braces treatment , even if they received active interceptive treatment.

Is interceptive treatment comfortable for kids?

While some kids may experience pressure at first, modern orthodontic appliances are designed with comfort in mind. Dr. McNeal takes a gentle, personalized approach to ensure your child feels supported and comfortable throughout the process.
