Platelet-Rich Fibrin

Platelet-rich fibrin, or PRF, uses a small sample of your own blood to support natural healing after surgery. When placed at the treatment site, PRF can help encourage tissue repair, reduce inflammation, and support a smoother recovery process.

Sinus Lift

For patients who need dental implants in the upper back jaw, a sinus lift can help create the supportive foundation needed for long-term success. This procedure gently adds bone beneath the sinus cavity, making implant placement possible when natural bone volume is limited.

Oral Pathology

Changes in the mouth, jaw, or soft tissues should always be evaluated with care. Dr. Consky can assess concerning spots, growths, lesions, or other abnormalities and determine whether further testing or biopsy is needed for a clear diagnosis and next steps.

Opioid-Free Pain Control

With Exparel , we can offer long-lasting pain relief without relying on opioids after certain procedures. This advanced option helps reduce discomfort during recovery while supporting a safer, more comfortable healing experience.

Pediatric Patients

We provide oral surgery care for children and teens with a gentle, thoughtful approach. Our team takes time to explain each step, support anxious patients, and create an experience that feels as comfortable and reassuring as possible for both kids and parents.

3D Imaging

Our 3D imaging technology gives us a complete, detailed view of your oral anatomy before treatment begins. By seeing more than traditional X-rays can show, we’re able to create a more personalized surgical plan and approach your care with added accuracy.

The Oral Surgery Treatment Process

Treatment Planning & Preparation

Treatment Planning & Preparation

Your consultation is typically scheduled on a separate day from your surgery so you have time to ask questions, review your treatment plan, and feel fully prepared before your procedure. During this appointment, Dr. Consky will walk you through your recommended treatment, explain what to expect on the day of surgery, and review how to prepare.

Day of Surgery

Day of Surgery

If you are undergoing IV or oral sedation, you will need to arrange for someone to drive you to and from your appointment, stay in the office during your procedure, and remain with you for the rest of the day after surgery. Dr. Consky will provide specific instructions based on your treatment plan, but in general, you should plan to take the day off and avoid driving, working, or making important decisions while the sedation wears off.

Frequently Asked Questions

What’s an oral surgeon?

An oral and maxillofacial surgeon completes specialized training and education beyond dental school. Some oral surgeons also choose to pursue both a medical degree and a dental degree, allowing them to bring a deeper understanding of medicine, dentistry, anesthesia, and surgical care to their patients.

At a minimum, oral surgeons complete a hospital-based surgical residency alongside medical residents in many different specialties, including anesthesia. Dr. Consky has both a medical degree and a dental degree, is licensed in both medicine and dentistry in the state of Georgia, and completed a six-year residency at Emory.

How long does oral surgery take?

It completely depends on the procedure and the complexity of your case. Simpler procedures, such as tooth extractions, can take minutes; more time may be needed to properly sedate you. Complex procedures, like placing dental implants for a full arch or teeth, can take hours. Your oral surgeon will give you a thorough explanation of the procedure, tell you how to properly prepare, and let you know what to expect on the day.

How much does oral surgery cost?

The cost of oral surgery varies from procedure to procedure. Similar to time commitment, simpler surgeries will be less expensive and complex surgeries will be more expensive. If your dental and/or medical insurance does not cover your oral surgery, consider loans or a payment plan to get the care you need.

Is oral surgery covered by insurance?

Possibly, depending on your insurance provider and coverage. You should talk to your dental and medical insurance providers to see if your recommended oral surgery is covered. It may also be worthwhile to file your claim with both your dental insurance provider and your medical insurance provider, since the surgery may qualify as a medically necessary procedure.
