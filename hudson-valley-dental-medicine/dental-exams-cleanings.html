What to Expect During a Dental Cleaning

A dental cleaning is an important part of maintaining good oral health. Here’s what happens during a cleaning:

A dental cleaning at Hudson Valley Dental Medicine is usually comfortable and takes about 30 to 60 minutes. It’s a simple yet effective way to keep your teeth and gums healthy.

  • Plaque and Tartar Removal: The hygienist will use special tools to remove plaque and tartar from your teeth. This process is called scaling. Removing these substances helps prevent cavities and gum disease.
  • Polishing: After scaling, your teeth will be polished using a gritty toothpaste and a special brush. Polishing helps remove any remaining plaque and stains, making your teeth smooth and shiny.
  • Flossing: The hygienist will floss between your teeth to remove any plaque or food particles that were missed during brushing and scaling. This step ensures your teeth are thoroughly clean.
  • Rinsing: You will be asked to rinse your mouth to wash away any debris from the cleaning process.
  • Fluoride Treatment: Depending on your needs, the dentist may apply a fluoride treatment to your teeth. Fluoride helps strengthen your enamel and protect against cavities.

How Often Should You Get Dental Exams and Cleanings?

The American Dental Association recommends that most people visit the dentist for exams and cleanings every six months. However, Dr. Turturro at Hudson Valley Dental Medicine might suggest a different schedule based on your individual needs. For example, if you have a history of cavities or gum disease, you might need to come in more often.

Regular visits help catch problems early, making treatment easier and more effective. If you’re unsure how often you should visit, talk to Dr. Turturro. He can provide a personalized recommendation based on your oral health.

Risks and Side Effects of Dental Cleanings

Dental cleanings are generally safe, but some people may experience minor side effects. These can include:

These side effects are usually mild and go away quickly. Regular cleanings help prevent more serious dental problems, making any minor discomfort well worth it.

  • Sensitivity: Your teeth may feel more sensitive to hot and cold for a few days after the cleaning. This is usually temporary.
  • Sore Gums: Your gums might feel a little sore or bleed slightly after the cleaning. This should go away within a day or two.
  • Discomfort: Some people may feel a bit uncomfortable during the cleaning, especially if they have a lot of plaque or tartar buildup. Let your dentist know if you’re feeling pain; they can make adjustments to help you feel more comfortable.

Tips for Maintaining Oral Health Between Visits

To keep your teeth and gums healthy between dental visits, follow these tips:

By following these simple steps, you can help maintain your oral health and reduce the need for dental treatments.

  • Brush Twice a Day: Use a fluoride toothpaste and brush your teeth for two minutes each time. Be sure to brush all surfaces of your teeth, including the front, back, and chewing surfaces.
  • Floss Daily: Flossing removes plaque and food particles from between your teeth and along the gumline, areas your toothbrush can’t reach.
  • Use Mouthwash: An antimicrobial mouthwash can help reduce plaque and prevent gum disease. Look for one with fluoride for added protection.
  • Eat a Balanced Diet: Avoid sugary snacks and drinks, which can lead to cavities. Instead, choose healthy foods like fruits, vegetables, and dairy products that help keep your teeth strong.
  • Stay Hydrated: Drinking water helps wash away food particles and keeps your mouth moist, reducing the risk of cavities and gum disease.
