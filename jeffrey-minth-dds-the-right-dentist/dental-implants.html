Dental implants are considered the gold standard in tooth replacement because they replace both the visible tooth and the root beneath the gumline, creating a restoration that looks, feels, and functions like a natural tooth.

What Are Dental Implants?

A dental implant is a small titanium post that is surgically placed into the jawbone where a tooth is missing. As the implant heals, it naturally fuses with the bone through a process called osseointegration, creating a stable foundation for a custom dental crown, bridge, or denture.

Unlike traditional tooth replacement options that rest on the gums or rely on neighboring teeth for support, implants become part of your smile, providing exceptional stability and long-term durability.

Implant Solutions for Every Smile

Every patient has different needs, which is why we offer implant solutions designed around your individual smile.

Single Tooth Implants

A single implant replaces one missing tooth without affecting the healthy teeth beside it. A custom crown is attached to the implant, restoring both function and appearance.

Implant-Supported Bridges

When several teeth are missing in a row, implant-supported bridges provide a secure solution without relying on adjacent natural teeth for support.

Implant-Supported Dentures

Patients missing most or all of their teeth can enjoy greater stability and comfort with implant-supported dentures. These restorations reduce slipping, improve chewing ability, and provide a more secure fit than traditional removable dentures.

Why Replace Missing Teeth?

Many people assume that losing a tooth is simply a cosmetic issue. In reality, missing teeth can lead to several oral health problems over time.

Without a tooth root stimulating the jawbone, the bone begins to shrink, which may affect your facial appearance and make future treatment more complex. Missing teeth can also cause neighboring teeth to shift, resulting in bite problems and uneven wear.

Replacing missing teeth with dental implants helps preserve the health and function of your entire smile while reducing the risk of future complications.
