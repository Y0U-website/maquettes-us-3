Why Orthodontic Retainers Matter

After orthodontic treatment, teeth naturally tend to shift back toward their original positions. This is because the bone and soft tissues around your teeth need time to stabilize after movement. Orthodontic retainers play a critical role in maintaining your new alignment and protecting your investment in your smile.

Without consistent use, it’s possible for teeth to relapse — even slightly — which can undo months or years of treatment progress. At JW Dentistry, we ensure every patient leaves with a retainer that fits perfectly and complements their treatment goals.

There isn’t a one-size-fits-all approach to retention. At JW Dentistry, Dr. Sohn offers several types of retainers, each designed to meet different needs, preferences, and lifestyles.

These are easy to take out for eating, brushing, and cleaning. The two most common types are:

Made of acrylic and wire, these are durable and adjustable.

Crafted from transparent, comfortable plastic that fits snugly over your teeth — similar to clear aligners.

Also known as bonded or permanent retainers, these are thin wires discreetly attached to the back of your front teeth. They stay in place to provide constant support and are a great option for patients who prefer not to worry about removing or replacing their retainer.

Caring For Your Retainer

Proper care is key to keeping your retainer clean, fresh, and functional. Here are a few simple guidelines:

Clean removable retainers daily with a soft toothbrush and mild soap (avoid toothpaste, as it can be abrasive)

Rinse your retainer after each use to prevent plaque buildup

Store it in a protective case when not in use to prevent damage or loss

Avoid heat exposure, such as leaving it in a hot car, which can cause warping

Bring your retainer to every dental visit for inspection and cleaning

Adjusting To Life With A Retainer

Wearing a retainer consistently is the best way to protect your smile. Most patients wear their removable retainers full-time at first, then transition to nighttime wear as their bite stabilizes. For fixed retainers, daily brushing and flossing around the wire are important to keep the area clean and free of plaque.

It’s normal to experience mild tightness or pressure when you first start wearing your retainer — this simply means it’s working. Dr. Sohn and our team will review detailed care instructions with you to make sure you feel confident about maintaining your results.

How Long You’ll Need To Wear A Retainer

Retention is a lifelong process because teeth can shift naturally over time due to aging and everyday habits like chewing or grinding. While every case is unique, most patients wear their retainers consistently for at least 6–12 months after treatment, then continue nighttime wear indefinitely to maintain alignment.

Dr. Sohn monitors your smile during routine visits and will let you know when adjustments or replacements are needed. With consistent use, your smile will remain straight, healthy, and beautiful for years to come.
