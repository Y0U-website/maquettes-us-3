What is a tooth extraction?

Simple extraction

This is the removal of a tooth that is visible in your mouth, meaning it is above the gumline. This type of extraction can be performed with local anesthesia to the tooth and gum tissue and the dentist removes the tooth with specialized tools.

Surgical extraction

When you need more than one tooth extracted

In extreme cases of severe gum disease and tooth decay, it may be necessary to remove multiple teeth at a time. In other cases, removing every tooth is necessary to address severe gum disease and infection and restore your oral health.

Multiple tooth extraction typically requires general anesthesia and can take a couple of hours to complete. The surgeon may recommend bone graft procedures in order to help maintain bone health in the jaw after all the teeth are removed. Dr. Curtis Chu will discuss your options during your initial visit.

When does a tooth require extraction?

While dentists work to save and repair teeth when they can, sometimes the damage is too severe, and the tooth must be removed. In addition, there are other occasions when a tooth extraction is necessary. These can include:

Removal of a tooth is necessary for orthodontic treatments.

Wisdom teeth are impacted, coming in crooked, or are pushing other teeth out of the way.

Decayed teeth can affect overall health

Tooth extractions help preserve your remaining teeth

Tooth extractions are recommended when a tooth is beyond repair, is impacted or unable to erupt, or is causing overcrowding and damage to the surrounding teeth.

What can you expect after the procedure?

After the tooth is removed, the surgeon applies gauze to the site. This gauze helps to control the bleeding and promote clot formation. The clot helps to keep bacteria from entering the open socket and exposing nerve and bone. During the first 24 hours, it is essential to avoid rinsing, brushing the area around the extraction, smoking, or drinking through a straw in order to help ensure the clot remains in place.

If it becomes dislodged, you run the risk of dry socket. This painful condition occurs when the nerves and bone are exposed. You can expect some swelling and discomfort but applying ice can help relieve this.

The recovery period can take a few days and it could take a couple weeks before the extraction site fully heals.

What does the procedure involve?

A tooth extraction procedure will vary depending on the condition of the tooth and whether a simple extraction or a surgical extraction is required. For a simple extraction, the area is numbed, and, with special tools, the tooth is removed.

For more complex extractions, such as wisdom teeth, surgical removal to expose the tooth is necessary. For this, you may require general anesthesia before the surgeon cuts the gum to expose the tooth for removal.

What can I do to promote gum healing and speed up healing time?

After your tooth extraction, it is important to follow all your after-care instructions. Keep the gauze in place for a minimum of 1-2 hours in order to allow time for a clot to form.

Make sure you take a day or two to rest and allow your body time to heal. Applying ice can help reduce swelling and discomfort as needed. Ice should be applied in the first 48 hours.
