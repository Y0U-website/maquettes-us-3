What this means for your experience

  • Greater accuracy. The implants we place with robotic guidance sit closer to the planned position than freehand placement allows.
  • Less invasive. Many Yomi cases are flapless. No incisions, no sutures, less bleeding.
  • Smoother healing. Smaller surgical sites mean less swelling and a more comfortable recovery.
  • Real-time tracking. If you shift in the chair, the robot follows. The plan stays accurate even with movement.
  • Adaptable. Unlike rigid static guides, Yomi allows adjustments during the procedure if conditions call for it.
  • One standard, every case. Robotic guidance is not reserved for select procedures at our practice. Every implant, whether a single tooth, multiple teeth, or a full arch, is placed with Yomi.

How Yomi compares to other approaches

Patients researching implants encounter three approaches: freehand placement, static surgical guides (3D-printed templates that seat over the teeth), and robotic guidance. We know all three from extensive firsthand experience, and we chose robotic guidance for every implant. Here is how they compare.

Yomi s place in modern dentistry

Yomi is a relatively new and uncommon technology. Approximately 200 Yomi systems have been installed in dental practices nationally, and the platform has assisted in more than 100,000 osteotomies (the precise drilling of the implant site) since its FDA clearance in 2017. The latest generation of the system, Yomi S, received FDA clearance in late 2025.

Practices that adopt Yomi typically do so, as we did, because of the precision and consistency it brings to implant care. We are proud to make this level of technology available to our patients.

Common questions about Yomi

Is the robot performing the surgery?

No. Your dentist is performing the procedure. Yomi is a guidance system, similar in concept to how GPS guides a driver. The doctor controls every motion of the drill, and the robot is designed to hold that motion on the planned path.

Is robotic implant surgery safe?

Yomi is FDA-cleared specifically for dental implant surgery. Robotic-assisted procedures have been used safely in medicine for decades and have been used in dentistry since Yomi s first FDA clearance in 2017. Robotic guidance lets us place an implant closer to the planned position than working freehand does.

Will my case be done with Yomi?

Yes. Every dental implant at our practice is placed with Yomi robotic guidance. It is our standard of care for implant surgery, whether you are receiving one implant or a full arch. At your consultation, we will walk you through exactly what that looks like for your treatment.

Do you use surgical guides?

No. Static surgical guides, the 3D-printed templates that seat over the teeth and direct the drill, are a sound technology, and Dr. Jackson personally designed and fabricated hundreds of them earlier in his career. Today, every implant we place is guided robotically instead. Yomi carries the same fidelity to the digital plan, adds real-time tracking if you move during the procedure, and allows adjustment during the procedure when conditions call for it. A static guide cannot offer those.

Practices that invest in Yomi typically prioritize digital precision and the long-term outcomes that better implant placement produces.
