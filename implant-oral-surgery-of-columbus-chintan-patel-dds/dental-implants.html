Overview of Implant Placement

Dental implant placement is a carefully planned surgical procedure completed in stages. The first step involves placing a small titanium post into the lower jaw or upper jaw at the implant site. This post serves as an artificial tooth root and provides a stable foundation for replacement teeth.

During implant surgery, local anesthesia is used to keep you comfortable. In some cases, additional sedation options may be appropriate based on treatment complexity and patient comfort. After placement, the healing process allows the implant to fuse with the surrounding bone, supporting long-term success rates and stability.

Once healing is complete, a second procedure may be scheduled to attach a connector that supports the final restoration. A temporary crown or temporary tooth may be placed while the gum tissue heals, followed by the final restoration that restores function and appearance.

Dental implant placement supports the function of natural teeth, protects oral health, and offers a reliable alternative to removable dentures for patients seeking a secure tooth replacement.

Same Day Teeth with Dental Implants in Columbus

Full Arch Solutions Using All-on-4 / All-on-X

Same-day teeth with dental implants offer a way for patients with missing teeth to leave the office with a full set of teeth in just one day. This approach uses advanced implant dentistry techniques to provide immediate support for replacement teeth following implant placement.

With All-on-4 or All-on-X full arch dental implant treatment, a small number of strategically placed implants support a complete set of teeth. This reduces the need for individual implants while still providing strong, stable results.

In many cases, immediate implant placement allows a temporary restoration to be attached the same day. Patients can leave with functional teeth while the healing process continues beneath the gums. Over time, the implants integrate with the jawbone, and a final restoration is placed.

Same-day teeth, also called “teeth in a day,” can improve comfort, restore chewing ability, and enhance appearance without waiting months between procedures. While healing still occurs, this approach reduces time without teeth and supports a smoother transition from missing teeth or a removable denture.

Dr. Patel will evaluate your oral health, bone structure, and overall health to determine if same-day teeth with dental implants is the right choice for your needs.

Bone Grafting and Jawbone Support

Bone grafting plays an important role when bone density is insufficient to support implant placement. Bone loss can result from periodontal disease, long-term tooth loss, or previous tooth extraction without timely replacement.

A bone graft adds volume and strength to the jaw, creating a stable foundation for dental implant placement. The graft material integrates with existing bone during the healing process, supporting long-term implant success.

Bone grafting may be completed before implant surgery or at the same time, depending on the treatment plan. Healing time varies, and your oral surgeon will provide guidance on proper care, soft foods, and pain relievers to support recovery. Bone grafting helps ensure implant placement is both safe and durable.

How Do Dental Implants Work?

Dental implants work by replacing the artificial tooth root and anchoring securely within the jawbone. Over time, the implant bonds with bone, creating a stable base for an artificial tooth or dental prosthetic. This structure supports chewing forces and helps protect the surrounding gum tissue and bone.

This process supports long-term oral health and allows replacement teeth to function in a way that closely mimics natural teeth.

Supporting Long-Term Success

Successful implant treatment depends on careful planning, precise surgical technique, and proper care after surgery. Maintaining good oral hygiene, attending regular dental visits, and following post-operative instructions help protect the implant placement and the surrounding gum tissue.

Dental Implant FAQs

Am I a candidate for dental implants?

Many adults with missing teeth are candidates for dental implants if they have healthy gum tissue and sufficient jawbone to support the implant. During your consultation, Dr. Patel evaluates bone structure, medical history, and overall oral health to determine whether implant placement is appropriate. In some cases, procedures such as bone grafting may help prepare the area for successful treatment.

What happens if a missing tooth is not replaced with a dental implant?

When a missing tooth is not replaced, the surrounding bone can gradually shrink due to a lack of stimulation. Over time, this bone loss may affect nearby teeth, facial structure, and overall oral function. Dental implants help preserve the jawbone and maintain stability within the mouth.

Are dental implants safe?

Dental implants have been used for decades and are considered a reliable tooth replacement option when planned and placed carefully. Modern imaging, surgical planning, and specialized training help oral surgeons perform implant procedures with a strong focus on safety and long-term success.

How do I care for dental implants after treatment?

Dental implants are cared for much like natural teeth. Brushing, flossing, and maintaining regular dental visits help protect the surrounding gum tissue and support long-term implant stability. Your dental team will also provide specific instructions following implant placement to support proper healing.

At what age can someone receive dental implants?

Dental implants are typically recommended once jaw growth is complete. This is usually in the late teenage years or adulthood. For younger patients with missing teeth, other temporary tooth replacement options may be considered until implant placement becomes appropriate
