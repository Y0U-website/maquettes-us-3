Your child’s first visit

First visits are about comfort and trust — for your child and for you. Here’s what to expect and how to prepare.

When to come

The American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry recommends that a child see a dentist when the first tooth appears, and no later than their first birthday. Early visits let us catch small issues before they grow, and — just as important — they establish a dental home : a familiar place your child returns to as they grow, where their full dental history lives in one spot.

What happens at the first visit

We get to know you

We review your child’s medical history and talk through any questions or concerns you have — teething, thumb sucking, bottles, brushing battles, all of it.

A gentle exam

A careful look at your child’s teeth, gums, and jaw, paced to your child’s comfort. For toddlers this is often done knee-to-knee with a parent.

A plan you understand

We explain what we see, what’s normal for your child’s stage, and what to do at home — no jargon, no pressure.

Tips for parents

Keep it light. Treat the visit like any other fun outing — avoid words like "hurt," "shot," or "drill," even reassuringly ("it won’t hurt!" plants the idea that it might). If your child asks what will happen, "the dentist is going to count your teeth" is honest and works well. Morning appointments, when little ones are rested and fed, tend to go smoothest.
