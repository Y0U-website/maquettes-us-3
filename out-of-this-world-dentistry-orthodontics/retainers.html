The Retainer Process

  • Evaluation: A brief exam confirms stability and identifies the best retainer type.
  • Digital scan or impressions: Precise models guide accurate retainer fabrication.
  • Custom fabrication: Your retainer is made to fit snugly and comfortably.
  • Fitting and instructions: You receive guidance on wear time, cleaning, and storage.
  • Wear schedule: Many patients start with full-time wear, then transition to nights.
  • Follow-ups: Periodic checks confirm fit, make adjustments, or plan replacement.

What To Expect And How To Care For Your Retainer

It is normal to notice extra saliva and mild pressure during the first few days of wear. Speech may sound different at first but typically returns to normal quickly. Consistent wear is essential because even small gaps in use can allow teeth to drift.

  • Clean Daily: Use cool water and a soft brush. For removable retainers, non-abrasive soap works well. Avoid hot water, which can warp plastic.
  • Soak Periodically: Use a retainer-safe cleanser as directed to help reduce plaque and odor.
  • Handle Carefully: Always store your retainer in a ventilated case when not in use.
  • Protect Fixed Retainers: Floss under the wire with a threader or interdental aids to keep the area clean.
  • Avoid Heat And Chemicals: Keep retainers away from hot cars, boiling water, and harsh cleaners.
  • Act Quickly If Lost Or Damaged: Call promptly for replacement to prevent unwanted tooth movement.

Fixed Vs. Removable: Choosing An Option

Removable retainers make hygiene straightforward and are easy to replace, while fixed retainers provide round-the-clock stability without relying on daily memory. Many patients benefit from a combination, such as a fixed lower retainer with a removable upper retainer at night. The choice depends on how your teeth moved, your risk of relapse, and your preferences for cleaning and maintenance.
