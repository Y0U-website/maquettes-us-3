Preventive Care for Healthy Family Smiles

Preventive care forms the foundation of family dental care in Missoula, MT. Routine checkups allow us to examine the teeth, gums, bite, and existing restorations. These visits may reveal early changes that are not yet causing pain, making it possible to discuss appropriate monitoring or care before the problem becomes more complex.

Professional cleanings clear plaque and hardened tartar from areas that brushing and flossing may miss. Our hygiene team can also identify areas that need more attention and offer practical recommendations for home care. Advice may include brushing technique, interdental cleaning, fluoride use, nutrition, or products suited to the patient’s needs.

Appointment schedules are not identical for every patient. Age, gum health, cavity history, medical conditions, oral hygiene, and previous dental work can all influence how often visits are recommended. Our team develops a recall schedule based on examination findings rather than applying the same interval to everyone.

Advanced Technology at Our Missoula Dental Office

Dental technology can provide detailed information about a patient’s teeth, gums, bone, and surrounding oral structures. Missoula Dental uses digital diagnostic and clinical tools when they can contribute to diagnosis, planning, comfort, or patient communication.

Intraoral cameras capture close-up images that allow patients to view areas being discussed during an examination. Digital scanners record detailed information about the teeth and oral structures without relying solely on conventional impression materials. When three-dimensional imaging is clinically appropriate, cone-beam computed tomography, or CBCT, can support the planning of dental implants and other advanced procedures.

Missoula Dental also uses 3D printing and dental lasers for selected applications. These technologies may support customized dental work, detailed planning, and less invasive approaches in certain situations. Our advanced dental technology complements Dr. Bond’s clinical judgment and helps our team explain examination findings and proposed care more clearly.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • At what age should my child first visit a dentist? The American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry recommends establishing a dental home by a child’s first birthday or within six months after the first tooth appears. An early visit allows the dentist to examine developing teeth, discuss home care, and answer parents’ questions. Missoula Dental provides general family dental care for children but is not a pediatric dental practice.
  • How often should my family have dental checkups? The appropriate schedule depends on each family member’s oral health, cavity history, gum condition, age, medical history, and dental work. Although six-month visits are common, some patients may benefit from a different interval. Dr. Bond and the hygiene team will recommend a schedule after evaluating each patient.
  • Can parents and children schedule appointments at the same dental office? Yes. Missoula Dental cares for multiple generations, so parents and children can receive care through the same practice. Appointment availability and the services needed will determine whether visits can be arranged on the same day. Contact our team to discuss scheduling options.
  • What should we bring to a first family dental appointment? Bring photo identification, current health and medication information, and any available dental records or recent X-rays. Parents or legal guardians should accompany children and be prepared to discuss their medical and dental history. You may also bring a list of questions or concerns you would like the dental team to address.
  • Can Missoula Dental help if a family member feels anxious about dental visits? Yes. Let our team know about dental anxiety when scheduling the appointment. We can explain what will happen, allow time for questions, and discuss comfort options based on the planned procedure and the patient’s health. Missoula Dental also offers sedation dentistry for eligible patients and procedures.
  • What should I do if someone in my family has a dental emergency? Call Missoula Dental promptly if a family member experiences severe tooth pain, facial swelling, a knocked-out tooth, uncontrolled bleeding, or substantial dental damage. The team can provide instructions based on the situation and appointment availability. Call 911 or seek hospital emergency care for difficulty breathing, difficulty swallowing, major facial trauma, or another potentially life-threatening condition.
