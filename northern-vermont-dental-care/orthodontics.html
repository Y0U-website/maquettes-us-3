Orthodontics Services Explained

Orthodontics is the area of dentistry focused on guiding tooth movement and jaw development. Treatment can help with crowding, gaps, overbite, underbite, crossbite, and open bite. In addition to a straighter smile, orthodontics can make daily cleaning easier, support balanced chewing forces, and protect teeth from uneven wear.

  • Metal braces that use small brackets and a wire to apply gentle, steady forces.
  • Ceramic braces that blend with tooth color for a lower-profile look.
  • Clear aligners that are removable trays used in sequence to move teeth.
  • Lingual braces that attach to the tongue side of teeth for hidden treatment.
  • Palatal expanders and other growth-guiding devices used in select cases.
  • Retainers used after active care to help hold your results.

Benefits of Orthodontic Treatment

  • Improved bite function that supports comfortable chewing and speech.
  • Better access for brushing and flossing, which may lower cavity and gum risks.
  • Balanced tooth wear that helps protect enamel over time.
  • Reduced risk of chipping or trauma for protruding front teeth.
  • Enhanced smile appearance that can boost confidence in daily life.
  • Long-term oral health benefits when paired with good home care.

How Orthodontics Services Work

Orthodontic care uses light, continuous forces to remodel the bone around teeth so they can move into healthier positions. If you have wondered “how do braces work” or how clear aligners guide teeth, both rely on planned, incremental changes that occur over weeks and months.

  • Step 1: Consultation and records. A thorough orthodontic consultation includes an exam, photos, digital scans or impressions, and X-rays.
  • Step 2: Personalized plan. Your plan outlines goals, appliance choices, estimated orthodontic treatment timeline, and expected retention needs.
  • Step 3: Getting started. Brackets are placed or your first set of aligners is provided, with guidance on care and wear time.
  • Step 4: Active treatment. Periodic visits check progress and make adjustments or provide the next aligner set.
  • Step 5: Retention. After active movement, retainers help maintain alignment and protect your new smile.

What to Expect During Treatment

Most patients feel mild pressure or tenderness after adjustments or when switching to a new aligner. This usually eases within a few days and can be managed with over-the-counter relief as advised. Good brushing and flossing remain essential; tools such as floss threaders, interdental brushes, or water flossers can help you clean around appliances.

Diet changes may be recommended with braces to avoid hard or sticky foods that can loosen brackets. Clear aligner wear typically requires 20 to 22 hours per day, removing trays only for meals and brushing. In general, treatment can range from several months to two years or more, depending on your case and how consistently you follow instructions. Retainers after braces or aligners are part of every plan to keep teeth stable.

Patients often ask about clear aligners vs braces. Many mild to moderate concerns respond well to aligners, while complex movements may be better suited to braces or a hybrid plan. An in-person evaluation is the best way to determine what is right for you.
