Who Is a Good Candidate for Orthodontic Braces?

Orthodontic braces are often a strong fit when teeth need more comprehensive correction, not just cosmetic straightening. Braces commonly address crowding, spacing, rotations, protrusion, midline shifts, and bite problems such as overbite, underbite, crossbite, and open bite.

For many patients, braces are recommended when predictable tooth control and reliable bite improvement matter most, especially when consistency is key. How Orthodontic Braces Work

Orthodontic braces use brackets and an archwire to apply steady, controlled force that guides teeth into healthier alignment over time. As teeth move, the surrounding bone gradually remodels, which is why progress is measured in small, planned steps.

Adjustments help keep movement on track and refine both alignment and bite as the smile develops. Most patients notice brief pressure or tightness after changes, which signals the system is actively working toward the next stage of improvement.

Self-Ligating Braces

Self-ligating braces use a built-in mechanism to hold the wire in place instead of elastic ties, which many patients like for a more streamlined feel, fewer ties to manage, and an easier cleaning experience in day-to-day routines when appropriate.

Clear Braces

Clear braces use tooth-colored or clear brackets that are designed to be less noticeable, making them a common choice for patients who want a more discreet look while still using braces, with the added benefit of keeping a little extra awareness around staining and everyday care.

LightForce Custom Braces

LightForce is a 3D printed braces system, designed with custom brackets made to fit a patient’s tooth shapes more precisely. Braces for Kids

For children, braces planning focuses on guiding development, including space management, monitoring how teeth are erupting, and supporting healthy bite growth as the smile takes shape. The goal is to keep things on track early when timing can make future correction simpler and more stable.

Braces for Teens

Teen years are often an efficient time for braces because most permanent teeth are in and treatment can be planned around school routines and activities. Braces for Adults

Adults often choose braces to improve long-term function and bite health, address shifting that happened over time, or correct relapse after past orthodontic treatment. Braces remain a common, effective option for adults, and planning can be tailored to fit work, family life, and esthetic preferences without making treatment feel like a disruption.
