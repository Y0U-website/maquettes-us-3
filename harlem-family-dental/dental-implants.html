Who Is a Candidate for Dental Implants?

You may be a candidate for dental implants if you:

We conduct a thorough exam, imaging, and consultation to determine the best path forward. If bone loss is present, we may recommend preparatory treatment before implant placement.

  • Have one or more missing teeth
  • Are in good overall health and have healthy gums
  • Have adequate bone structure, or are a candidate for bone grafting
  • Are seeking a stable, fixed alternative to dentures or bridges

Benefits of Dental Implants

Dental implants offer life-changing benefits that go beyond appearance:

Because they fuse directly with the jaw, implants become a permanent part of your mouth, allowing you to eat, smile, and speak with confidence every day.

  • Prevent jawbone loss and preserve facial structure
  • Improve chewing and speech function
  • Eliminate the need for adhesives or removable appliances
  • Offer a high success rate and long-term reliability
  • Support restorative and cosmetic goals

Caring for Your Dental Implants

Maintaining your dental implants is simple and similar to caring for natural teeth:

With proper care, your implants can last decades—or even a lifetime.

  • Brush and floss daily
  • Visit us regularly for professional cleanings
  • Avoid using teeth as tools or biting hard objects
  • Report any changes or discomfort promptly

Frequently Asked Questions

Are dental implants painful? Most patients report that implant placement is more comfortable than expected. Local anesthesia and gentle techniques make the procedure smooth. Post-op soreness is manageable and typically resolves within a few days.

How long do dental implants last? Dental implants are designed to last 20+ years with good oral hygiene and routine dental visits. They are one of the most durable and predictable treatments in modern dentistry.

Is the implant procedure safe? Yes. Dental implants are widely considered safe and effective. Our team conducts detailed evaluations and uses advanced planning technology to reduce risks and ensure success.

Can I get an implant if I’ve been missing a tooth for years? Yes, but bone loss may have occurred. In these cases, a bone graft may be necessary to prepare the site for successful placement.

What’s the difference between implants and dentures? Implants are fixed and act like natural teeth, while dentures are removable and rest on the gums. Implants offer more stability, preserve bone, and feel more like your own teeth.
