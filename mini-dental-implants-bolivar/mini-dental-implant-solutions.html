The functionality of mini dental implants can benefit people of all ages and are particularly beneficial for those with less available bone within their jaw, as the small diameter of mini implants do not require as much.

  • Stabilizing a loose fitting denture or to secure a bridge.
  • Filling a narrow gap left by a missing tooth.
  • Securing a smaller replacement crown for an incisor or premolar.
  • Providing an anchor for patients with narrow jaws or missing bone or tissue.

Benefits of Mini Dental Implants

Compared to conventional crowns or dentures, mini dental implants offer a great user-friendly alternative, ultimately providing a better quality of life for patients.

  • Conventional crowns require enough tooth to create a secure bond. The tooth must also be strong enough to hold the crown without cracking and the root must be solid enough to support the crown and remaining tooth. A mini implant replaces the root and remaining tooth with a strong metal such as titanium. This titanium implant is much stronger than a tooth and holds the crown securely in the jaw.
  • Dentures that are not supported by implants can be too loose or fit poorly. Ill-fitted dentures can cause various problems like speech impediments, mouth sores, and inflamed gums. Loose dentures can even lead to gum infections. Anchoring dentures with mini dental implants secure these dentures into place, greatly minimizing these issues.
  • Patients with dental appliances often lack confidence and chew cautiously to prevent the bridge or denture from slipping, even if their appliances fit well. In addition to this, certain foods that may be hard, sticky, or fibrous may be avoided by some to avoid destabilizing their dentures. These concerns are all but eliminated when dentures are secured with implants, as they function like natural teeth allowing patients to chew like normal.
  • Similarly, crowns can become loose over time as well. Not only will this lead to continued chewing difficulties, but may allow bacteria to decay the tooth holding the crown. An implant solves this problem, because when an underlying tooth is removed and a crown is secured to it, the patient does not need to worry about the implant decaying.
  • Denture wearers often are subject to tissue and bone loss from lack of stimulation; developing facial collapse where bone and tissue loss can lead to sunken features. Implants, however, provide pressure on the jaw bone that prevents bone and tissue loss.
  • If a crown breaks, replacing it over a tooth root requires cleaning the tooth and fitting the crown to it. But with implants, it’s much simpler since the crown is usually cemented or screwed to the post.
