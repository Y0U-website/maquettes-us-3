Types of Dental Implants

We place different types of dental implants to meet the varying needs of our patients. The structure of each type of implant is designed to support the condition of a patient’s jaw bone and their unique placement needs. Main types of implants include:

  • Endosteal Implants: Endosteal implants are the most common type of dental implant. They’re placed directly into the jawbone during surgery. These implants look like small screws, cylinders, or blades.
  • Subperiosteal Implants: Subperiosteal implants are placed just under the gum but rest on top of the jawbone, rather than inside it. These implants are often used for individuals who lack sufficient bone in their jaw to support regular implants. They’re a good option for patients with a shallow jawbone who can’t get bone grafting.
  • Zygomatic Implants: This type is anchored in the cheekbone (zygoma) rather than the jawbone. We use zygomatic implants in complex cases of significant jawbone loss. These implants are longer and require advanced surgery.
  • Mini Implants: These implants are smaller in diameter than traditional implants. We can use mini implants in narrower areas of the jaw. Mini implants also require less invasive surgery.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you want to know more? We’re here to help. Read answers to commonly asked questions about dental implants in Wilmington, DE.

How do I know if I’m a good candidate for dental implants?

Good candidates for dental implants have sufficient jawbone density to support the implant, healthy gums, and overall good health. During your consultation at our Wilmington office, Dr. Campbell or Dr. Rafetto will conduct a thorough examination. Our dentists will use digital imaging to assess your bone structure.

Even if you’ve experienced bone loss, procedures like bone grafting may make you eligible for implants. Certain conditions, like uncontrolled diabetes or heavy smoking, may require special consideration. We’ll discuss your health concerns as we provide personalized treatment planning.

What’s the success rate of dental implants?

Dental implants have an impressive success rate of 95-98 % when placed by experienced professionals, such as our team. With proper care, including good oral hygiene and regular dental checkups, results can last a lifetime. You may need to replace your crown, bridge, or denture after 15-20 years because of normal wear, but the titanium implant will remain stable.

Proper home care and avoiding habits like teeth grinding significantly contribute to long-term success. We provide all our implant patients with detailed maintenance instructions to maximize the longevity of their results.

Is the implant procedure painful, and what’s the recovery like?

Most patients report the implant procedure involves less discomfort than expected thanks to modern anesthesia and sedation options. You may experience some swelling, bruising, or mild discomfort for a few days after surgery. Our office will prescribe over-the-counter medication to you as you heal.

The initial healing phase can take 7 to 10 days, during which we recommend soft foods and gentle cleaning. The complete osseointegration process takes 3 to 6 months, but you can resume normal activities within a day or two. We use advanced techniques to minimize discomfort at every stage.

How do dental implants compare to bridges?

While implants have a higher initial cost than bridges, they often prove more cost-effective long-term. Bridges typically last 7-15 years before needing replacement, while implants can last decades. Bridges require altering adjacent healthy teeth, but implants preserve your natural tooth structure.

Implants also prevent bone loss and don’t decay like natural teeth under bridges. We offer flexible financing options and will help you evaluate all factors to make the best choice for your oral health and budget.

Can I get implants if I’ve been missing teeth for many years?

Yes, even patients who lost teeth years ago can often receive implants. If you’ve never replaced your missing teeth and have bone loss, we can help with bone grafting or zygomatic implants. These treatments help rebuild the bone so you can get dental implants.

Our dentists will take 3D scans of your mouth to check your bone health at your visit. Then, we’ll talk with you about the best type of dental implant or any extra treatments you might need before you get your implant.

How do I care for my dental implants after the procedure?

Caring for implants is similar to caring for natural teeth, but with some special considerations. You’ll need to brush twice daily with a soft-bristled toothbrush. You should also use non-abrasive toothpaste to avoid scratching your new teeth. We may recommend using interdental brushes to clean around the implant.

We’ll also recommend that you continue to schedule regular professional cleanings every 6 months. Avoid chewing hard items, such as ice or hard candy, that could damage the crown. Your implants should last for years with the proper care.
