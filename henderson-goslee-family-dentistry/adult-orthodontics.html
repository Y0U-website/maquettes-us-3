How Adult Orthodontics Works

Consultation and Planning

The process starts with a comprehensive exam, dental imaging, and digital scans or impressions. These records map tooth positions and bite relationships. A step-by-step plan outlines recommended appliances, expected orthodontic treatment timeline, and key milestones. For some adults, periodontal evaluation is coordinated to ensure gum health before or during tooth movement.

Active Treatment

Braces use brackets and archwires to guide teeth through planned movements. Ceramic braces blend with teeth for lower visibility, while metal braces are durable and efficient. Lingual braces are placed behind teeth for hidden mechanics. Clear aligners use a series of custom trays that are changed on schedule to make small, precise adjustments. Aligners must be worn 20 to 22 hours per day to stay on track. Your choice is guided by case complexity, esthetic goals, comfort, and daily habits.

Retention

After active movement, retainers hold teeth in their new positions while bone and soft tissues adapt. Retainers after braces or aligners may be removable, fixed behind the teeth, or a combination. Long-term wear, often at night, protects your results and reduces the chance of relapse.

What to Expect

  • Initial visits include evaluation, records, and a discussion of appliance options and timelines.
  • Most adults are seen every four to eight weeks for adjustments or aligner checks.
  • Mild tenderness or pressure is common after adjustments and typically fades within days.
  • Oral hygiene is essential, including thorough brushing, interdental cleaning, and fluoride use.
  • With braces, certain foods are limited to avoid damage, while aligners are removed for meals.
  • Average treatment ranges from about six months to two years, depending on goals and complexity.
  • Consistent retainer use after treatment supports long-term stability and protects your smile.
