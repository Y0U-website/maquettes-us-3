Discover How Implants Can Restore Your Health, Confidence, and Comfort

Dental implants are crafted to blend seamlessly with your existing smile. They’re designed to mimic the shape, shade, and function of your natural teeth—so you can smile, laugh, and speak confidently.

Because implants replace tooth roots, they stimulate your jawbone and help preserve your natural bone structure. This prevents the sunken appearance often caused by bone loss after missing teeth.

Unlike removable dentures, implants stay firmly in place—letting you bite and chew your favorite foods without worry. Enjoy steak, crunchy veggies, and chewy bread without discomfort.

With proper care, implants can last decades or even a lifetime. That makes them a more cost-effective option compared to bridges or dentures that may need replacing every few years.

Understanding Dental Implants

Understanding the foundation of a lasting smile.

Dental implants consist of three components working together to replace missing teeth . A titanium post surgically placed into your jawbone serves as an artificial tooth root, providing the stable foundation that other tooth replacement options lack. An abutment connects to the implant post, projecting slightly above the gum line. Finally, a custom dental crown attaches to the abutment, completing the restoration with a tooth that looks, feels, and functions like your natural teeth.

The titanium post integrates with your jawbone through a process called osseointegration, where bone cells grow around and bond to the implant surface.

This fusion creates exceptional stability that allows implants to withstand normal chewing forces without shifting or slipping. According to the American Academy of Implant Dentistry, dental implants have a success rate exceeding 95 percent, making them the most predictable and durable tooth replacement option available.

Advantages Over Other Replacement Options

Dental implants offer significant advantages compared to traditional bridges or dentures. Unlike bridges that require grinding down healthy adjacent teeth to serve as anchors, implants replace missing teeth without compromising surrounding natural teeth. This preservation of tooth structure benefits your long-term oral health by maintaining the integrity of neighboring teeth.

Implants also prevent the bone loss that occurs after tooth loss. When teeth are missing, the jawbone in that area no longer receives stimulation from chewing forces, causing it to gradually shrink and weaken. Implants transmit these forces into the bone just like natural tooth roots, maintaining bone volume and facial structure. Dentures and bridges rest on top of gums without providing this crucial stimulation, allowing progressive bone deterioration that can eventually affect facial appearance and the fit of removable appliances.

The Implant Placement Process

Your implant journey begins with a comprehensive evaluation where we assess your oral health, review your medical history, and determine if you’re a good candidate for implant treatment. We’ll take digital X-rays and may use our iTero intraoral scanner to create precise models of your mouth. These diagnostic tools help us plan your treatment and coordinate with the oral surgeon who will place your implant.

During the surgical phase, the oral surgeon places the titanium implant post into your jawbone where the tooth root once existed. This procedure typically uses local anesthesia, and most patients report minimal discomfort during recovery. The implant then requires several months to integrate with your bone before we can attach the final restoration. We may provide a temporary tooth replacement during this healing period to maintain your appearance and function.

Restoring Your Implant

Once your implant has fully integrated with your jawbone, you’ll return to our office for the restorative phase of treatment. We attach an abutment to the implant post, which extends slightly above your gum line. Using our advanced digital impression technology, we capture precise measurements that allow our dental laboratory to fabricate a custom crown designed specifically for your implant.

Your final crown is carefully crafted to match the size, shape, and color of your natural teeth, creating a seamless appearance that blends perfectly with your smile. We’ll check the fit and appearance before permanently cementing the crown to the abutment. The completed restoration functions just like a natural tooth, allowing you to eat, speak, and smile with confidence.

Replacing Multiple Teeth

Dental implants offer versatile solutions for replacing multiple missing teeth. Implant-supported bridges use two or more implants as anchors, supporting several artificial teeth between them without requiring natural teeth for support. This approach is ideal for patients missing three or four consecutive teeth in the same area.

For patients missing most or all of their teeth, implant-supported dentures provide stability and function far superior to traditional removable dentures. Several implants placed strategically in the jaw support a full arch of replacement teeth that snap securely into place. These restorations eliminate the slipping, clicking, and dietary restrictions associated with conventional dentures while preventing the bone loss that makes traditional dentures increasingly loose over time.

Candidacy and Considerations

Most healthy adults with missing teeth are candidates for dental implants, though certain factors affect eligibility and success. Adequate jawbone volume and density are essential to support implants. If bone loss has occurred, bone grafting procedures can rebuild the area before implant placement. Chronic health conditions like uncontrolled diabetes or current tobacco use may compromise healing and integration, requiring management before proceeding with treatment.

During your consultation, we’ll thoroughly evaluate your situation and discuss any factors that might affect your treatment outcome. Some patients require preliminary procedures like tooth extractions, gum disease treatment, or bone grafting before implant placement. We’ll create a comprehensive treatment plan that addresses these needs in the proper sequence, ensuring optimal conditions for implant success.

Caring for Your Dental Implants

Dental implants require the same diligent home care as natural teeth. Brush twice daily with a soft-bristled toothbrush and fluoride toothpaste, paying careful attention to where the crown meets your gum line. Floss daily to remove plaque from between teeth and around implant restorations. Regular dental checkups and professional cleanings allow us to monitor your implant health and address any concerns before they become problems.

With proper care, dental implants can last for decades or even a lifetime. The crown portion may eventually require replacement due to normal wear, but the implant post typically remains functional indefinitely. Avoiding habits like chewing ice or using your teeth as tools protects both natural teeth and implant restorations from damage.
