What Are Dental Implants?

A dental implant consists of a titanium post that is surgically placed into the missing tooth’s socket. Over the course of the next several months, the gums heal, and the jawbone grows to surround the post, just as it would with a natural tooth. This process is called osseointegration. Then, a metal piece called an abutment is attached to connect the post to the dental crown, bridge, or denture that is placed on top.

Altogether, dental implants are the most efficient way to restore your smile after tooth loss. Their unique ability to recreate the full structure of lost teeth gives them unmatched strength, function, and longevity with the right care, your implants could last for 30 years or longer!

Dental Implant Surgery

Dental implant surgery is relatively easy and minimally invasive. A small incision is made in your gum tissue to expose the implant site. We prepare the bone with small titanium or ceramic burs and the implant is placed, followed by a healing cap or a temporary crown. The soft tissues are then sutured closed around the implant to heal. Two weeks after surgery, we will follow up for post operation check-up and ensure that the tissues around the dental implant are healing well.

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment

We allow a period of time for osseointegration prior to proceeding with the last phase of treatment. Osseointegration is the process where your bone cells grow and fuse to the dental implant.

Placement of Dental Implant Restorations

Once we have determined that your implant has successfully integrated, we will attach a ceramic crown to the dental implant via an abutment and screw. The end result is a vibrant, natural feeling restoration that blends in and functions well with your own teeth.

Day-to-Day Benefits

Health Benefits

  • Prevent dental drift When a tooth is lost, the teeth on either side of it will start to drift out of place. Eventually, this can make cause orthodontic problems and make it more difficult to fill the gap down the road. Dental implants work to keep your remaining teeth in their proper positions.

Daily maintenance is often as simple as brushing, flossing, and rinsing with mouthwash. Because dental implants are so easy to clean, they naturally decrease your risk for oral health problems like gum infections.

What to Do Directly After Dental Implant Surgery

In most cases, you should plan on resting at home for a few days after receiving your dental implants. During your initial recovery, your biggest concern should be to prevent a dry socket. A blood clot will form at the implant site and will need to remain in place while you’re still healing. Keep your blood clot safe by:

  • Not using straws.
  • Not spitting.
  • Not smoking.
  • Not touching the site with your fingers or tongue.

Common Side Effects When Recovering from Dental Implant Placement

When you first get dental implants, you might experience swelling and general discomfort. Also, there may be some off-and-on bleeding. These side effects are nothing to worry about; they’ll go away on their own given enough time. To make sure your post-surgical symptoms stay under control, you can:

Do your side effects seem to be getting worse? Are they lasting longer than expected? If the answer to either of these questions is “yes,” please call our office immediately.

  • Use gauze to put light pressure on the site whenever there’s bleeding.
  • Apply a cold compress to keep any swelling down.
  • Taking prescribed or over-the-counter pain medication.

Your Diet After Dental Implant Surgery

One thing you’re likely to be wondering after getting dental implant surgery is, “What will I be able to eat during my recovery?” In general, anything hard or crunchy needs to be avoided entirely at first. Your diet should only include softer options that require very little if any chewing. Mashed potatoes, pasta, and scrambled eggs are all good options to stock up on. You can also have soup as long as it isn’t overly hot.

Post-Op Health & Oral Hygiene

Preventing infection is a major concern after any type of oral surgery, so you can’t afford to neglect your oral hygiene following implant placement. Brush your teeth like you normally would while being very gentle and careful around the surgical sites. It’s also a good idea to rinse with salt water or alcohol-free prescription mouth rinse two or three times a day.
