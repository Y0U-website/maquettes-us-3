Dental Implants

  • Improves jawbone health When you lose a tooth, the bone that supports it starts to shrink, but placing a dental implant in the spot helps the jawbone to stay strong and healthy.
  • Provides superior support for replacement teeth A dental implant is the only restorative option that replaces a missing tooth from the roots up.
  • Improves chewing function Tooth loss can make it painful or difficult to chew all the foods you enjoy. Dental implants are stable and secure and allow you to go back to eating whatever you like.
  • Replaces a single tooth or multiple teeth We can replace a single missing tooth with an implant crown or multiple teeth with an implant-supported denture or dental bridge.
  • Looks like a real tooth Thanks to an experienced dentist who delivers beautiful, long-lasting tooth restorations, your new implant tooth will look, feels, and function like a real tooth.

Are You a Good Dental Implant Candidate?

Nearly anyone with missing teeth is a good implant candidate. Your gums must be healthy and free of periodontal disease, and there must be enough dense bone to support the implant; if not, bone grafting is an option that would make an implant possible for you in the future.

Being a smoker does not disqualify you from being an implant candidate, but it does increase the risk of implant failure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why are dental implants better than dentures?

Many people prefer dental implants to replace missing teeth because they offer several benefits that dentures don’t. Unlike a denture that sits on top of the gums, an implant is a titanium post placed surgically into the jawbone.

Dental Implant Benefits

After implant placement, surrounding bone and tissue grow around the implant as if it were a natural part of your body. Through a process called osseointegration , the implanted post is absorbed by the jawbone. This creates a solid base that is essentially a root replacement for a missing tooth.

The benefit is that the titanium root prevents jawbone deterioration, a common problem with tooth loss. Once the implant heals, it provides a stable base for a tooth restoration, such as a dental crown, bridge, or implant denture. The result is a fully functional replacement tooth that allows you to eat, speak, and smile confidently.

If you care for the implant properly, it can last a lifetime. This involves brushing twice a day, flossing once, and visiting our Hempstead, NY, dental office every six months for exams and teeth cleanings.
