How Invisalign Works

Invisalign does the exact same job as metal braces, but rather than using brackets and wires that are bonded to the teeth, a series of custom-made, clear plastic aligners are used instead. These are worn over the teeth, and they exert subtle pressure to encourage them to shift into healthier, straighter positions. As long as patients wear the aligners for 22 hours each day and advance to new sets when needed (usually every 2 weeks), they’ll be able to achieve their orthodontic goals, often more quickly than traditional braces.

The Benefits of Invisalign

Invisalign comes with all kinds of unique benefits that traditional braces can’t match, including:

  • The aligners are made from a special clear plastic, meaning they’re very discreet no one will be able to tell you’re undergoing orthodontic treatment!
  • Since Invisalign is fully removable, you don’t have to worry about any dietary restrictions, so long as you take out your aligners before eating.
  • While treatment times vary, Invisalign can often offer faster results compared to traditional braces in addition to requiring fewer check-in appointments.

Crowded Teeth

If there isn’t enough room in your mouth for your teeth to sit in their ideal positions, they will crowd together, negatively impacting your confidence and oral health in the process. There is good news, though: traditional braces are no longer the only solution. Patients with mild to moderate cases of overcrowding are often candidates for Invisalign, which means there won’t be any metal at all.

Gaps Between Teeth

Unfortunately, having too much space in your mouth can also increase your risk of common oral health problems, including gingivitis. If you’re struggling with noticeable spaces, you might be wondering if Invisalign can fix teeth gaps. Fortunately, the answer is yes! To find out if clear aligners are ideal for you, take the next step: schedule a consultation with our team at Pacific Oak Dental.

Overbite

While the upper teeth sit in front of the lower ones in a healthy bite, they don’t jut out too far. If that’s the case for you, then you should come to our office so we can determine if Invisalign is the solution. If that’s the case, then you won’t need to wear metal brackets and wires. Instead, we can guide your bite into proper alignment with a series of aligners that are custom-made for you.

Underbite

Even relatively mild underbites can lead to several serious problems, including trouble chewing properly, premature wear and tear on certain teeth, and chronic jaw pain. For all of these reasons, we recommend scheduling an Invisalign consultation with us. That way, we can determine if you’re a good candidate and, if you are, move your bite into proper alignment with clear, custom-made trays.

Crossbite

What if some, but not all, of your upper teeth sit in front of your lower ones? If that’s the case, you may have a crossbite, which is an orthodontic problem that can negatively impact your self-esteem and oral health. The best thing to do is to come to our office so we can complete a comprehensive oral exam. From there, we can determine if Invisalign is the solution you’ve been looking for.

Open Bite

When you bite down, is there a space between your upper and lower teeth? If so, you might have an open bite. Like the other bite problems on this list, there are several risks that come with not getting the orthodontic care you need. So, don’t take the wait and see approach. Instead, call us to schedule a consultation so we can review all of your treatment options, including Invisalign.

Invisalign vs. Mail-In Aligners: Which Costs More?

The cost of Invisalign is usually higher. There are a few reasons for this, including that mail-order alternatives usually skip the initial exam and ask patients to take their own impressions. While this does cut costs, it also leads to an increased risk of dental damage and worsened misalignment. That’s why it’s best to invest in a reputable, safe, and reliable orthodontic treatment, like Invisalign, even if it requires a larger investment.
