Prevents Further Bone and Tooth Loss

A natural tooth root stimulates the healthy regeneration of bone around it. When the root is missing, bone in the area starts to shrink, and surrounding teeth lose support. As the bone continues to deteriorate, your risk for tooth loss increases.

Because a dental implant replaces the tooth root, bone remains strong and healthy.

Preserves a Fuller Facial Appearance

With tooth loss and bone loss, your facial contours change, and your cheeks and lips sag, making you appear older than you are. Placing dental implant teeth fills out these facial features to maintain a fuller, more youthful appearance.

How Do Dental Implants Work?

Unlike bridges and dentures that sit on top of the gums, a dental implant is a tiny titanium post placed surgically in the jawbone. After the implant has time to heal and integrate with the bone and tissue around it, it becomes part of your dental anatomy, just like the natural tooth was.

When our implant dentist, Dr. Mehul Shah, attaches a crown, bridge, or denture to the implant, the result is a stable, secure prosthetic tooth. If you need full tooth replacement, several implants can support a denture that stays securely in place and allows you to return to eating your favorite foods.

The key to maintaining a dental implant is to protect the jawbone that supports it. Untreated gum disease can lead to bone loss, putting your implant at significant risk for failure.

Who is a suitable candidate for dental implant surgery?

There’s no explicit age restriction on candidacy for dental implants; there are specific criteria that one must meet to be deemed a suitable candidate. To qualify as an appropriate candidate for implants, an individual must:

  • Have a fully-formed jawbone. Again, there’s no specific age at which this happens, though in most cases, it’s between 18 and 24.
  • Not suffer from gum disease and otherwise have healthy and strong gums to support a dental implant.
  • Have enough density in their jawbone to support a dental implant. (This, in some cases, can be rectified through a bone graft).

What Exactly Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are small titanium pegs placed into the jawbone s empty socket after tooth loss. Once in place, over a few weeks, in a process called "osseointegration," these pegs fuse permanently with the jawbone, creating both a completely natural-feeling tooth root and a solid point upon which to affix a tooth replacement (like a dental crown). Implants stand out as long-term tooth replacement options because they tackle tooth loss above the gumline and at the root.
