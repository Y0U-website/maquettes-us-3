What to Expect During Early Intervention

Your child’s orthodontist will perform a thorough evaluation, including dental X-rays and impressions if needed, to assess tooth position and jaw development. Depending on the findings, a customized treatment plan may include appliances such as expanders, partial braces, or space maintainers designed to guide teeth and jaws into proper alignment.

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends that children have their first orthodontic evaluation by age 7. While not every child will require early treatment, scheduling a consultation can provide valuable insight into their dental development and help determine the best timing for any necessary care.

Early intervention orthodontic care is a smart investment in your child’s oral health and overall well-being. Contact us to schedule an evaluation and take the first step toward ensuring a lifetime of healthy, confident smiles.

  • Simplify Future Treatment: Addressing problems early can reduce the need for more invasive treatments like tooth extractions or jaw surgery later on.
  • Promote Proper Facial Development: Guiding jaw growth can improve facial symmetry and overall oral function.
  • Improve Oral Habits: Early care can help correct habits like thumb sucking or tongue thrusting that may affect teeth alignment.
  • Boost Confidence: Correcting orthodontic issues early supports a beautiful smile and healthy self-esteem from a young age.

Fillings

Fillings are used to repair minor to moderate tooth decay or damage. When a cavity or small crack is present, the decayed material is removed, and the empty space is filled with a material such as composite resin, amalgam, gold, or porcelain. Fillings restore the tooth’s shape, strength, and function while preventing further decay.

Crowns

A crown, or cap, is a custom-made covering that fits over a damaged or weakened tooth. Crowns are typically used when a tooth has extensive decay, a large filling, or has undergone root canal treatment. They provide strength and protection to the tooth, restoring its shape, size, and appearance. Crowns can be made from porcelain, metal, or a combination of materials.

Bridges

Bridges are dental appliances used to replace one or more missing teeth by spanning the gap between the remaining natural teeth or implants. A bridge typically consists of artificial teeth (pontics) anchored to crowns placed on the adjacent teeth (abutments). Bridges restore the ability to chew and speak correctly, maintain facial shape, and prevent remaining teeth from shifting.

Dentures

Dentures are removable prosthetic devices used to replace multiple missing teeth and surrounding tissues. They can be complete (replacing all teeth in the upper or lower jaw) or partial (replacing some teeth). Dentures improve chewing ability, speech, and facial aesthetics. They require proper care and periodic adjustment to ensure comfort and fit.
