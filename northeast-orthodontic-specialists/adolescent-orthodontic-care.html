What Is the Best Age for a Teen to Start Treatment?

Most teens begin orthodontic treatment for teens between ages 11 and 15, once most permanent teeth have come in and the jaw is still developing. That window makes tooth movement more predictable and often shortens the overall process. Every teen is different, though. Waiting too long can let crowding or bite problems worsen and make care more involved down the road. Even if your teen is a little older, it is never too late to start. The important step is having an orthodontist evaluate their smile so treatment begins at the right moment for them.

How Do I Know if My Teen Needs Braces?

A few clear signs suggest your teen may benefit from braces for adolescents , and an evaluation can confirm what is going on. Watch for:

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends an orthodontic evaluation by age seven so problems are caught early, but the teen years remain the most common time for active care. If you notice any of these signs, schedule a visit so Dr. Stadiem can take a closer look.

  • Crowded, overlapping, or noticeably crooked teeth.
  • Difficulty chewing or biting normally.
  • Jaw pain, clicking, or shifting.
  • Teeth that do not meet properly when the mouth is closed.
  • Lingering habits like thumb-sucking or mouth breathing.

How Long Does Teen Orthodontic Treatment Take?

Most teens finish active treatment in 12 to 24 months, though the exact length depends on the complexity of the case. Braces typically run 12 to 24 months with regular adjustments, while clear aligners often land in the 6 to 18 month range when worn consistently. Cases involving significant bite correction may take a little longer, and simpler ones can wrap up sooner. Dr. Stadiem gives you a personalized timeline at the consultation so there are no surprises along the way.

How Often Will My Teen Need Appointments?

Plan on short visits every few weeks so we can track progress and keep treatment on schedule. For braces, that usually means an adjustment every four to six weeks. For aligners, we typically schedule progress checks every eight to ten weeks and send your teen home with several new trays to swap out on their own. These regular touchpoints let us catch small issues early and keep the smile moving in the right direction, and we make scheduling and rescheduling easy around school, sports, and family life.

How Does My Teen Keep Their Teeth Clean with Braces?

Good hygiene is the single biggest thing your teen can do to protect their smile during treatment. Brackets and wires trap food, so a solid daily routine matters:

Our team coaches every teen on these habits at their appointments, so your child knows exactly how to keep their teeth healthy while their smile straightens out.

  • Brush after every meal with fluoride toothpaste.
  • Use an orthodontic brush to clean around brackets and wires.
  • Floss daily with orthodontic flossers or a water flosser.
  • Rinse with an antibacterial mouthwash to cut down on plaque.
  • Limit sugary snacks and drinks that feed decay.

Can Teens Play Sports and Instruments with Braces?

Yes, teens can stay active in sports and music throughout treatment with a few simple precautions. For contact sports, a custom orthodontic mouthguard protects both the teeth and the braces from impact. Teens using aligners can simply remove them before a game and store them in a protective case. Musicians may need a short adjustment period when playing brass or wind instruments with braces, but most adapt quickly. Ask Dr. Stadiem about a custom mouthguard to keep your teen’s smile safe on the field.
