Single-Tooth Implant Crowns

When one tooth is missing, a dental implant topped with a custom crown can replace it without relying on neighboring teeth for support.

The implant functions as the replacement root while the crown recreates the visible portion of the tooth.

Your implant crown is carefully designed to complement the size, shape, and shade of the surrounding teeth.

  • Natural-looking appearance
  • Stable chewing function
  • No need to prepare neighboring healthy teeth
  • Support for the surrounding jawbone
  • Easy integration into your existing smile

Implant-Supported Bridges

When several neighboring teeth are missing, dental implants may be used to support a bridge.

Instead of placing an individual implant for every missing tooth, strategically positioned implants can support multiple replacement teeth.

An implant-supported bridge may provide:

We’ll evaluate the number and location of your missing teeth to determine which treatment options are appropriate.

  • Excellent stability
  • Improved chewing ability
  • Natural-looking tooth replacement
  • Reduced dependence on neighboring natural teeth
  • A fixed alternative to certain removable restorations

Implant-Supported Dentures & Full-Arch Restorations

Patients missing most or all of their teeth may benefit from using dental implants to support a larger restoration.

Implants can provide additional stability compared with traditional removable dentures and may significantly improve confidence when eating, speaking, and smiling.

Depending on your needs, implant-supported treatment may involve a removable or fixed restoration.

Because every case is different, careful evaluation and treatment planning are essential when considering full-arch implant dentistry.

How Dental Implant Restoration Works

The implant process typically involves several stages and may include coordination between Greenhill Family Dentistry and a dental specialist when appropriate.

Implant Placement

The dental implant is surgically positioned in the jawbone. The area is then given time to heal and integrate with the surrounding bone.

Healing & Evaluation

Once healing has progressed appropriately, the implant is evaluated to ensure it is ready to support the final restoration.

Designing Your Restoration

Detailed records are taken to create a crown, bridge, or other restoration customized for your mouth and bite.

Final Restoration

Your completed restoration is attached to the implant and carefully evaluated for fit, appearance, and comfortable function.

We’ll explain your individual treatment sequence before beginning care.

Implant Crown vs. Dental Bridge

For a single missing tooth, patients often compare a traditional dental bridge with a dental implant and crown.

A traditional bridge generally relies on neighboring teeth for support.

An implant crown is independently supported by the implant and doesn’t require those neighboring teeth to be prepared solely to support the replacement tooth.

Both treatments can be excellent options depending on your circumstances.

We’ll consider the health of your surrounding teeth, jawbone, gums, bite, overall health, treatment goals, and budget before making a recommendation.

Caring for Dental Implant Restorations

Dental implants cannot develop cavities, but the tissues surrounding them still require excellent care.

Healthy gums and supporting bone are critical to the long-term success of implant treatment.

If you grind or clench your teeth, a custom night guard may be recommended to help protect both natural teeth and implant restorations.

  • Brushing twice daily
  • Cleaning carefully around the implant
  • Flossing or using recommended interdental cleaners
  • Maintaining professional dental cleanings
  • Scheduling routine examinations
  • Avoiding tobacco
  • Addressing teeth grinding when necessary

Do Implant Restorations Need Maintenance?

Although dental implants and restorations are designed for durability, they should still be professionally monitored.

Regular preventive care helps us identify small concerns before they threaten the health of the implant or restoration.

  • The implant restoration
  • Your bite
  • Surrounding gum tissue
  • Supporting bone when appropriate
  • Cleanliness around the implant
  • Signs of excessive wear or stress
