Dental implants are small titanium posts surgically placed into your jawbone to serve as artificial tooth roots. Through a process called osseointegration, the implant fuses with your bone over several months, creating a stable and permanent foundation. An abutment is then attached to the post, and a custom-crafted porcelain crown is placed on top, completing the restoration.

The implant itself has three components: the titanium post that anchors into the jaw, the abutment connector, and the crown that serves as the visible tooth. Together, these three elements create a replacement that is indistinguishable from a natural tooth in both appearance and function.

One of the most clinically significant benefits of implants is their effect on bone health. According to the American Academy of Periodontology , the jaw can lose significant bone volume in the area of a missing tooth within the first year after loss.
