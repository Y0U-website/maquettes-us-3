What are the Differences between Implant and Dentures?

When it comes to replacing missing teeth, two popular options are dental implants and dentures. Both solutions offer significant benefits, but they differ in terms of their structure, functionality, durability, and cost.

definition and structure

Dental implants are artificial tooth roots made of titanium that are surgically placed into the jawbone. They serve as a foundation for supporting individual replacement teeth or bridges.

Dentures, also known as false teeth, are removable prosthetic devices that replace missing teeth and surrounding tissues. They consist of artificial teeth set in a flesh-colored acrylic base, which rests on the gums.

stability and functionality

Implants are known for their exceptional stability and functionality. They fuse with the jawbone through a process called osseointegration, providing a strong and permanent foundation for artificial teeth. Dental implants function and feel like natural teeth, allowing for comfortable eating, speaking, and smiling.

Dentures rely on suction or adhesive agents to stay in place. While they can provide functional teeth replacement, they may feel less secure compared to implants. Dentures may occasionally slip or shift during eating or speaking, requiring adjustments for proper fit and comfort.

longevity and durability

With proper care and maintenance, dental implants can last a lifetime. The artificial tooth or bridge attached to the implant may need replacement after several years, but the implant itself remains intact.

Dentures typically have a shorter lifespan compared to implants. They may require relining or replacement every 5 to 10 years to maintain functionality and comfort.

cost considerations

Dental implants are generally more expensive upfront due to the surgical procedure involved and the use of high-quality materials. However, their long-term durability and functionality often outweigh the initial investment, making them a cost-effective solution over time.

Dentures are typically more affordable initially, but they may require periodic adjustments, relining, or replacement, which can add to the long-term costs. Additionally, the adhesive gels or creams used with dentures may incur ongoing expenses.
