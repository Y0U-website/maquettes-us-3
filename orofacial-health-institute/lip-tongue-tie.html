Lip & Tongue Tie

These tethers are necessary for coordinated orofacial function to enable effective feeding, speaking, and swallowing. When a frenulum is too short, thick or inelastic, it is considered a tie because it lacks the flexibility needed to keep up with development and muscle movement. Limited motion and function can cause a cascade of other dysfunctions.

Symptoms to Watch Out for in Each Age Group

Lip and tongue ties can be diagnosed at any age, but the warning signs may be slightly different depending on the age of the patient.

Signs Your Infant May Have a Lip or Tongue Tie

Is your baby struggling to feed, or are you having a painful breastfeeding experience? In infants, a lip or tongue tie can restrict their ability to nurse and eat effectively. A tight frenulum makes feeding much harder than it should be. Symptoms to watch out for are a shallow latch, noisy gulping, and nipple pain, as well as baby slipping off the breast quickly when you try to feed them. All these signs can signal a potential problem that requires evaluation and monitoring.

Signs Your Child Might Have a Lip or Tongue Tie

Children are born with lip and tongue ties; they do not develop them after birth. These conditions are often missed in infancy or mistaken for other issues, so it’s not uncommon for older children to be diagnosed with a lip or tongue tie. A child with a restrictive tie may experience speech problems. They often experience increased cavities because it’s more difficult to clear the mouth of food debris, even with diligent brushing. Because palate development is affected, incoming teeth may also be crowded, leading to orthodontic issues that require intervention.

Mouth breathing, snoring, and restless nights can also be a sign your child has a lip or tongue tie. Behavioral issues that mimic signs of ADHD can also be a sign. This is because a chronically tired child has an impaired ability to focus, develop impulse control, and regulate themselves emotionally.

Signs a Teen or Adult May Have a Lip or Tongue Tie

Years of restricted movement can leave teens or adults with chronic congestion or sinus issues, head, neck, or shoulder tension, migraines, teeth grinding, and temporomandibular joint (TMJ) problems. Sleep disruption is common, and as a result, patients may experience daytime sleepiness and difficulty regulating emotions and staying focused. The body’s natural ability to heal itself and recover from the cellular stress of daily living is impaired. Sleep-disordered breathing can also increase the risk of heart disease and other systemic health issues.

Results Timeline

When it comes to infants, expect a decrease in function for the first week following surgery. As healing progresses, parents will see improved feeding and longer stretches of sleep within several weeks.

School-aged children usually feel tongue freedom right away, but additional therapies may be necessary to retrain the orofacial muscles and their muscle memory.

For teens and adults, changes are more gradual and often depend on how disciplined the patient is in sticking to their treatment plan.

Could the frenulum tighten again after treatment?
