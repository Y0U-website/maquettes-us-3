How dental implants work

Dental implants are made from titanium, a strong, biocompatible material that’s gentle on bone and soft tissue. After the implant is placed, your jawbone naturally grows around it and fuses to it, locking it firmly in place. The titanium implant becomes the “root” of your missing tooth.

Once your bone has healed completely around the implant, Dr. Chaudhry caps it with a lifelike porcelain crown. Your dental implant looks and feels completely natural, you brush and floss it the same way you do your other teeth.

Why patients choose implants

Removable appliances, by contrast, can press against your jaw and slowly cause bone to diminish over the years. With proper care, dental implants can last a lifetime.

  • They look just like your natural teeth.
  • You clean them like natural teeth and never have to take them out.
  • They’re secure and stable, embedded in bone, a permanent part of your smile.
  • Treatment is conservative, the adjacent healthy teeth aren’t touched, the way they would be for a bridge.
  • They help retain bone in your jaw, supporting a firm jawline and youthful facial contours.
  • They’re stain-resistant and don’t decay.

The implant post itself is designed to stay in your jaw for decades, often for the rest of your life, as long as you brush, floss, and keep up with checkups. The porcelain crown on top sees daily chewing wear and sometimes needs replacement after 10 to 15 years.

Placement is done under local anesthetic, so you should not feel pain during the procedure. Most patients tell us the recovery was easier than they expected, with soreness managed by over-the-counter medication for a few days. Dr. Chaudhry walks you through exactly what to expect before anything is scheduled.

Most healthy adults with a missing tooth are candidates. The main requirements are healthy gums and enough jawbone to hold the implant, which is exactly what the 3D CBCT scan at our Orange Ave NE office shows us. Even if you have had bone loss, grafting can often rebuild enough support.

The timeline is driven by healing, because your jawbone needs time to fuse to the implant before the final crown goes on. Straightforward single-tooth cases sit at the shorter end, while cases involving grafting or several implants take longer. You get a realistic timeline for your own case at your consultation.
