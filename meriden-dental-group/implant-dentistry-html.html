The 4 Step Dental Implant Process

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

To begin your journey, you will attend a consultation with your implant dentist in Meriden. They will get to know you as a person and thoroughly examine your mouth. Then, they will be able to determine whether you are currently a candidate for dental implants or if you need some preliminary treatments before you qualify. Feel free to ask any questions you may have during your consultation we hope that all of our patients are well-informed!

After your mouth is healthy enough to support dental implants, we will begin to plan your implant placement surgery.

Dental Implant Surgery

While many dentists must send their patients to outside specialists for the implant placement surgery, our team has the training and experience necessary to conveniently handle every step of the implant process at our office, saving you valuable time and making your treatment as convenient as possible.

The surgery itself is fairly simple. First, we will make sure you are as comfortable as possible, after which we will make incisions in your gums. After the implants are inserted at precise locations in your jawbone, the incisions will be closed, and you can go home to begin recovery. Most patients are feeling well enough to resume a fairly normal routine within a few days of their surgery.

Dental Implant Osseointegration & Abutment Placement

After your surgery, osseointegration will begin. Osseointegration is the biological process through which your bone will fuse to your dental implants in Meriden. It is what makes it possible for the implants to serve as sturdy prosthetic tooth roots.

Following osseointegration, which usually takes 3 6 months or so, you might have to undergo a second minor surgery. The tops of your implants will be exposed, and healing caps will be placed on them. Later, after your gums have healed, abutments will be attached to your implants. Abutments are small attachments that will serve to keep your new prosthetic teeth in place.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restorations

The last step on your dental implant journey is the restoration phase. Our team will take detailed images and impressions of your mouth, which your dentist will use as the basis for designing the crown, bridge, or denture that will go on top of your dental implants. After a laboratory fabricates the restoration, we will verify its fit before we attach it to your implants. Then, you can go off and begin to enjoy the benefits of your renewed smile!

Benefits of Dental Implants

Every tooth replacement option has its own advantages and disadvantages. However, we you compare the pros of cons of dental implants to that of dentures and dental bridges, you will quickly see why thousands of patients choose to replace their missing teeth with dental implants every year. Dental implants have the potential to improve your confidence, health, and quality of life in numerous ways that other options simply cannot.

Natural Form & Function

Dental implants replace both the roots and crown of missing teeth, whereas other tooth replacement treatments only replace the visible portion of lost teeth. Dental implants’ unique structure enables them to provide extremely sturdy function that feels natural. You will be able to eat practically anything without worrying that you will destabilize your implants. Many patients find that their dental implants are so comfortable and natural-feeling that it is easy to forget that the implants are even there.

Remarkable Aesthetics

We custom design dental implant restorations with the goal of creating artificial teeth that complement your natural orofacial structures. The materials we use closely resemble real tooth enamel. When you smile, onlookers will likely have no idea that your teeth are not the same ones nature bestowed on you.

Maintain Your Youthful Appearance

When you lose teeth, the bone that once supported them begins to be resorbed; it becomes smaller and weaker, which can greatly affect facial aesthetics. Dental implants help to preserve your face’s natural shape. They fortify the bone structure of the jaw, helping to keep it strong and whole. You will therefore be able to avoid the sunken appearance that often accompanies tooth loss.

Easy Care

You can care for implants in much the same way as you would care for your natural teeth. Thorough oral hygiene practices, along with regular dental visits, will help your implants and your restorations stay functional, clean, and problem-free.

Longevity

Dental implants form a strong bond with the tissue around them. In ideal conditions, that bond can last for several decades perhaps for the rest of your life. You can also expect the restorations on top of your implants to endure for many years.

Protection for Your Remaining Teeth

When you lose a tooth, the ones around it may begin to drift out of place. Eventually, they might fall out altogether. Dental implants serve as placeholders, helping your remaining natural teeth to stay where they belong. Also, placing dental implants does not typically require that any of your natural teeth be modified or extracted.

Who Dental Implants Can Help

No matter how many teeth you are missing, you can get a new smile with dental implants. This versatile treatment can be used to address all levels of tooth loss, whether you are missing just one tooth or an entire arch. During your free initial consultation, we will discuss your goals for your smile, inspect your oral health, and review your medical history to make sure dental implants are right for you.

The ideal candidate for dental implants in Meriden is in fair oral and overall health, has good bone density and bone height in their jaw, and does not smoke. However, even if you do smoke or have a weakened jaw, it may still be possible for you to receive implants. The only way to find out for sure if the procedure is right for you is to consult with an implant expert. Missing 1 Tooth

Replacing a single tooth is a fairly straightforward process. We will simply place one implant in your jaw, and, after your bone has had a chance to heal, place a custom crown on top of it.

Missing Multiple Teeth

If you are missing several teeth in a row, an implant-supported bridge may be the ideal solution for you. This type of restoration is supported by two implants and their accompanying crowns. Pontics (artificial teeth) are suspended between the two crowns.

Missing All Teeth

Patients who are missing all or most of their teeth are often candidates for implant dentures, which can replace an entire arch of pearly whites. Your dentist will strategically plan your procedure in order to place as few implants as possible while still providing your new smile with the strong support it needs. An implant denture may be either a fixed or removable prosthesis.

What to Do After Dental Implant Surgery

Blood clots will form at your incision sites soon after your surgery. It is important that you do not disturb them. Follow these guidelines:

  • Swallow saliva or use tissues to absorb it; do not spit it out.
  • Avoid drinking straws because the suction can dislodge blood clots.
  • Do not smoke smoking can interfere with healing and increase the risk of complications.
  • Keep your tongue and fingers away from the surgical sites to prevent infection and irritation.

Common Side Effects When Recovering from Dental Implant Placement

In the days following your surgery, you are likely to experience:

If you notice any severe or unusual side effects, please contact our team immediately for assistance.

  • Intermittent bleeding: This may persist for several days. Apply clean gauze and light pressure to control it.
  • Swelling: This can continue for the first 72 hours and may last a week or longer. Cold compresses may keep it under control.
  • General discomfort: Use pain medication and arrange your schedule so you can get plenty of rest. Discomfort should start to wear off within a few days.

Your Diet After Dental Implant Surgery

During the initial phase of recovery, you should adhere to a no-chew diet, which may include options such as applesauce, meal replacement shakes, pudding, ice cream, and yogurt. Later, you can progress to fork-tender foods like scrambled eggs, meatloaf, soft breads, and pasta. Once the soft tissue at the surgical sites has healed, you may choose to resume normal eating habits, though it is advisable to avoid extremely hard, chewy, or crunchy foods.

Post-Op Health & Oral Hygiene

You may brush your teeth the day after surgery, but be sure to exercise caution around the surgical sites. If your dentist prescribes a special mouthwash for you, use it as directed. When using over-the-counter mouthwash, avoid those with high alcohol content, like traditional Listerine and Scope. You may also find that it is beneficial to periodically rinse your mouth with warm salt water; it is helpful for fighting bacteria and calming inflammation.

What to Do After Your New Teeth Are Attached

Once your beautiful new teeth are attached to your dental implants, you are essentially done with your treatment! There will be no prolonged recovery period. Although you might notice mild gum sensitivity, you can begin eating with your restored teeth immediately. If you run into any questions about how to use them or care for them, our team would be pleased to speak with you.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

Dental implants are designed to be a long-term solution for missing teeth; they have the potential to last for decades or even an entire lifetime! Still, even the strongest restorations need consistent attention. The implant posts, replacement teeth, and surrounding tissues all benefit from daily care and routine professional monitoring. As your implant dentist in Meriden, our team can provide personalized tips to keep your restored smile in excellent shape. Until then, the following guidelines can help you protect your investment and your oral health:

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

Caring for dental implants in Meriden is similar to caring for natural teeth. Brush thoroughly at least twice a day with a soft-bristled toothbrush and gentle, non-abrasive toothpaste. This helps remove plaque before it can irritate the tissues around your restorations. Clean between your teeth every day as well. Traditional floss, interdental brushes, or a water flosser may all be useful depending on your needs. An antibacterial mouthwash can also support a cleaner oral environment by reducing harmful microorganisms.

Eat a Healthy Diet

A nutritious diet can make a real difference in the health of your implants and the tissues that support them. Try to enjoy a variety of wholesome foods, including fruits, vegetables, lean proteins, dairy products, and other sources of calcium and vitamin D. These nutrients help maintain strong bones and healthy gums. It is also wise to limit sugary snacks, sticky treats, and acidic drinks, since they can contribute to plaque buildup and gum irritation.

Are Dental Implants Safe?

  • Dental implants are made of a titanium alloy that tends to work well within the human body. Adverse reactions to it are rare.
  • When the implant procedure is performed by a qualified dentist, the risk of severe complications is very low.
  • Our team takes extensive precautions to protect patients. For example, we will take a thorough medical history to find out if you have any risk factors that might the outcome of affect your implant process.

What Can Cause Dental Implants to Fail?

  • An infection develops. If harmful bacteria are allowed to proliferate in the mouth, they can invade the tissue around an implant and compromise its base of support. Poor oral hygiene, smoking, and other bad habits increase the risk of infection.
  • There is a serious injury. An accident might cause damage to both your oral tissues and your implants.
  • You have an underlying medical condition. Certain types of cancer, uncontrolled diabetes, and other medical conditions might make it difficult for your body to support dental implants.
