What We’re Actually Looking For

Early orthodontics is not just about straight teeth. It’s about how your child’s mouth, jaw, and face are developing together.

The AAPD emphasizes that timing, diagnosis, and growth patterns are critical in determining whether early treatment is beneficial.

  • Jaw growth and alignment (bite issues like overbite, underbite, crossbite)
  • Crowding and space for adult teeth
  • Tooth eruption patterns (blocked or misdirected teeth)
  • Oral habits (thumb sucking, mouth breathing)
  • Airway and breathing concerns

When Early Treatment Makes a Real Difference

Interceptive (Phase 1) orthodontics is typically considered between ages 6–10 , when children are still growing.

Clinical evidence shows that early intervention can reduce complications like impaction, severe crowding, and skeletal imbalance when timed appropriately.

  • Guide jaw development instead of correcting it later
  • Create space to avoid extractions
  • Correct crossbites before they affect facial symmetry
  • Help impacted or misdirected teeth erupt properly

Signs Your Child Should Be Evaluated

Even subtle signs can indicate underlying developmental issues .

  • Teeth look crowded or “coming in sideways”
  • Early or delayed loss of baby teeth
  • Difficulty chewing or biting
  • Mouth breathing or snoring
  • Thumb sucking beyond age 3–4
  • Jaw shifts, clicking, or asymmetry

Our Approach: Conservative, Strategic, Individualized

Not every child needs early orthodontics.

The American Academy of Pediatric Dentistry clearly states that treatment should be:

  • Based on accurate diagnosis and growth assessment
  • Timed appropriately for maximum effectiveness
  • Planned with parent involvement and clear communication
  • Monitor growth carefully before recommending treatment
  • Use diagnostic imaging and clinical evaluation to guide decisions
  • Focus on intervening only when it improves long-term outcomes
