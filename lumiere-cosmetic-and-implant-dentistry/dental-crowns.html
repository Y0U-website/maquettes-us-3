Dental Crowns

If Dr. Mersh recommends a crown during your exam, it is because one of these clinical conditions has made it the most effective and long-lasting solution for the tooth in question.

  • Cracked or fractured teeth — A crown holds the tooth together and prevents the crack from spreading, which can otherwise lead to pain, infection, or tooth loss
  • Root canal treatment — Teeth that have undergone a root canal are more brittle and vulnerable to fracture; a crown placed over the tooth restores its strength and appearance
  • Severe decay — When decay has advanced to the point where a standard restoration cannot support the remaining tooth structure, a crown rebuilds the entire visible surface
  • Dental implants — The crown is the artificial tooth attached to the implant abutment, completing the tooth replacement and giving the implant a natural look and function
  • Dental bridges — Crowns placed on the teeth adjacent to a gap support and anchor the bridge that replaces the missing tooth between them
  • Cosmetic correction — Severely discolored, misshapen, or worn teeth can be covered with a crown to restore proportion and a natural appearance

What Materials Are Used for Dental Crowns?

Crown materials have advanced considerably, and the best choice depends on the tooth’s location, the forces it will face, and your aesthetic priorities. At Lumiere, we use high-quality materials selected for each specific case.

All-Porcelain and Ceramic Crowns

Porcelain and ceramic crowns are the most natural-looking option. They are made without metal, which allows them to interact with light in a way that closely mimics natural tooth enamel. These crowns are ideal for front teeth where appearance is a priority, and modern ceramic materials are strong enough for back teeth as well. For patients with metal sensitivities, all-ceramic crowns are a comfortable and reliable alternative.

Zirconia Crowns

Zirconia is one of the strongest crown materials available today. It is particularly well-suited for molars and premolars where chewing forces are highest, and it can be made highly translucent to closely match natural tooth color. Dr. Mersh uses zirconia where durability and aesthetics need to be balanced in the same restoration.

Porcelain-Fused-to-Metal Crowns

These crowns have a metal substructure for strength with a porcelain exterior for aesthetics. They are a reliable option for back teeth, though the metal margin can occasionally show at the gumline over time. Dr. Mersh will discuss whether this material suits your specific situation during your consultation.

The Dental Crown Placement Process

Whether your crown is placed in one visit or two, the process follows a consistent sequence designed to ensure a comfortable fit and a durable result.

Preparation

The tooth is numbed with local anesthesia so you remain comfortable throughout. Dr. Mersh then shapes the tooth by removing a precise amount of outer structure to make room for the crown. The goal is to remove only what is necessary, preserving as much healthy tooth as possible. His biomimetic training informs this step: conservative preparation leads to better long-term outcomes for the remaining tooth structure.

Impression or Digital Scan

Once the tooth is prepared, we capture either a digital scan or a physical impression that records the exact shape of the prepared tooth and the surrounding bite relationship. This data drives the creation of your crown, whether it is milled in-office the same day or sent to a dental lab.

Crown Placement

When the crown is ready, Dr. Mersh checks its fit, color match, and bite alignment before cementing it permanently in place. Any minor adjustments are made chairside to ensure the crown seats correctly and does not disrupt your natural bite. Most patients leave the office with a fully restored tooth the same day.

How Long Do Dental Crowns Last?

With proper care, dental crowns typically last between 10 and 15 years, and many last significantly longer. The lifespan of a crown depends on the material used, the location of the tooth, your oral hygiene habits, and whether you grind or clench your teeth. Patients who grind at night may benefit from a custom nightguard to protect both natural teeth and crowns from excessive wear. Regular checkups allow Dr. Mersh to monitor the crown and identify any issues before they compromise the restoration.

Caring for Your Dental Crown

A crown does not require special maintenance beyond consistent oral hygiene. Brush twice daily with a soft-bristled toothbrush and floss once a day, paying attention to the gumline where the crown meets the tooth. Avoid chewing on extremely hard objects such as ice or hard candy, which can crack even a well-made crown. At your regular cleaning appointments, our hygienist will clean around the crown and check that the margins remain sealed and the surrounding gum tissue is healthy.

Frequently Asked Questions About Dental Crowns in Maitland

How long does it take to get a dental crown? +

At Lumiere in Maitland, Dr. Mersh offers same-day crown technology that allows many patients to have their crown designed, milled, and placed in a single visit. Traditional crown placement involves two appointments: one to prepare the tooth and take an impression, then a second visit two to three weeks later to cement the permanent crown. Which approach is used depends on the complexity of your case and the materials recommended for your specific tooth.

Does getting a dental crown hurt? +

Crown preparation is performed under local anesthesia, so you should not feel pain during the procedure. Some patients experience mild sensitivity or soreness in the gum tissue around the prepared tooth in the days following the appointment, which typically resolves on its own. If you experience significant or lingering discomfort after your crown is placed, we encourage you to contact our Maitland office so Dr. Mersh can evaluate whether any adjustment is needed.

How long do dental crowns last? +

Dental crowns typically last between 10 and 15 years, and many last considerably longer with proper care. The lifespan depends on the material used, the location of the tooth in your mouth, your oral hygiene habits, and whether you grind or clench your teeth. Zirconia and porcelain crowns placed by Dr. Mersh are selected based on the demands of each specific tooth, which helps maximize durability alongside aesthetics. Regular checkups allow us to monitor your crown and catch any issues early.

What is the difference between a crown and a filling? +

A filling repairs a portion of a tooth by replacing the material removed during decay treatment, leaving most of the natural tooth intact. A crown covers the entire visible surface of the tooth above the gumline, making it appropriate when so much tooth structure has been lost that a filling would not provide reliable support or protection. Crowns are also used after root canals, on cracked teeth, and as part of implant and bridge restorations. Dr. Mersh uses a conservative approach to recommend the most minimally invasive option that still provides long-term stability.

Does dental insurance cover crowns? +

Many dental insurance plans cover a portion of crown treatment when the crown is recommended for restorative reasons, such as repairing a cracked tooth, protecting a tooth after a root canal, or restoring a tooth with extensive decay. Coverage amounts and annual limits vary by plan, and pre-authorization may be required. At Lumiere, we accept PPO insurance plans and will work with you to understand your benefits and any out-of-pocket cost before treatment begins.

Can a crown fall off, and what should I do if it does? +

While uncommon, a crown can become loose or come off if the underlying tooth changes, the cement weakens over time, or if there is a strong impact. If your crown comes off, keep it safe, avoid chewing on that side of your mouth, and contact Lumiere Cosmetic and Implant Dentistry in Maitland as soon as possible. In many cases the crown can be re-cemented, especially if it is otherwise intact. Do not attempt to reattach it yourself, as improper seating can damage the prepared tooth underneath.
