How Dental Implants Work

An implant takes the place of your tooth root when Dr. Halasa places it into your jawbone. The biocompatible titanium implant fuses and integrates with your bone over time, typically taking about three to six months.

When it’s healed and fully integrated, it’s ready to support your new tooth. Dr. Halasa uses custom porcelain crowns for single-tooth replacement. Two to six dental implants can also support a dental bridge or a denture to replace multiple missing teeth.

Are implant-supported dentures really more comfortable and stable than traditional dentures?

Many patients considering dentures wonder how implant-supported dentures in Marysville compare to traditional removable dentures in terms of comfort and stability.

Implant-supported dentures are anchored to dental implants that are surgically placed in the jawbone. This provides a secure foundation for the denture, helping to prevent slipping or shifting during speaking, chewing, or other daily activities. Because the denture is supported by the implants rather than resting solely on the gums, many patients experience improved stability and feel more confident when eating or talking.

Traditional dentures , while effective at replacing missing teeth, rely on suction or adhesives to stay in place. Over time, changes in the jawbone can reduce suction, leading the denture to move or fit less comfortably.

It’s important to note that implant-supported dentures are not suitable for every patient. During a consultation at our Marysville office, your dentist will evaluate your jawbone, oral health, and overall medical history to determine whether this option is appropriate for your tooth replacement .

Does the dental implant procedure hurt?

Our patients are often pleasantly surprised by how comfortable dental implant surgery is. Because we perform placement under local anesthesia, the treatment area will be fully numb. During the procedure, you should not feel pain, only some pressure or movement at most.

During the Implant Procedure

If you experience anxiety or want a more relaxing visit, we offer nitrous oxide and oral sedation. The procedure is straightforward, and many patients compare the experience to having a filling or extraction.

Recovery After Implant Placement

Some discomfort is normal once the anesthesia wears off. This typically includes mild soreness, swelling, or tenderness around the implant site for a few days. Most patients manage this effectively with over-the-counter pain medication.

Following your aftercare instructions helps keep discomfort to a minimum. This includes eating soft foods, avoiding the implant site while it heals, and keeping the area clean as directed.

  • Mild soreness for the first two to three days
  • Swelling that gradually subsides within a week
  • A return to normal activities for most patients within a day or two

Can I get a dental implant if I’ve had a tooth missing for years?

In many cases, yes. Having a tooth missing for years does not automatically rule out dental implants . However, it does make one factor more important: the condition of the bone in the area.

How Missing Teeth Affect Your Jawbone Over Time

When you lose a tooth, the jawbone in that area is no longer stimulated by its root. The bone in the area gradually shrinks in a process called resorption. The longer a tooth has been missing, the more bone loss is likely to have occurred. Sufficient bone volume is needed to securely support a dental implant.

Can Bone Grafting Make Implants Possible After Bone Loss?

If you have experienced bone loss, you may still be a candidate for implants. A bone graft rebuilds volume in the area, creating a stable foundation for the implant. Your dental implant dentist at Marysville Dentistry uses imaging to assess your bone before recommending whether grafting is needed.
