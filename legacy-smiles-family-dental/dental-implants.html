How dental implants work

The process includes several phases to ensure precision and healing:

  • Evaluation and planning: A comprehensive exam, 3D imaging, and a review of health history determine candidacy and plan positioning. This is where “what are dental implants” and “how do dental implants work” questions are addressed in detail.
  • Preparatory care: If needed, tooth extractions, bone grafting, or a sinus lift are performed to create a stable foundation.
  • Implant placement: The implant is placed into the jawbone under local anesthesia, with sedation options available when appropriate. A small healing cap may be used.
  • Healing period: Over several months, bone fuses to the implant (osseointegration). A temporary tooth may be provided for appearance and function during this time.
  • Abutment and restoration: An abutment connects the implant to a custom crown, bridge, or implant-supported denture. The final restoration is shaped and shaded to match your smile.

Your implant experience

Before surgery, you will receive instructions about eating, medications, and hygiene. Most people report mild to moderate soreness for a few days, managed with recommended pain relief. Swelling and minor bruising can occur and typically resolve within a week.

During recovery, a soft diet, gentle brushing, and careful flossing around the area support healing. Follow-up visits check progress and confirm when you are ready for the final restoration. Once restored, ongoing care includes twice-daily brushing, daily flossing or interdental cleaning, and routine checkups.

Many patients in Meridian, Idaho are also curious about “single tooth implant vs bridge” choices. A single tooth implant avoids altering adjacent teeth and can better maintain bone volume, while a bridge may be quicker but relies on neighboring teeth for support.
