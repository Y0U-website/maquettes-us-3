It’s important to note that not everyone who snores has sleep apnea, but snoring can be a sign of an airway issue that warrants an evaluation. Snoring occurs when the flow of air through the mouth and nose is partially blocked when you sleep. While some snoring is benign, loud and frequent snoring could indicate a more serious issue, such as sleep apnea. An evaluation at the Orofacial Health Institute can help you determine whether your snoring is pointing to a larger issue and what treatment options could work for you.

Treatment Options

For Snoring Cessation and Sleep Apnea Management

Snoring cessation is possible. At the Orofacial Health Institute, we offer several options to help you stop snoring so you can get better-quality sleep and lower your risk of systemic health issues.

Thorough Personalized Evaluations

Our evaluation process is comprehensive. It all starts with a consultation with one of our orthodontists to understand your health history, sleep patterns, and any symptoms you’re experiencing. We will then perform a physical evaluation, including X-rays, and check your temporomandibular joints (TMJs) to see if there is a misalignment or other issue affecting your sleep or jaw function.

For patients who need further evaluation for sleep apnea, we offer a convenient home sleep test. This test is conducted over two nights in the comfort of your own home. After completing the test, you simply return the device, and a sleep physician will analyze the results. Dr. Dahm or Dr. Johnson will then recommend the best course of treatment. If you experience severe sleep apnea, we may refer you to a local sleep physician for a hospital sleep test.

CPAP Therapy

For those diagnosed with moderate-to-severe forms of sleep apnea, CPAP (continuous positive airway pressure) is the most common treatment. If CPAP is the best option for you, we will refer you to a sleep specialist to get fitted with the appropriate equipment.

Oral Appliance Therapy

For patients with mild-to-moderate sleep apnea who do not want or cannot tolerate a CPAP machine, oral appliance therapy is a great alternative. At the Orofacial Health Institute, we provide custom-made mandibular advancement devices that help reposition the jaw during sleep to keep the airway open.

Epigenetic Orthodontic Treatment

Our practice also offers epigenetic orthodontic treatment, which helps address improper facial growth and development that contributes to snoring and sleep apnea. This treatment encourages the orofacial structure to grow in a way that enhances both form and function. When your orofacial structure is optimized, the face and mouth can function harmoniously.

Lip- and Tongue-Tie Evaluation and Release

For some patients, restricted oral tissues, such as lip- and tongue-ties, can contribute to airway issues. We offer lip- and tongue-tie evaluations and can release these bands of restrictive tissue if necessary to improve a patient’s oral health.

Collaborative Care

We work closely with myofunctional therapists, ENTs, chiropractors, physical therapists, and allergists as needed to ensure you receive comprehensive care. This team-based approach helps us uncover the root causes of airway issues and offer options to help provide you with lasting relief.
