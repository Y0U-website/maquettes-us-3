How Adult Orthodontics Works

Treatment plans are personalized, but most adult cases follow a similar path:

  • Comprehensive Evaluation: A consultation includes photos, digital scans or impressions, and X-rays to study tooth positions, roots, and jaw relationships.
  • Customized Plan: The plan aligns with your goals and oral health. Options may include metal braces, ceramic braces, or clear aligners for adults.
  • Active Movement: Brackets and wires or a series of aligners guide teeth. Small elastics, slenderizing between teeth, or tooth-colored attachments may be used to fine-tune movement.
  • Check-Ins: Visits or virtual check-ins track progress and make adjustments. Typical intervals range from every 6 to 10 weeks.
  • Finishing And Detailing: Final adjustments refine bite contacts and tooth positions for balanced function and aesthetics.
  • Retention: Retainers after braces or aligners are essential to keep teeth from shifting. Options include clear removable retainers or a bonded retainer behind front teeth.

What To Expect During Treatment

  • Comfort: Mild tenderness is common for a few days after starting or adjusting. Over-the-counter pain relief and a soft diet help during this period.
  • Daily Wear: Clear aligners must be worn 20 to 22 hours per day and changed on schedule. Braces work around the clock without patient removal.
  • Diet And Care: With braces, avoid very hard or sticky foods to protect wires and brackets. Aligners are removed for meals and cleaned daily.
  • Oral Hygiene: Brush after meals and floss daily. Interdental brushes, floss threaders, or water flossers can make cleaning around braces easier.
  • Speech And Appearance: Ceramic braces and clear aligners blend in well. Minor speech changes with aligners usually resolve within a few days.
  • Timeline: Most adult orthodontic cases take 6 to 24 months, depending on goals, case complexity, and appliance type.

Candidacy And Planning For Adults

Healthy gums and stable supporting bone are important for predictable movement. If gum disease is present, periodontal therapy is completed before orthodontics. Teeth with crowns can usually be moved, and brackets or attachments are adapted to restorations. Implants do not move, so the plan may shift neighboring teeth to create ideal space before implant placement. Coordinating orthodontics with restorative care often improves the final outcome.

Before And After: Retention Matters

Teeth have a natural tendency to drift, especially after closing spaces or correcting rotations. Retention is not optional. Wearing retainers as directed preserves your result and helps prevent future orthodontic relapse. Clear removable retainers are popular for nighttime wear, while bonded retainers hold select front teeth in place. Your plan will outline how long to wear retainers and how they will be maintained.
