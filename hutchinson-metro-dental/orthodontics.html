Adult Orthodontics vs. Braces for Teens

Two in five Americans say they won’t date someone with crooked teeth. In fact, 58 percent of Americans think straight teeth equals wealth and success. Perhaps this is why one in five adults now seek orthodontic treatment, especially with all the available options for clear braces and teeth straightening without braces.

Adult orthodontic treatments don’t differ significantly from the process used to straighten teeth in adolescents, but some noticeable differences include:

  • Adults don’t keep growing like teens, which make the treatments more stable and long-lasting.
  • Orthodontic treatment sometimes takes longer in adults because teeth have settled into their respective misalignment for a longer period of time.
  • Unhealthy habits like smoking, chewing tobacco or excessive alcohol consumption hinder successful orthodontic treatment. These bad habits are more prevalent in adults.
  • Adults tend to have more serious medical conditions like diabetes, sleep apnea and high blood pressure. These conditions prohibit the most successful response to teeth straightening.
  • There’s a greater chance that gum disease has set in, requiring additional dental procedures, such as a bone graft or gum graft.
  • Adults may have had previous dental work that can affect orthodontic treatments. If you have veneers, crowns or bridgework, your teeth straightening options may be more limited.

Generally, you will need to have teeth that are strong and healthy so they can be moved into the correct positions. You may be unsuitable for orthodontics if you have advanced gum disease or tooth decay; these conditions will need to be treated before you can have braces.

What Are the Most Common Orthodontic Problems?

  • Overlapping teeth.
  • Rotated teeth.
  • Overcrowded teeth.
  • Overbite, where the upper teeth protrude over the lower teeth too far.
  • Underbite, where the lower teeth protrude beyond the upper teeth.
  • Crossbite, where the upper teeth bite inside the lower teeth on one or both sides.
  • Open bite, where the upper and lower front teeth do not meet when you close your mouth.
  • Gaps between teeth
