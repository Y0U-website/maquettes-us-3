How All-on-X Dental Implants Work

Consultation and 3D Planning

Your treatment begins with a comprehensive evaluation, including advanced imaging and digital planning. This ensures precise implant placement and optimal long-term results.

Implant Placement

A small number of implants are strategically placed in areas of the jaw with the strongest bone support. Angled placement techniques may be used to maximize stability.

Temporary Teeth Placement

In many cases, a temporary fixed prosthesis can be placed shortly after surgery, allowing you to leave with functional teeth.

Final Restoration

After healing, your final custom-designed teeth are placed for optimal aesthetics, strength, and long-term performance.

Risks and Limitations

As with any surgical procedure, All-on-X implants have considerations:

A thorough evaluation helps determine your suitability and reduce risks.

  • Healing time is required before final teeth placement
  • Not all patients qualify for immediate loading
  • Implant failure is possible but uncommon with proper care
  • Smoking and certain medical conditions may impact success

Who Is a Good Candidate for All-on-X?

  • Have multiple missing or failing teeth
  • Wear dentures and want a fixed solution
  • Have adequate jawbone or qualify for grafting
  • Are in good overall health
  • Want a long-term, stable alternative to removable options

Who Is NOT a Candidate?

Alternative solutions can be discussed during your consultation.

  • Have uncontrolled medical conditions
  • Have severe bone loss without grafting options
  • Are unable to undergo oral surgery
  • Have poor oral hygiene habits
  • Are not committed to long-term implant maintenance

All-on-X vs Traditional Dental Implants

All-on-X differs significantly from placing individual implants:

For patients missing most or all teeth, All-on-X is often the more practical and streamlined option compared to replacing each tooth individually with Dental Implants .

  • Uses fewer implants to support a full arch
  • Requires less surgical time in many cases
  • Can reduce the need for bone grafting
  • Offers a more efficient full-mouth restoration

Alternatives to All-on-X

Removable Dentures

A lower-cost option, but lacks stability and can affect speech and eating.

Implant-Supported Dentures

Provides more stability than traditional dentures but may still be removable.

Individual Implants

Best for replacing a few missing teeth rather than a full arch.

In cases of insufficient bone, procedures like Bone Grafting may be recommended to support implant placement.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much do All-on-X dental implants cost in Charleston, SC?

Costs vary depending on the number of implants, need for additional procedures, and final restoration type. A consultation is required to provide an accurate estimate tailored to your case.

What is the difference between All-on-X and Teeth-In-A-Day?

All-on-X refers to the number and placement of implants supporting a full arch, while Teeth-In-A-Day refers to the ability to receive temporary teeth the same day as surgery. Many treatments combine both concepts.

How many implants are used in All-on-X?

Typically, 4 to 6 implants are placed per arch, depending on bone quality and treatment goals.

Is the procedure painful?

The procedure is performed with sedation, and most patients report minimal discomfort during and after surgery.

How long do All-on-X implants last?

With proper care and maintenance, implants can last decades, while the prosthetic teeth may need periodic replacement.

Who is a candidate for All-on-X implants?

Patients missing most or all teeth, or those with failing dental work, are often good candidates pending a full evaluation.
