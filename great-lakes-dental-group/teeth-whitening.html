What Causes Teeth to Stain?

Tooth discoloration happens for a variety of reasons. Some stains develop on the surface, while others form deeper within the enamel. Surface stains are often caused by foods and drinks like coffee, tea, red wine, berries, and soda. Tobacco use also leaves behind stubborn discoloration that’s difficult to remove with brushing alone.

Aging naturally darkens teeth as enamel thins and the yellowish dentin beneath becomes more visible. Certain medications, especially tetracycline antibiotics taken during childhood, can cause deep staining that’s harder to treat. Trauma to a tooth can also change its color over time.

Professional whitening works best on surface stains and age-related discoloration. If your staining is caused by other factors, the dentist can discuss alternative options like veneers or bonding.

Who Is a Good Candidate for Teeth Whitening in Wadsworth?

Most people with healthy teeth and gums are good candidates for professional whitening. If you have cavities, gum disease, or other oral health issues, those should be addressed first. Whitening works best on natural teeth, so if you have crowns, veneers, or fillings on your front teeth, keep in mind that those restorations won’t change color.

Whitening may not be ideal if you have extremely sensitive teeth or exposed roots. The dentist can assess your situation and recommend modifications or alternatives if sensitivity is a concern. Pregnant or nursing women are typically advised to wait before undergoing whitening treatments.

If you’re unsure whether whitening is right for you, a consultation can help clarify your options. The team will evaluate your teeth, discuss your goals, and determine the best approach for your specific needs.

How Long Do Results Last?

The longevity of your whitening results depends on your habits and lifestyle. Most people enjoy bright, white teeth for several months to a few years after treatment. If you drink a lot of coffee, tea, or wine, or if you smoke, stains will return more quickly. Brushing twice a day, flossing regularly, and scheduling routine cleanings help maintain your results.

Touch-up treatments can extend the life of your whitening. Some patients prefer to do a quick touch-up every six months or once a year to keep their smile looking fresh. The dentist can recommend a maintenance schedule based on how your teeth respond to treatment.

Managing Sensitivity After Whitening

Tooth sensitivity is a common side effect of professional whitening, but it’s usually temporary. You might notice increased sensitivity to hot or cold foods and drinks for a day or two after treatment. Using a toothpaste designed for sensitive teeth can help reduce discomfort. Avoiding very hot or very cold foods right after whitening also minimizes irritation.

If sensitivity persists or becomes uncomfortable, let the dentist know. Adjustments can be made to future treatments to reduce the likelihood of sensitivity. Most people find that any discomfort fades quickly and the results are worth the brief adjustment period.

Maintaining Your Brighter Smile

Once you’ve invested in professional whitening, a few simple habits can help you protect your results. Rinse your mouth with water after drinking coffee, tea, or wine. Use a straw for beverages that are likely to stain. Brush and floss daily to remove surface debris before it has a chance to settle in. Regular dental cleanings keep your teeth looking their best and prevent new stains from taking hold.

If you notice your teeth starting to dull over time, talk to the dentist about touch-up options. Maintaining a bright smile doesn’t require constant effort, just a bit of consistency.
