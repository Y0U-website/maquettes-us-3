Dental Implant Surgery

Taking these steps ahead of time helps us keep your procedure safe and your recovery on track.

  • Fasting: If receiving general anesthesia, follow fasting instructions carefully to prevent complications.
  • Medications: Review all current medications with our team in advance. Blood thinners in particular may need temporary adjustment.
  • Transportation: Arrange a ride home, as anesthesia and pain medication will affect your ability to drive.
  • Day-of essentials: Arrive in comfortable clothing and bring your insurance card, ID, and a current medication list.

Post-Op Recovery Guide

Research published in PMC on patients’ perception of recovery confirms that swelling and discomfort peak in the first few days and improve steadily with proper care. Follow these instructions closely during the first week.

If bone grafting was part of your procedure, our team will provide modified instructions specific to your case.

  • Gauze: Bite gently on gauze pads, changing every 30–45 minutes until bleeding subsides.
  • Ice: Apply 15 minutes on, 15 minutes off on day one; switch to warm compresses on day two.
  • Pain medication: Take prescribed pain relievers as directed. Don’t wait until the pain is severe.
  • No straws: Avoid for at least one week to protect the healing site.
  • Diet: Soft, protein-rich foods like eggs, yogurt, and smoothies for the first week; avoid hot, spicy, or chewy foods near the surgical site.
  • Rest: Take one to two days off from strenuous activity; keep your head elevated while sleeping.
  • Oral hygiene: Starting the day after surgery, rinse gently with warm salt water several times daily; avoid brushing directly over the surgical area.

When to Contact Us

Some swelling and discomfort after dental implant surgery are completely normal. Reach out to our office right away if you notice any of the following.

Early attention to these signs prevents more serious complications from developing, so don’t hesitate to reach out to us with any concerns.

  • Severe pain that does not respond to prescribed medication
  • Excessive bleeding that does not subside
  • Fever above 101°F
  • Increased swelling, pus, or a persistent bad taste
