Dental Implant Bone Grafting

  • Graft Placement : The surgeon makes an incision in the gum to expose the jawbone, placing graft material (from your body, a donor, or synthetic) to support new bone growth.
  • Securing and Closing : The graft is secured, and the area is sutured to protect it during healing.
  • Healing and Integration : Over the following months, the graft integrates with your natural bone, strengthening the jaw for future implant placement.

FAQ

  • What is a bone graft? There may be inadequate bone for implant placement if your tooth was removed many years ago and your bony ridge is extremely thin. In this case, a bone graft can be placed next to the thin bone and allowed to heal for three to six months. After the graft has fused to your pre-existing bone, the ridge will be re-entered and the implant placed. Bone grafting is usually a relatively comfortable office procedure. Many different bone-grafting materials are available, including your own bone.
  • Why would I need a bone graft? You may need bone grafting if the sinus cavities in your upper jaw are very large or very low and extend into the tooth-bearing areas. This often occurs when teeth in the back of a person’s upper jaw have been removed many years before, and the amount of bone available for implant placement is limited. A sinus grafting procedure is then required.
  • What is a sinus grafting procedure? The surgeon often performs the procedure in the office using local anesthesia and perhaps sedation. During this procedure, the membrane that lines the sinus will be located and elevated. Bone will then be added to restore the bone height and ensure that dental implants of an adequate length can be placed. This procedure often can be performed at the time of implant placement.

Dental Implants

Dental implants are widely considered the gold standard for replacing missing teeth. This advanced solution involves placing a small titanium post into the jawbone, where it acts as an artificial tooth root. Over time, the implant fuses with the bone, providing a strong and stable foundation for a custom crown, bridge, or denture.
