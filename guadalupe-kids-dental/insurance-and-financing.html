Understanding Your Dental Insurance Benefits

Dental insurance can vary widely from plan to plan, and it helps to know the basics before your child’s visit. Most plans are structured to cover a portion of preventive, diagnostic, and restorative treatments, though annual maximums and co-pays differ by provider. According to the American Dental Association’s Health Policy Institute, children with dental insurance are significantly more likely to receive regular dental care than those without it, and coverage rates among children remain higher than any other age group in the United States.

Medicaid and CHIP Coverage

We are committed to serving families who rely on Medicaid through the Texas Medicaid and Healthcare Partnership (TMHP). Public dental coverage through Medicaid and the Children’s Health Insurance Program (CHIP) gives eligible children access to the same high standard of preventive and restorative care that all of our patients receive. Our team is experienced in working with TMHP and will verify your child’s eligibility and benefits prior to their appointment so there are no surprises at checkout.

Additional Financial Policies

Responsibility for Minors

The parent or guardian who brings the child to their appointment is financially responsible for all services rendered, unless arrangements have been made prior to the appointment.

Missed Appointments

Appointment times are reserved specifically for your child. Unless rescheduled at least 24 hours in advance, we charge a $50 no-show fee. For scheduled sedation appointments, a $50 no-show fee will be applied to the account. Excessive missed appointments may result in being asked to seek dental services elsewhere.

Emergency Appointments

For emergency appointments after regular business hours, an after-hours fee is charged in addition to any services rendered at the visit. All emergency treatment must be paid in full at the time of service.

Past-Due Accounts

You are responsible for any balance on your account after 30 days, whether insurance has paid or not. After 90 days, the account will be considered past due and may be referred to a collection agency, with additional fees added to cover collection costs.
