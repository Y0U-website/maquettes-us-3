Who Is a Good Candidate for Dental Implants?

A good candidate for implant dentistry usually has healthy gums, enough jawbone support, and overall health that allows for dental treatment. However, candidacy is not always a simple yes-or-no answer. Some patients who have bone loss, gum concerns, or a history of tooth loss may still have options with the right treatment plan.

During your evaluation, our team may review the number of missing teeth, the condition of your gums, the amount of available jawbone, your bite, your medical history, and your goals for comfort, appearance, and function.

Patients who are unsure whether implants are possible should begin with a consultation. A personalized exam allows us to explain your options clearly and help you understand what may be needed before implant treatment can move forward.

What Is the Dental Implant Process Like From Start to Finish?

The dental implant procedure is usually completed in phases. This staged approach gives the mouth time to heal and allows the final restoration to be designed with the right fit, shape, and function.

Your first visit focuses on understanding your needs. We review your dental history, examine your mouth, discuss your goals, and answer your questions about implant treatment. The dentist then evaluates the missing tooth space, jawbone, bite, surrounding teeth, and gum tissue.

If an implant is appropriate, the implant post is placed in the jawbone. For adult patients who feel anxious about treatment or need additional comfort support, Dr. Nathan Jensen is IV Sedation Certified and can discuss whether sedation may be appropriate. After placement, the implant needs time to heal before the final crown, bridge, or denture is attached.

Do I Need a Bone Graft Before Getting Dental Implants?

Some patients need a bone graft before receiving an implant, but not everyone does. A bone graft may be recommended when the jawbone does not have enough height, width, or density to support the implant securely.

Bone loss can happen after a tooth has been missing for a long time, after gum disease, or after dental trauma. If grafting is needed, the goal is to create stronger support for the future implant.

The only way to know whether a graft is necessary is through an exam and treatment planning. Our team will explain what we see, why it matters, and how it may affect your timeline.

Can Dental Implants Replace Multiple Missing Teeth?

Yes, implants can often be used to replace more than one missing tooth. The right option depends on how many teeth are missing, where they are located, and what type of restoration will provide the best function.

A single tooth implant can support one custom crown. When several teeth are missing, implants may be used to support an implant bridge. An implant-supported denture may help improve stability for patients who are missing many teeth. For patients with extensive tooth loss or failing teeth, a broader full mouth restoration plan may be considered.

What Should I Expect During Dental Implant Recovery?

Recovery after implant treatment varies. Some patients have a straightforward healing process, while others need more time because of additional procedures, multiple implants, or individual health factors.

Common short-term expectations may include mild swelling, tenderness near the treatment site, temporary changes in diet, gentle cleaning instructions, and follow-up visits to monitor healing.

Most patients are asked to eat softer foods at first and avoid putting too much pressure on the healing implant. Our team provides specific aftercare instructions so you know how to protect the site and when to contact the office with questions.

How Do I Care for Dental Implants After Treatment?

Dental implant care is similar to caring for natural teeth, but consistency matters. While the implant itself cannot get a cavity, the surrounding gum tissue and supporting bone still need to stay healthy.

To care for an implant restoration, patients should brush daily, floss or use recommended cleaning tools, keep routine dental cleaning appointments, schedule regular exams, avoid chewing extremely hard objects, and ask about a night guard if clenching or grinding is a concern.

How Long Do Dental Implants Last?

Implants are designed to be a long-term tooth replacement option. Their longevity depends on oral hygiene, gum health, bite forces, routine dental visits, and overall health.

Factors that may affect how long an implant lasts include daily home care, professional cleanings, gum health, bone support, bite alignment, teeth grinding, tobacco use, medical conditions, and follow-up care.

With the right care, implants can provide lasting support for a crown, bridge, or denture. Our team monitors implant restorations during ongoing visits to help protect your investment in your smile.

How Much Do Dental Implants Cost?

The cost of implant tooth replacement depends on the details of the treatment plan. Because implants can be used in different ways, there is no single cost that applies to every patient.

Your treatment cost may depend on the number of teeth being replaced, whether an extraction is needed, whether bone grafting is recommended, the type of final restoration, the complexity of the case, the number of appointments involved, and any additional restorative needs.

During your consultation, our team can review your options and provide more specific information based on your exam.
