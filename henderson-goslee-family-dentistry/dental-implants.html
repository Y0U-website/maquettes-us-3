The Dental Implant Process

The dental implant procedure is planned and completed in stages to promote comfort and healing.

  • Evaluation and planning: A comprehensive exam, imaging, and a review of medical history help determine candidacy and plan the ideal position of each implant.
  • Preparatory care when needed: Tooth extraction, bone grafting, or a sinus lift may be recommended to build enough bone for support.
  • Implant placement: Under local anesthesia, the implant is positioned in the jawbone. Most patients report pressure rather than sharp pain.
  • Healing and osseointegration: Over several weeks to a few months, bone bonds to the implant. A temporary tooth or modified denture may be used during this time.
  • Abutment placement: A small connector is attached to the implant to support the final restoration.
  • Final restoration: A custom crown, bridge, or full-arch prosthesis is crafted for a precise fit and natural appearance.

Your Dental Implant Options

Treatment can be tailored to your needs and bone health:

  • Single-tooth implant: Replaces one missing tooth with an implant and crown.
  • Implant-supported bridge: Replaces several teeth using fewer implants than one per tooth.
  • Implant-retained overdenture: A removable denture that snaps to implants for improved stability.
  • Full-arch fixed bridge: A nonremovable prosthesis supported by multiple implants for full-arch tooth loss.

What to Expect

Most procedures are completed with local anesthesia. Mild soreness after placement is common and usually managed with over-the-counter pain relief, cold compresses, and a soft-food diet for a short period. Healing time varies by individual and the number of implants placed. Many patients can receive the final crown or bridge within three to six months, though some cases require more time for grafts to mature.

Good oral hygiene is essential. Brush twice daily, clean around implants with floss or specialty tools, and keep regular dental checkups. Factors such as smoking, uncontrolled diabetes, or teeth grinding can affect healing and long-term success. Discuss medical conditions and medications during your consultation to plan the safest approach.

With consistent home care and routine professional maintenance, dental implants can offer lasting function and aesthetics.
