What is wisdom tooth removal?

Your wisdom teeth, also known as your third set of molars, are located at the farthest position of your upper and lower jaw. These teeth typically begin to erupt between the ages of 17 and 25. Some wisdom teeth can erupt as early as 14!

While some people can have their wisdom teeth erupt similar to any other molar and without any complications, others do not have enough space for these teeth to come through or they become trapped and impacted in the jawbone. If these teeth only partially erupt, they can increase the risk of oral infection and the decay of surrounding teeth.

In most cases, your general dentist will see these teeth coming well before their normal eruption time and can see whether they may become a problem and often refer you to an oral surgeon for wisdom tooth removal before they become a problem.

Because of their location, and the fact they are often beneath the gumline, they require a more extensive surgical procedure than a simple tooth extraction.

Benefits from removing wisdom teeth

When a wisdom tooth does not come in as it should, it can cause all sorts of complications that can contribute to poor oral health and discomfort. Having your wisdom teeth removed, when necessary, offers a wide range of benefits including:

Reduces the risk of decay to the surrounding teeth

In addition, during these years, the bone is still soft, making for an easier extraction and faster healing time. As you get older, the risks for complications as well as difficulty of extraction increases over time. This may cause delayed healing as well as extended recovery / healing period.

Wisdom teeth can also contribute towards damaging other teeth, which may need to be removed as well if the damage is severe.

What happens afterwards? What will I feel like? What can I expect during recovery?

After surgery, you can expect to experience some swelling, pain, and bleeding. Within a few hours, bleeding should stop, though you may still experience a pink color to your saliva. Follow your surgeon’s after-care instructions, including what to avoid in order to reduce your risk of dry socket.

Symptoms should improve within a few days and be completely gone within a week. If you experience continued bleeding, new bleeding, or increasing pain, contact your surgeon immediately.

What happens on the day of wisdom tooth removal?

On the day of wisdom tooth removal, the surgeon begins by administering local anesthesia alone, or in combination with nitrous oxide sedation or general anesthesia. When you are ready, the surgeon begins with an incision to expose the tooth and bone and then works to remove the tooth.

Once removed, sutures may be used to close the wound and gauze is put in place to control bleeding and promote the formation of a clot. This clot helps to keep the socket closed and reduces the risk of exposure of the nerves and bone to air, or what is known as dry socket.

What is dry socket?

Dry socket is a painful condition that occurs when the clot that forms in the tooth socket is prematurely removed or does not form correctly, leaving the nerves and bone exposed.

To reduce the risk of dry socket, avoid spitting, smoking, using a straw, or brushing near the extraction site for 24-48 hours after your wisdom tooth removal.
