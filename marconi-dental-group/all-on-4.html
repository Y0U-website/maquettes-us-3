Signs You May Need All-on-4 Dental Implants

  • ✦ Failing Arch — Most of the teeth in one jaw are cracked, decayed, or loose, and repairing them one by one would cost more than replacing the whole arch.
  • ✦ Loose Teeth — Advanced gum disease has left teeth mobile, which means any restorations placed on them would sit on a foundation that keeps on giving way.
  • ✦ Unstable Denture — Your denture rocks, slips, or needs adhesive throughout the day, and the ridge underneath it has continued shrinking year after year since.
  • ✦ Restricted Diet — You have cut out foods you used to eat because chewing them is no longer realistic, and meals have quietly become something you manage.

Hear From Our Patients

The All-on-4 protocol is designed so a provisional arch can be fitted right after surgery, which is why patients rarely go without teeth. Whether your case allows for that depends on bone quality and stability at placement, and Dr. Kovar tells you before surgery day.

A denture rests on the gum ridge and comes out, while an All-on-4 arch is secured to implants and stays in your mouth. The fixed arch also transfers chewing force into bone, which helps preserve the ridge rather than letting it shrink.

A water flosser and a threader clear the space between the arch and the gum, which is the part that matters most for keeping the implants healthy. Regular hygiene visits include cleaning around the implants and checking the fit of the prosthesis.

Cost depends on how many arches are treated, the materials chosen, and whether extractions are part of surgery, so you receive a written figure covering every stage first. Cherry, CareCredit, and in-house financing are available for treatment of this scale.

Full coverage is rare, though many PPO plans pay toward the extractions or the prosthesis while treating the implants separately. We accept all major providers, work with out-of-network plans, and confirm exactly what applies before treatment begins.
