Dental Implants

In summary, dental implants provide a reliable, durable, and natural-looking solution for tooth replacement while preserving oral health and function, often better than traditional dental bridges. For patients seeking a long-term solution, implants are often the preferred treatment.

  • Bone Health Maintenance Implants are anchored directly into the jawbone, stimulating bone growth and preventing bone loss that typically occurs after tooth extraction. Dental bridges span the gap over a missing or extracted tooth. They do not provide the same level of stimulation, often leading to bone atrophy in the extraction site over time.
  • Longevity and Durability With proper care, dental implants can last a long time. Bridges generally need replacement every 7-15 years due to wear, decay, or loosening. Implants’ titanium posts integrate with the bone, creating a stable and long-lasting foundation.
  • Improved Oral Hygiene Dental implants can be cleaned like natural teeth since they do not rely on support from adjacent teeth. Bridges require specialized cleaning beneath the pontic (false tooth) area, which can be challenging and increase the risk of gum disease or decay.
  • Enhanced Function and Comfort Implants provide superior chewing efficiency and comfort because they mimic the root structure of natural teeth. Bridges may feel less stable and can sometimes cause discomfort due to their support method.
  • Aesthetic Advantages Dental implants offer a more natural appearance, as they are custom-made to match the surrounding teeth and gingiva. Bridges, especially if older or poorly fitted, can sometimes appear less natural.
