Frequently Asked Questions about Orthodontics

The following are some common questions that patients often ask when considering orthodontic treatment.

At what age should my child first see an orthodontist?

The American Association of Orthodontists recommends a check-up with an orthodontic specialist no later than age 7. Early examination allows the orthodontist to detect and evaluate problems and plan appropriate treatment at the right time.

Can adults benefit from orthodontic treatments?

Absolutely. Age is not a barrier to orthodontic treatment. Healthy teeth can be moved at any age. Many orthodontic problems can be corrected in adults, and more adults are seeking treatment now than ever before.

How long will my orthodontic treatment take?

The length of treatment varies on a case-by-case basis and depends on the severity of the problem, the patient’s growth, and the patient’s cooperation. On average, treatment with braces typically lasts from 1 to 3 years.

Will the Orthodontic treatment be painful?

Orthodontic treatments have improved significantly over the years. Discomfort after the application of braces is typically short-lived and can be managed with over-the-counter pain relief. After certain visits, teeth may be sore for a few days, but the overall process is relatively comfortable for most patients.

Will I still need to see my general dentist during orthodontic treatment?

Yes. Regular check-ups with your general dentist are essential during orthodontic treatment. Your dentist will determine the intervals between cleaning appointments while you are in braces or undergoing other orthodontic treatments.

Can I play sports or musical instruments with braces?

Yes. It might take a little adjusting, but braces will not stop you from participating in your favorite activities. If you play a contact sport, it’s recommended that you wear a mouthguard to protect your braces and your mouth.

How much does orthodontic treatment cost?

The cost of orthodontic treatment depends on the complexity of the problem, the type of treatment, and the length of time it takes. Your orthodontist will discuss the cost with you during your orthodontic consultation .
