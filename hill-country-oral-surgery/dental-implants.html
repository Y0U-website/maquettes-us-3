What Are Dental Implants?

A dental implant is a small, screw-shaped post made of titanium that is surgically placed into the jawbone to replace the root of a missing tooth. Over several months, the implant fuses with the surrounding bone through a natural process called osseointegration, creating a stable, permanent foundation.

Once your jaw is fully healed, an abutment and custom-made crown are attached to the implant by your restorative dentist, restoring the complete look and function of a natural tooth. Implants can also be used to support bridges, dentures, or full-arch restorations for patients missing multiple teeth.

What Are the Benefits of Dental Implants in Buda, TX?

Dental implants offer advantages that no other tooth replacement option can match. Because they replace the entire tooth structure, from the root beneath the bone to the crown above the gumline, implants function, feel, and look like natural teeth.

People consistently choose dental implants over other restorative dentistry options because:

At Hill Country Oral Surgery, our surgeons routinely provide patients with reliable implants that will predictably support their implants for a lifetime.

  • They protect your jawbone. Dental implants are the only restoration that mimics this natural stimulation, actively preserving bone density and preventing the facial changes that come with long-term bone loss.
  • They look and feel natural. Implant-supported crowns are custom-designed to match the shape, size, and shade of your surrounding teeth. Most patients say they cannot tell the difference between their implant and their natural teeth, and neither can anyone else.
  • They are built to last. With proper care, dental implants can last a lifetime. Traditional bridges typically need replacement every 10 to 15 years, and dentures require periodic relining and eventual replacement as the jawbone changes shape. Implants eliminate that cycle of ongoing maintenance and replacement costs.
  • They stand on their own. Unlike a bridge, which requires grinding down healthy adjacent teeth for support, a dental implant is a completely independent restoration. Your surrounding teeth remain untouched and uncompromised.
  • They restore full chewing strength. Dentures restore only a fraction of natural bite force, which limits what you can comfortably eat. Dental implants restore near-full chewing function, allowing you to eat the foods you enjoy without restriction or worry.
  • They are easy to care for. There are no special cleaning routines, adhesives, or soaking solutions required. You brush and floss around your implants the same way you care for your natural teeth, and you continue with your regular dental checkups.
  • They improve confidence and quality of life. Missing teeth and loose dentures can affect the way you eat, speak, and smile. Dental implants eliminate those daily frustrations and give patients the confidence to laugh, talk, and eat in social situations without a second thought.

How Do Dental Implants in Austin, TX Work?

A dental implant is made of three separate structures: the implant, the abutment, and the crown. The dental implant that oral surgeons refer to corresponds to the root portion of the tooth. The screw is what we place into the bone.

The screw is typically made of titanium, a strong and corrosion-resistant biocompatible metal that is commonly used in medical devices. The abutment serves as an “adapter” between the crown and the screw. The crown itself goes over the abutment and serves as the chewing surface, or what most people know as the actual tooth. In most healthy situations, excluding implant-supported dentures, the only visible structure of these three is the crown.

  • Used to support a single tooth
  • Bridged together to support multiple teeth
  • Used to support an entire arch of teeth—also known as the All-On-4 treatment concept

What Are Dental Implants Made Of?

During a typical dental implant surgery, several materials work together for long-term success and lifelike appearance, including:

Patients are increasingly asking about options with fewer visible metals, especially those near the smile line. This is why we also provide metal-free dental implants using zirconia when appropriate, offering a tooth-colored alternative that can blend beautifully with thinner gumlines.

  • Implant fixture (posts): Most commonly titanium or titanium alloy for strength and predictable osseointegration. Zirconia (ceramic) posts are used in metal-free cases.
  • Abutment (connector): Titanium for durability or zirconia for enhanced esthetics at the gumline.
  • Crown (tooth): High-strength zirconia, all-ceramic, or porcelain-fused-to-metal (PFM), depending on bite forces, location, and cosmetic goals—each selected to harmonize with surrounding crowns and natural teeth in crowns dentistry.
  • Prosthetics for full-arch solutions: For dental implant dentures (such as All-on-4/All-on-X), teeth may be crafted from acrylic or composite over a titanium or zirconia bar for durability and comfort.
  • Bone and soft-tissue support (when indicated): Autograft, allograft, xenograft, or alloplast graft materials and barrier membranes may be used to rebuild bones or contours for ideal implant placement.
  • Small components: Healing caps, cover screws, multi-unit abutments, and attachment systems (e.g., locators or bars) fine-tune fit and function for single-tooth, multi-tooth, or full-arch treatments.

Metal-Free Dental Implants

In addition to traditional metal dental implants, we also offer metal-free dental implants . These advanced implants are crafted from a strong ceramic material called zirconia. We recommend these implants for patients with a titanium allergy or sensitivity. They are also cosmetically beneficial for patients with thinning gums. Metal-free dental implants are white, similar in color to your natural teeth. The silver coloration of a metal implant may show through a thinning gumline, while metal-free implants look more natural.

How Do I Know I’m a Good Candidate for Dental Implants in Austin, TX?

Oral surgeons can use a CT scan or X-rays to determine if enough bone exists. Patients who lack adequate bone structure can undergo a bone grafting procedure for additional support. Further, patients who grind their teeth are also suitable candidates for a dental implant procedure. These patients are fitted with a special dental splint to wear during sleep. The splint protects dental implants during the healing process. Your dentist will measure and fit the dental splint during the procedure.

You may not be a suitable candidate for dental implants if:

  • You are experiencing periodontal disease (gum disease) and need additional treatment before receiving dental implants.
  • You have diabetes.
  • You are young and have an undeveloped jaw structure.
  • You smoke.

How Do I Prepare for Dental Implant Surgery in Austin, TX?

Preparing for your dental implant surgery in Buda, TX will help set you up for success. At Hill Country Oral Surgery, our team will walk you through everything you need to know during your consultation, but here is a general overview of what to expect as you get ready for your implant surgery.

Follow pre-operative instructions. If you will be receiving IV sedation or general anesthesia, you will be asked to avoid eating or drinking for a specified period before your appointment, typically after midnight the night before surgery. Your board-certified oral surgeon will provide detailed instructions tailored to your specific procedure and anesthesia plan. Please also visit our pre-anesthesia instructions page for further information.

Arrange for transportation. Patients who receive IV sedation or general anesthesia will not be able to drive themselves home after the procedure. Plan to have a responsible adult drive you to the appointment and back home afterward.

Review your medications. During your consultation, let your surgeon know about all medications and supplements you are currently taking. Certain medications, such as blood thinners, anti-inflammatory drugs, or specific supplements, may need to be paused or adjusted before surgery. Never stop or change a medication without first receiving guidance from your surgeon and your prescribing physician.

Stock up on soft foods. You will need to stick to a soft diet for the first several days following surgery. Having foods like yogurt, applesauce, mashed potatoes, smoothies, scrambled eggs, and soup ready at home before your appointment will make your recovery much easier.

Avoid smoking. If you use tobacco products, we strongly recommend stopping well in advance of your procedure. Smoking restricts blood flow to the surgical site and significantly increases the risk of implant failure and delayed healing. The longer you can abstain before and after surgery, the better your outcome will be.

Wear comfortable clothes. On the day of your procedure, dress in loose, comfortable clothing with short sleeves or sleeves that can be easily rolled up, especially if you will be receiving IV sedation or if PRF will be prepared from a blood draw.

If you have any questions as your surgery date approaches, please don’t hesitate to call our office. Our staff at Hill Country Oral Surgery is committed to ensuring every patient feels informed, prepared, and confident heading into their procedure.

What Can I Expect from the Dental Implant Process in Austin, TX?

The entire dental implant process is variable and dependent on each patient’s situation. The time from placement to final restoration can take, on average, 3-6 months. In extreme circumstances, it can take longer.

Dental implant surgery itself is more comfortable than most patients anticipate. After your anesthesia is administered, your Austin oral surgeon will make a small incision in the gum tissue and carefully place the implant post into the jawbone at the precise location determined during your treatment planning.

Implant surgery typically takes about 30 to 60 minutes per implant, though this can vary depending on the complexity of your case and whether additional procedures such as bone grafting or a sinus lift are being performed at the same time. Most patients who opt for IV sedation have little to no memory of the procedure and are pleasantly surprised by how straightforward the experience is.

What are All-On-4 Dental Implants?

All-On-4 dental implants are a set of permanent fabricated teeth surgically placed into the jaw as dental implants. They have the appearance and the feel of natural teeth. All-On-4 dental implants:

  • Are not taken out
  • Require no adhesives
  • Can be brushed and cleaned like natural teeth
  • Eliminate the discomfort of dentures
  • Allow for increased chewing force
  • Prevent bone deterioration
