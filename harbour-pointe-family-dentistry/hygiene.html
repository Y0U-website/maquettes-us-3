  • What happens if I skip a cleaning visit? Skipping a cleaning doesn’t just mean plaque builds back up — it also means we miss the chance to catch small issues, like a cavity or early gum disease, while they’re still simple and inexpensive to treat. What would have been a straightforward cleaning can turn into a longer, costlier procedure down the road.
  • Does my dental insurance cover the cost of a cleaning? Most dental insurance plans cover two cleaning visits per year at little to no out-of-pocket cost. Coverage details vary by plan, so our team is happy to verify your specific benefits before your appointment.
  • What’s the difference between a regular cleaning and a deep cleaning? A regular cleaning removes plaque and tartar from the surface of your teeth and just below the gumline. A deep cleaning (scaling and root planing) goes further beneath the gumline to treat early gum disease, and is only recommended when your hygienist sees signs that a standard cleaning won’t be enough.
  • What can I do between visits to keep cavities from forming? Brushing twice a day, flossing daily, and cutting back on sugary or acidic snacks go a long way. Sticking to your six-month cleaning schedule matters too, since it lets us catch and treat the very early stages of decay before a cavity actually forms.

Getting the Most Out of Your Cleaning Visit

Brush and floss as you normally would beforehand. There’s no need to do a deep clean before your appointment — that’s exactly what we’re there for — but showing up with your usual routine intact helps your hygienist get a clear picture of your day-to-day habits.

Let your hygienist know about any new sensitivity, bleeding gums, or changes to your medical history since your last visit. Small details like these often shape how we approach your cleaning that day.

Some patients notice mild gum tenderness or tooth sensitivity for a day or so after a thorough cleaning, especially if it’s been a while between visits. This is normal and settles quickly with your regular brushing routine.
