Dental Implants

Whether you’re missing one tooth or several, implants provide a rock-solid foundation that lets you eat, speak, and smile with complete confidence.

Unlike dentures that slip around or bridges that require grinding down healthy teeth, dental implants stand on their own. They’re surgically placed into your jawbone, where they fuse with the bone tissue to create a permanent anchor that won’t budge.

Understanding How Dental Implants Work

A dental implant consists of three main components that work together. The implant itself is a small titanium post that acts as an artificial tooth root. This post gets placed directly into your jawbone during a surgical procedure. Titanium is special because your body readily accepts it, and bone actually grows around it in a process called osseointegration—basically, your jawbone and the implant become a single, solid unit.

The second piece is an abutment, which connects to the top of the implant and pokes through your gum tissue. Think of it as the connector piece that links everything together. Finally, the crown—the part that looks like a tooth—attaches to the abutment. We custom-design each crown to match your natural teeth in color, shape, and size, so it blends right in with your smile.

We use advanced 3D imaging and CBCT scanning technology to plan your implant placement with incredible precision. These tools let us see your jawbone structure in detail and identify the perfect position for your implant before we ever begin the procedure.

Why Dental Implants Beat Other Options

With proper care, dental implants can last a lifetime. That’s pretty remarkable when you compare them to bridges, which typically need replacement every 10 to 15 years, or dentures, which require adjustments and replacements as your mouth changes over time. Implants are a one-time investment in your smile.

Here’s something most people don’t realize—when you lose a tooth, the jawbone underneath starts to deteriorate because it’s no longer getting stimulation from chewing. Dental implants solve this problem by acting just like natural tooth roots. They stimulate the bone when you chew, which keeps it strong and prevents that sunken facial appearance that sometimes happens with tooth loss.

Traditional bridges require filing down the healthy teeth on either side of the gap to serve as anchors. That means you’re damaging perfectly good teeth to replace a missing one. Dental implants don’t involve your other teeth at all—they stand independently and leave your healthy teeth untouched.

People with dentures often have to avoid certain foods because they’re worried about slippage or discomfort. Dental implants eliminate those concerns completely. Bite into an apple, enjoy a steak, eat corn on the cob—your implants can handle it all because they’re anchored directly into your bone.

The Dental Implant Process at Kraja and Sidhu Dental

Getting dental implants happens in stages, which gives your body time to heal properly between steps. During your initial consultation, we examine your mouth thoroughly and take detailed digital images to assess your bone density and overall oral health. We’ll discuss your medical history and determine whether you’re a good candidate for implants.

The first surgical phase involves placing the titanium post into your jawbone. We make sure you’re completely comfortable during this procedure, and most patients are surprised by how manageable the process is. After placement, you’ll need several months for the implant to integrate with your bone—usually around three to six months, though this varies by individual.

Once osseointegration is complete, you’ll return for a quick procedure to attach the abutment. Your gums need a couple of weeks to heal around this connector piece. Finally, we attach your custom-made crown, and you’re all set with a fully restored tooth.

Some patients can receive same-day temporary crowns depending on their situation, but permanent crowns always go on after the healing process finishes.

Single Tooth vs. Multiple Teeth Replacement

Missing just one tooth? A single dental implant with a crown handles that perfectly. But if you’re missing several teeth or even all of them, implants still work beautifully. Multiple implants can support a bridge without touching your natural teeth, or they can anchor a full denture securely in place.

Implant-supported dentures are game-changers for people who’ve struggled with traditional dentures. Instead of relying on suction or adhesives, these dentures snap onto implants, so they never slip or click when you’re talking or eating. You get the stability of implants with the comprehensive coverage of dentures.

Taking Care of Your Dental Implants

The great news? Caring for dental implants is exactly the same as caring for natural teeth. Brush twice daily, floss daily, and visit us for regular checkups and cleanings. That’s it. The crown can’t get cavities since it’s made of porcelain or ceramic, but you still need to keep your gums healthy and prevent infection around the implant.

Dr. Emona Kraja or Dr. Randeep Sidhu and our hygiene team will check your implants during routine visits to make sure everything looks good and functions properly. We’ll also take periodic X-rays to monitor the bone around your implants.

Am I a Good Candidate for Dental Implants?

Most healthy adults make excellent candidates for dental implants. You need adequate bone density to support the implant, but even if you’ve experienced some bone loss, we might be able to perform a bone graft to build up the area first. Good oral hygiene habits and overall health matter too, since they affect how well you heal.

Certain conditions, like uncontrolled diabetes or active gum disease, need management before implant placement, and heavy smoking can interfere with healing. We will evaluate your individual situation and let you know whether implants are right for you or if we need to address any issues first.
