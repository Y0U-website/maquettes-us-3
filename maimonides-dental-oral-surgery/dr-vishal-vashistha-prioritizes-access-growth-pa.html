  • Non-small cell lung cancer: Clinical investigators are exploring a new therapy, targeting the KRAS G12C mutation, which promotes lung cancer growth. They’re comparing outcomes in patients who receive olomorasib (a new inhibitor that targets this mutation) and pembrolizumab (the standard immunotherapy) with those who receive pembrolizumab alone.
  • Advanced esophageal cancer: Researchers are evaluating a potential treatment for pretreated, metastatic esophageal cancer. They’re examining the efficacy of ifinatamab deruxtecan, a “smart” cancer treatment wherein this antibody–drug conjugate has a potent antitumor activity on tumors that have a high B7-H3 expression.

A commitment to strong relationships with patients and providers

Maintaining patient and provider relationships built on trust and transparency is paramount, according to Dr. Vashistha.

“When patients come to Maimonides, they can be confident they’re seeing an expert with great knowledge and insight into the types of cancer they treat,” he said, noting that Maimonides strives to see every patient within three days of referral. “And, referring providers know when they send us their patients, they’re getting a cadre of oncology, surgery, imaging, and pathology partners who are dedicated to collaboration and open communication.”

Ultimately, as he steps further into his leadership role, Dr. Vashistha wants providers—and the patients they care for—to know that Maimonides remains committed to its role as a world-class institution in Brooklyn’s backyard.

“We’re very proud that we take care of a large volume of patients from diverse backgrounds,” he said. “We have excellent, experienced oncologists, surgeons, and radiologists who work side by side with referring providers to deliver the highest level of care to every patient we serve.”
