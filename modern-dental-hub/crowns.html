Treatment Purpose

Dental crowns serve both cosmetic and restorative purposes. They’re used when a tooth is too damaged for a filling but not so compromised that it needs to be removed. Crowns are designed to:

A crown effectively restores the structure, strength, and function of your tooth while giving you a natural, confident smile.

  • Protect a cracked, worn, or weakened tooth
  • Restore a tooth after root canal therapy
  • Cover a large filling when little tooth remains
  • Anchor a dental bridge
  • Cover a dental implant
  • Improve the appearance of discolored or misshapen teeth

Treatment Outcome

Once placed, your dental crown will function like a natural tooth. You’ll be able to chew, smile, and speak comfortably without worrying about damage or pain. Most crowns last 10 to 15 years or more with proper care.

Our team works with precision and artistry to ensure your crown not only protects your tooth—but enhances your overall smile.

  • A natural-looking restoration that matches your existing teeth
  • Improved chewing and speaking function
  • Relief from sensitivity or discomfort in a damaged tooth
  • Long-term protection against further decay or cracking

Am I a Candidate?

You may be a good candidate for a crown if you:

If your tooth is beyond repair or cannot support a crown, we may recommend an extraction and discuss options like implants or dentures. During your consultation, we’ll assess your teeth and recommend the most effective treatment to restore your smile.

  • Have a cracked or fractured tooth
  • Have a tooth that is severely worn or decayed
  • Recently had a root canal
  • Need to replace a large filling
  • Are receiving a dental implant or bridge

Preparing for Treatment

Preparing for a crown is simple, and we’ll make sure you feel comfortable from start to finish. Here’s what to expect:

We’ll review all your options, including materials and timelines, and keep you informed every step of the way.

  • Exam & Consultation: We’ll take digital X-rays and evaluate the tooth to determine the best treatment approach.
  • Tooth Preparation: The tooth is gently reshaped to accommodate the crown. If decay is present, it’s removed and the structure is rebuilt as needed.
  • Digital Impressions: We take accurate scans or molds of your tooth to ensure a perfect fit.
  • Temporary Crown: A temporary crown is placed to protect your tooth while your final crown is being made.

With regular dental checkups and proper care, your crown can stay strong and beautiful for many years.

Avoid chewing hard foods until the numbness wears off and you’re used to the new crown.

Treatment Side Effects

Crowns are safe and reliable, but you may experience some mild, short-term effects:

These effects are usually minor and temporary. Our team will monitor your progress and make sure your crown fits and functions perfectly.

  • Sensitivity: It’s common to feel slight sensitivity to hot or cold for a few days. This typically resolves on its own.
  • Tender gums: After placement, your gums around the crown might feel tender or inflamed for a short time.
  • Bite adjustment: If your bite feels “off,” let us know. A small adjustment can make a big difference in comfort.

What Our Patients Say

Will my crown look natural?

Yes! We use tooth-colored materials like porcelain, ceramic, or zirconia to match your existing teeth. Your crown will be custom-designed for a seamless look.

Is getting a crown painful?

Not at all. We use local anesthesia to ensure you’re completely comfortable during the procedure. Afterward, you might feel a little soreness, but it’s mild and temporary.

What materials do you use for crowns?

  • Porcelain and ceramic – for natural appearance, ideal for front teeth
  • Zirconia – highly durable and tooth-colored, great for back teeth
  • Porcelain-fused-to-metal – a blend of strength and aesthetics (less common today)

Can I eat normally with a crown?

Yes, once it’s fully bonded and healed, your crown will function like a natural tooth. Just avoid very sticky or hard foods to prevent damage.

Do I need a crown after a root canal?

In most cases, yes. After a root canal, the tooth can become brittle. A crown reinforces and protects it, preventing fractures and preserving function.

Can I get a crown on a dental implant?

Absolutely. Once your implant has healed, we can place a crown on top to restore the full look and function of your tooth.

Myths vs. Facts

While crowns can improve appearance, they are primarily used to restore strength and function to a damaged tooth.

When properly bonded, crowns are very secure. They can last over a decade with the right care.

Modern crowns are made from high-quality materials that look just like natural teeth —even up close.
