What Exactly Is Gum Disease?

Gum disease, or periodontal disease, manifests in two primary forms: gingivitis a mild form causing gum inflammation and bleeding, and periodontitis a more severe stage that can lead to bone and tissue damage. These conditions are often a result of plaque and tartar buildup, leading to infections that affect your oral health. It can also contribute to systemic issues like diabetes and heart or lung diseases.

What is the Best Way to Prevent Gum Disease?

Preventive dentistry remains the most effective line of defense against gum disease. Regular dental exams and cleanings form the foundation of good oral health, helping you avoid issues like cavities and gingivitis that can diminish your quality of life. In our practice, we combine new technologies and traditional approaches to offer comprehensive preventive care, aligning with the American Dental Association s recommendation of bi-annual checkups.

What Steps Can I Take On My Own?

Preventing gum disease revolves around diligent oral care and regular dental checkups. Daily brushing and flossing effectively remove plaque, a critical factor in the development of gum disease. However, even the most thorough home care can miss some areas, making regular professional cleanings essential. These cleanings eliminate tartar buildup and help in detecting early signs of gum disease.

Lifestyle choices also play a significant role in prevention quitting smoking and maintaining a balanced diet can significantly lower the risk of gum disease. Additionally, being aware of one s medical conditions, like diabetes, and managing them effectively can also aid in preventing periodontal issues. Consistent dental visits, typically recommended every six months, enable early detection and management of any signs of gum disease, thereby maintaining overall oral health.

What Is the Treatment for Gingivitis?

Gingivitis, the early stage of gum disease, can usually be reversed with professional cleanings and improved oral hygiene at home. Our hygienists provide thorough cleaning and personalized brushing and flossing instructions, ensuring your gums remain healthy. Regular teeth cleanings are crucial, as they address areas your brush and floss can t reach. Since gingivitis can sometimes be asymptomatic, these routine visits are vital for early detection and treatment.

How Is Advanced Gum Disease Treated?

Untreated gingivitis can progress to periodontitis, where the infection attacks the bone supporting your teeth, potentially leading to tooth loss. At our Encino dental office, we offer non-surgical periodontal therapy, including scaling and root planing. This treatment removes plaque and tartar and smooths root surfaces to prevent further infection. A complimentary six-week reevaluation follows to monitor your progress and ensure your gums are healing properly.
