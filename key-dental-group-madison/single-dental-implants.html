What Is a Single Dental Implant?

A single dental implant is a small titanium post. Our dentist places it directly into your jawbone, right where your missing tooth used to be. Think of it like an artificial tooth root.

Over a few months, the implant fuses with your bone. This process is called osseointegration. Once it is fully bonded, a dental crown — a custom-made cap that looks like a real tooth — is placed on top.

The result? A replacement tooth that looks, feels, and works just like the real thing. No one will be able to tell the difference.

A single dental implant has three main parts:

The implant post — The titanium screw that goes into the jawbone

The abutment — A small connector that holds the crown in place

The dental crown — The visible part that looks like your natural tooth

Together, these three parts create a complete tooth from root to tip.

Advantages of Getting Single Dental Implants

There are several ways to replace missing teeth: bridges, partial dentures, and implants. But implants stand apart for good reasons. Here is what makes them special:

They Look and Feel Natural

Your crown is custom-made to match the color, shape, and size of your other teeth. Nobody around you will know it is a replacement. You will even forget it is not your real tooth.

They Are Built to Last

Dental implants are designed to be a permanent solution. With proper oral hygiene — brushing, flossing, and regular dental checkups — your implant can last many decades. For many patients, it lasts a lifetime.

No Cavities

Since implants are not living tissue, they cannot develop dental caries (cavities). Your crown can keep its color and shine for years. That means less worry about decay at that spot.

They Keep Your Jawbone Healthy

When you lose a tooth, your jawbone starts to shrink in that area. This is called bone resorption. An implant acts like a natural tooth root, stimulating bone and keeping it strong and healthy. Our dentist in Madison recommends implants for patients who want a long-lasting, natural-looking, and healthy solution for missing teeth.

They Do Not Damage Nearby Teeth

Traditional dental bridges require filing down the healthy teeth on either side. With an implant, those adjacent teeth are left completely untouched.

Easy to Care For

You brush and floss an implant the same way you do your natural teeth. No soaking in a glass overnight. No special adhesives. Just your normal dental routine.

Improved Chewing and Speaking

A missing tooth can make it hard to bite into an apple or pronounce certain sounds. With single dental implants, you can eat what you love and speak clearly again.

The Single Dental Implant Process — Step by Step

We know that dental procedures can feel a little scary when you do not know what to expect. At Key Dental Group, we follow a simple and careful process for single dental implants in Madison, MS:

Step 1: Your Initial Consultation

Your journey starts with a conversation. Our dental office near you will examine your mouth, take dental X-rays, and review your medical and dental history. We check that your jawbone is strong enough to support an implant. If there is any bone loss, we may discuss a bone graft first.

Step 2: Implant Placement

On the day of your implant surgery, the area is numbed with a local anesthetic. Most patients feel little to no pain during the procedure. The titanium post is carefully inserted into the jawbone. You might feel some mild soreness in the days after, but it is manageable.

Step 3: Healing and Osseointegration

This is the healing phase. Over the next few months, the implant fuses with your bone through a process called osseointegration. This creates a strong, stable foundation. During this time, you can go about your normal life.

Step 4: Placing the Abutment

Once the implant is fully bonded, a small connector called an abutment is attached to the top of the post. This is a quick and simple step.

Step 5: Fitting Your Crown

We take impressions of your teeth so the lab can create a crown that matches your smile perfectly. Once it is ready, we attach it to the abutment. Your new tooth is complete, and your implant looks and functions just like a natural tooth.

Step 6: Aftercare and Follow-Up

We schedule a follow-up visit to make sure everything looks and feels right. After that, keep up with your regular dental visits every six months, and your implant should serve you well for many years.
