Sometimes implants are needed in highly esthetic or difficult-to reach areas. Dr. Consky might elect to take digital impressions and use CBCT imaging to make a digital model of your mouth. She will then perform your surgery with an engineer and use a 3D printer to make a surgical guide printed just for you. This guide improves the predictability of your implant placement on your day of surgery. More upfront planning often leads to better outcomes, less time for you in the dental chair, and better esthetic and functional results for your implant restorations.

Full Arch Replacement

Whether you’re missing one tooth or several, dental implants can be customized to restore strength, function, and confidence. We’ll evaluate your needs and create a personalized plan designed to replace missing teeth in a way that feels stable, natural, and secure.

The Benefits of Dental Implants

Restored Function Dental implants are designed to function like natural tooth roots, giving your restoration the support it needs for comfortable biting, chewing, and daily care.

Personalized Treatment Each implant restoration is custom-made to match the color, shape, and size of your surrounding teeth, helping it blend naturally with your smile.

Long-Term Oral Health Support Implants stimulate the jawbone, preventing bone loss after a tooth is missing, protecting both your oral health and facial structure over time.

The Implant Treatment Process

Implant Placement

Implant Placement

At the consultation appointment, the implant is placed into the jawbone where the missing tooth root once was. The area is then cleaned and closed so the healing process can begin. This crucial step creates the stable foundation your future restoration will rely on.

Healing Begins

Healing Begins

After placement, the implant needs time to bond with the surrounding bone. The gum tissue typically begins healing within the first couple of weeks, while the deeper integration process can take several months. We will schedule follow-up visits to ensure your healing stays on track.

Smile Restored

Smile Restored

Once the implant has fully integrated, a custom restoration is created to match your surrounding teeth. This restoration is attached to the implant with a small connector piece, completing your new tooth with a secure, natural-looking result.

Frequently Asked Questions

Check out these frequently asked questions, or call us to speak with our team.

What is a single tooth implant?

A single tooth implant is a long-term solution for one missing tooth. A titanium post is placed into the jawbone to act as a tooth root, topped with a custom crown that restores the visible part of the tooth.

Because the implant fuses with the bone, it does not rely on neighboring teeth or removable parts. It looks natural, feels comfortable, and stays secure while you eat, speak, and smile.

How are dental implants made?

Dental implants are made from medical-grade titanium, which bonds with the jawbone during healing for lasting strength and stability. The restoration on top is custom-designed for your smile, and once in place, the two work together to replace the missing tooth completely.

How long will my dental implant last?

Dental implants are known for lasting many years, and with proper care, the implant post can often last for decades. The crown attached to the implant may eventually need to be replaced because of normal wear, but the implant itself is designed to provide long-term support. Good home care, routine dental visits, and healthy gum tissue all play an important role in protecting your implant over time.

Can my dental implant become infected?

Yes, infection around a dental implant can happen, although it is not common when the implant is cared for properly. Brushing, flossing, following aftercare instructions, and keeping up with recommended checkups can help reduce your risk. If you notice tenderness, swelling, bleeding, or discomfort around an implant, it is best to schedule an evaluation promptly.

Do dental implants stop bone loss?

Dental implants preserve bone by stimulating the jawbone the way a natural tooth root does. Dental implants preserve bone by stimulating the jawbone the way a natural tooth root does. When a tooth is missing, the bone in that area no longer receives pressure from chewing and begins to shrink. By replacing the root, an implant maintains bone density and supports the natural shape of your face.

Can I get an implant in one day?

In some cases, an implant and temporary tooth can be placed on the same day, depending on your bone health, the location of the missing tooth, and your overall treatment needs. The implant will still need time to heal and bond with the bone before your final custom restoration is placed. A consultation is the best way to find out if same-day treatment is right for you.

What conditions can hurt implant healing?

Some conditions, such as uncontrolled diabetes, smoking cigarettes, osteoporosis or vitamin D deficiency, history of IV or long-term oral bisphosphonate use, or head and neck radiation affect bone healing in the jaws. Talk to your surgeon about these and other health concerns to discuss risks and benefits of implant placement.
