Cosmetic dentistry can be used to straighten teeth, close gaps, brighten teeth, repair cracks and/or chips and more. Physical appearance is the most obvious benefit, but physical, emotional and mental health are also improved thanks to cosmetic dentistry.

Dental crowns are similar to veneers, but instead of simply covering the front of the tooth, they encase both the front and the back of the tooth. This option is typically used on to keep cracked or otherwise very weak teeth in place. Dental bridges allow gaps between your teeth due to extractions to be filled in an aesthetic, functional way.

Dental extractions, also known as tooth removals, are a common dental procedure performed to address a variety of dental issues. While the thought of having a tooth pulled may raise anxiety, it is important to understand that extractions are often necessary to maintain oral health and prevent further complications.

Dental implants have emerged as a revolutionary treatment modality in the field of restorative dentistry, offering a permanent and aesthetically pleasing solution for replacing missing teeth. Unlike traditional dentures or bridges, which rely on adjacent teeth for support, dental implants are surgically anchored into the jaw bone, functioning as artificial tooth roots. This unique approach provides several advantages over conventional tooth replacement options.

Digital X-rays have revolutionized the field of dentistry, offering a multitude of benefits over traditional film-based X-rays. Unlike traditional X-rays, which require chemical processing and produce physical film, digital X-rays capture images electronically and store them on a computer. This shift to digital technology has transformed the way dentists diagnose and treat dental conditions, resulting in enhanced patient care and improved clinical outcomes.

In the field of dentistry, cone-beam computed tomography (CBCT) X-ray has emerged as a revolutionary diagnostic tool, providing dentists with unparalleled insights into the intricate structures of the jaw, teeth, and surrounding tissues. Unlike traditional two-dimensional (2D) X-rays, CBCT X-ray generates high-resolution three-dimensional (3D) images, offering a comprehensive view of the oral cavity from multiple angles.

The Solea CO2 laser stands as a groundbreaking innovation in the realm of dental technology, offering a transformative approach to a wide range of dental procedures. Unlike traditional methods that employ drills, needles, and scalpels, the Solea laser utilizes a precisely controlled beam of light to perform a variety of tasks, from cavity fillings and root canals to soft tissue surgeries and bone recontouring.
