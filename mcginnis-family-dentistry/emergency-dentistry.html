What to Do First

For a cracked tooth, rinse gently with warm water and use a cold compress on the outside of the face if swelling is present.

For life threatening symptoms, uncontrolled bleeding, severe facial trauma, difficulty breathing or swallowing, or a suspected broken jaw, call 911 or seek emergency medical care.

  • Call the dental office as soon as possible and provide details.
  • For a knocked out permanent tooth, avoid touching the root. Keep the tooth moist and seek immediate dental care.
  • Do not place aspirin directly on a painful tooth or gum tissue.

Emergency Examination

The visit will focus on identifying the source of the problem, relieving urgent symptoms when possible, and explaining the recommended next step. Definitive treatment may occur the same day or may be scheduled or referred depending on the condition.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do you see new patients for emergencies?

Yes, we welcome new patients for urgent dental concerns. Call our office and our team will do their best to see you promptly, alongside our established patients.

Can I wait to see if tooth pain goes away?

Persistent, severe, or worsening pain should be evaluated. Delaying care may allow some conditions to become more complex.

Should I go to the hospital for a dental emergency?

A dentist is usually the appropriate provider for tooth related treatment. Seek emergency medical care for life threatening symptoms, severe facial trauma, difficulty breathing or swallowing, uncontrolled bleeding, or a suspected broken jaw.
