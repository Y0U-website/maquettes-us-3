This approach is often more conservative than a traditional three-unit bridge because it preserves surrounding enamel and tooth structure.

  • Helps preserve jawbone and facial structure over time.
  • Does not require altering adjacent healthy teeth for support.
  • Requires standard oral hygiene practices to maintain health.

Implant-supported bridges for multiple gaps

For two or more adjacent missing teeth, implant-supported bridges replace the missing crowns while using implants for support. This solution restores balanced chewing forces and prevents adjacent teeth from shifting, all without grinding down healthy teeth for support.

Full-arch solutions: fixed or removable options

Full-arch restoration using a strategic number of implants offers patients a permanent alternative to conventional dentures. Options can range from fixed hybrid bridges—anchored and not removed by the patient—to implant-retained overdentures that attach securely yet can be removed for cleaning. Both approaches provide improved stability and confidence in daily activities.

Preparing for Treatment and What to Expect

Who makes a good candidate?

Candidacy for implants depends on several factors, including general health, oral hygiene, and available bone. Many patients who have been told they are not candidates in the past can now be treated thanks to advanced imaging and bone grafting techniques. A comprehensive evaluation determines the safest and most effective path forward for each individual.

When bone grafting becomes part of the plan

Sufficient bone volume is essential to support an implant long term. In cases where bone has resorbed due to prior tooth loss or periodontal disease, bone grafting can rebuild the ridge to create a stable foundation. This restorative step is routine and can be performed either at the time of extraction or as a preparatory procedure before implant placement.

The placement process and recovery

Implant surgery is usually completed as an outpatient procedure with local anesthesia and options for sedation when appropriate. After placement, there is a healing period while the implant integrates with bone. During that time we monitor progress and provide clear guidance on caring for the site to encourage predictable healing. In many cases a temporary restoration can be provided so you don’t have to wait to regain function and appearance.

Frequently Asked Questions What are dental implants and how do they work? + Dental implants are small biocompatible posts, typically made from titanium or titanium alloy, that are placed into the jawbone to replace the root of a missing tooth. Once placed, the surrounding bone gradually bonds with the implant through a process called osseointegration , creating a stable foundation for a crown, bridge, or denture. The implanted post supports a custom restoration that restores chewing function and the appearance of a natural tooth.

This root-level support is what distinguishes implants from other tooth replacements because it preserves bone stimulation and helps maintain facial contours. Implant restorations are custom shaded and contoured to match adjacent teeth so the result blends seamlessly with your smile. With proper planning and care, implants integrate into a patient’s oral health plan as a durable long-term solution.

How are dental implants different from bridges and dentures?

Dental implants replace both the tooth and its root, while traditional bridges and removable dentures typically replace only the visible portion of the tooth. Because implants are anchored in bone, they provide greater stability for chewing and speaking and avoid the slipping or rubbing sometimes associated with removable prostheses. Implants also do not require alteration of adjacent healthy teeth for support in the way some bridges do.

Another important distinction is that implants help maintain bone volume by transferring chewing forces to the jaw, which preserves facial structure over time. Restorations attached to implants are fabricated with precise digital workflows and custom shading to achieve a lifelike result. For many patients these characteristics translate into improved comfort, function, and long-term oral health.

Who is a good candidate for dental implants?

A good candidate for dental implants is generally someone in good overall health with adequate jawbone volume and healthy soft tissues in the area where the implant will be placed. Factors such as uncontrolled systemic disease, active untreated periodontal disease, or habits that compromise healing are evaluated during the consultation. Advanced imaging and clinical exams help determine bone quality, nerve locations, and any preparatory treatments that may be needed to support an implant.

Many patients who were previously told they were not candidates can now be treated thanks to modern bone grafting and guided implant techniques. Age alone is not a disqualifying factor; candidates range from adults replacing a single tooth to older adults seeking full-arch solutions. A personalized evaluation at our Brookfield office ensures the safest and most effective approach for each individual.

What is the implant placement process and how long does it take to heal?

Implant placement typically begins with a comprehensive evaluation and treatment plan, which includes clinical exams and 3D imaging to map bone anatomy and vital structures. The surgical placement of the implant is usually performed under local anesthesia and, when appropriate, sedation options are available for comfort. After placement there is a healing phase during which the implant integrates with the bone; this process commonly takes several weeks to a few months depending on the case and site-specific factors.

During the healing period patients receive clear instructions for caring for the surgical site and may be provided with a temporary restoration so function and appearance are maintained. Follow-up visits monitor osseointegration and tissue health before the final abutment and restoration are attached. Careful planning and phased treatment allow for predictable outcomes and long-term stability.

When is bone grafting necessary and what does it involve?

Bone grafting is recommended when there is insufficient jawbone volume or density to support an implant safely over the long term. Bone loss can occur after tooth extraction, chronic periodontal disease, or long-standing missing teeth, and grafting rebuilds the ridge to provide a stable foundation. The procedure involves placing bone material—autograft, allograft, xenograft, or synthetic substitute—into the deficient area to encourage new bone formation.

Bone grafting can be performed at the time of extraction, before implant placement, or in conjunction with implant surgery depending on clinical needs. Healing time varies with the graft type and site but often ranges from a few months to allow adequate bone maturation. With modern techniques and thorough planning, grafting expands treatment options for patients who initially lack sufficient bone.

What are All-on-4 and All-on-6 full-arch solutions and how do they differ?

All-on-4 and All-on-6 are full-arch treatment concepts that use a strategic number of implants to support a fixed prosthesis, providing a stable alternative to conventional dentures. The All-on-4 approach typically uses four implants, often angled in the posterior to maximize available bone, while All-on-6 uses six implants to increase support and distribution of chewing forces. Both methods aim to restore an entire dental arch with a single, anchored restoration that the patient does not remove daily.

Choice between these options depends on bone quantity, occlusal demands, aesthetic goals, and the individual treatment plan developed during consultation. Both approaches can be used to provide immediate or staged provisional restorations to restore function quickly. The restorative design, maintenance needs, and long-term monitoring differ slightly, so careful discussion with your clinician determines the best option for your needs.

How should I care for my dental implants after treatment?

Caring for dental implants is very similar to caring for natural teeth and centers on effective daily oral hygiene and regular professional examinations. Brushing twice daily with a soft-bristled brush and cleaning around the implant with floss or interdental brushes helps control plaque and protect surrounding gum tissue. Routine dental checkups and professional cleanings allow your clinician to monitor implant health and address early signs of complications.

Avoiding tobacco and managing medical conditions that affect healing are important for implant longevity because systemic health influences tissue response. Your dental team will provide personalized home-care instructions and may recommend specific tools or techniques for cleaning around restorations. With consistent hygiene and scheduled maintenance, implants can remain healthy and functional for many years.

What should I expect during recovery in terms of discomfort and activity?

Mild to moderate discomfort, swelling, and minor bleeding are common after implant surgery and typically peak within the first 48 to 72 hours before improving steadily. Pain can usually be managed with prescribed or over-the-counter medications and by following post-operative instructions such as using cold packs, resting, and avoiding strenuous activity. Eating soft foods and maintaining gentle oral hygiene around the surgical site supports comfort and healing during the initial recovery.

Most patients can return to normal light activities within a few days, but complete integration of the implant with bone takes longer and requires follow-up care. Your clinician will advise when it is safe to resume full dietary and physical activity based on clinical healing. Clear post-operative guidance and scheduled check-ins help ensure a smooth recovery process.

What risks and complications are associated with dental implants?

Dental implant surgery is predictable and safe for many patients, but like all medical procedures it carries some risks such as infection, delayed healing, nerve injury, and sinus complications in posterior upper-jaw cases. Early identification and management of soft tissue inflammation or infection are important to prevent progression to more serious conditions. Careful treatment planning, sterile surgical technique, and patient adherence to aftercare instructions reduce the likelihood of complications.
