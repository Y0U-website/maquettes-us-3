Dentures as a Replacement for Missing Teeth

Also referred to as false teeth, dentures have been the tried-and-true replacement for missing teeth for a long time. Although their quality has improved in recent years, there are still problems with this option for tooth replacement

One of the significant issues with dentures is that they can move or slip out of place. This unpredictable movement can make denture wearers self-conscious about speaking or laughing. It can also limit their options as far as what foods they can comfortably eat.

Dentures also do nothing to prevent facial collapse. When a smile is missing teeth, that jawbone doesn t receive any nutrients from the body. This lack of nutrients causes the jawbone to shrink as time goes on, giving people a sunken-in or aged look. It also means that dentures will have to be adjusted as the jawbone changes in size over the years.

When your Concord, NH dentists pair dentures with our quality dental implants, however, the result is a tooth replacement that fits, feels, and functions as close to natural teeth as possible. Your dentures will stay firmly in place, and since the dental implant stimulates healthy bone regeneration, they eliminate the sunken or aged common with tooth loss.

Proper Care for Your Dentures

Dentures do require some special care. You will need to remove and clean them daily to prevent bacteria from building up. Handle your dentures carefully because dropping them could lead to damage that affects the way they fit.

If you do happen to drop your dentures (or if they feel like they fit differently than usual), please call our Concord, NH dental office. We will want to examine your dentures immediately. Wearing damaged dentures can be uncomfortable and can even lead to painful sores, so let us know right away if you break your denture.

The Dental Implants Alternative for Missing Teeth

Dental implants in Concord, NH offer a restoration option that most closely mimics the look and feel of your natural teeth. Usually made of titanium, dental implants are placed directly into the jawbone, where the root of the tooth is missing.

What Is The Difference Between Denture Implants & Traditional Dentures?

Traditional dentures rest on your gum tissue and relies on suction to stay in place. They often take the coordination of cheeks and tongue to remain in proper position. Implant supported dentures are anchored to your jaw making them more reliable to chew harder food items and cause less discomfort as they are much more stable. Can you eat with a partial denture?

Yes, partial dentures are designed to be worn while eating. There may be certain food items that are more challenging with a traditional partial, which uses clasps around your natural teeth to hold it in. Certain foods can wear of break the partial denture of time. Many patients select implants to help support their dentures to allow for more stability and comfort in order to eat their favorite foods.

Can you sleep with dentures in?

If you wear traditional removable dentures, we typically recommend that you remove them at night to clean them and that you sleep without them.

Cleaning your dentures daily with a soft toothbrush and non-abrasive toothpaste keeps them looking their best. Then you can soak your dentures overnight in a cleaning solution or room temperature water, which keeps them from drying, cracking, and losing their shape.

Never use hot or boiling water to clean or soak your dentures, as it can cause them to warp. Also, avoid leaving your dentures where your pets can reach them, as dogs like the smell of saliva and are prone to chew on them.

It is also advisable to rinse your mouth at night with an antibacterial mouth rinse to cleanse your soft tissues and keep them healthy.

Yearly checkups with your dentist in Concord are also essential to ensure your denture is still fitting correctly and not causing any problems for your gums and soft tissues. In addition, removable dentures need to be realigned periodically to maintain a proper, comfortable fit.

What are some common denture-related issues?

Here are a few of the most common denture-related issues, and some practical techniques for addressing them:

  • Discomfort and sore spots . A new set of dentures can initially cause sore spots or discomfort in the mouth. If the dentures are properly fitted, this will abate with time and acclimation. Ill-fitting dentures can cause the same issues but without the improvement. These cases may call for adjustment or realignment by a dentist.
  • Difficulty speaking . Speaking with dentures requires time and adaptation. In particular, certain pronunciations and sounds may initially present a challenge. Practicing speaking with dentures helps a person to regain normal speech function.
  • Chewing challenges . Effectively chewing, especially hard or sticky foods, can be more difficult with dentures. To minimize chewing challenges, begin with softer foods when starting to wear dentures.
  • Slippage . Lower dentures, in particular, can slip or move while eating or speaking, resulting in discomfort and reduced confidence. Using denture adhesive helps to reduce or altogether prevent this slippage.
