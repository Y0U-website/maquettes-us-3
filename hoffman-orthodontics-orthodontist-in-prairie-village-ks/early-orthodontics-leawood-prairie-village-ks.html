What Is Early Orthodontic Treatment?

Early orthodontic treatment is the first stage of care used to address certain orthodontic concerns while a child is still growing. Also called interceptive treatment, it focuses on guiding development at a time when the teeth, jaws, and bite are still changing.

This type of treatment may help support healthier jaw growth, more favorable tooth eruption, and better bite development before concerns become more established. Two-Phase Treatment for Children

Two-phase treatment is an orthodontic approach used in select cases when care is most effective in two separate stages rather than one later round of treatment.

Phase One: Early Treatment to Improve Development

Phase one focuses on improving the conditions for healthy development while a child is still growing. This stage may help guide jaw growth, create space for incoming teeth, improve bite relationships, and reduce the severity of problems that could become more difficult to correct later.

The Resting Phase

After phase one, active treatment pauses and the focus shifts to observation while permanent teeth continue to erupt. During this time, Hoffman Orthodontics monitors growth and development closely so families in Leawood and Prairie Village, KS have a clearer sense of when the next step should begin.

Phase Two: Comprehensive Treatment with Permanent Teeth

Phase two begins after more permanent teeth have erupted and focuses on the detailed work of final alignment and bite correction. This stage builds on the progress made earlier and helps bring the teeth and bite into a more stable, functional long-term result.

Braces for Kids

Braces help correct alignment problems and bite issues by moving teeth into better positions with steady, controlled force. At Hoffman Orthodontics, braces may be recommended for some children during earlier treatment or later on, depending on when more direct tooth movement is needed for the best result.

Palatal Expander

A palatal expander is designed to widen the upper jaw in a gradual, controlled way while a child is still developing. This can help create space, improve the fit between the upper and lower jaws, and support a healthier foundation for the way the bite comes together.

FAQs About Early Orthodontic Treatment

Does every child need early orthodontic treatment?

No. Early orthodontic treatment is recommended only when a child shows developing concerns that may benefit from treatment before all permanent teeth come in.

What are signs that a child may need an orthodontic evaluation?

Common signs can include crowding, early or late tooth loss, bite problems, or jaws that do not seem to fit together properly. Some concerns are less obvious, which is why an orthodontic checkup can still be helpful even without clear symptoms.

What happens if treatment is not needed at age 7?

In many cases, no treatment begins right away. The orthodontist may simply monitor growth and tooth eruption until the timing is more appropriate.

How long does early orthodontic treatment usually last?

The length depends on the child’s needs and the type of problem being addressed. Some early treatments are relatively short, while others require monitoring before later care.

Can early orthodontic treatment help prevent more serious problems later?

In some cases, yes. Early treatment can reduce the severity of certain issues and create better conditions for future development, though it does not eliminate the need for later treatment in every case.
