The Implant-Supported Denture Process

Planning starts with a consultation, a review of your goals, and 3D imaging to evaluate bone. A personalized plan outlines the number and position of implants and whether a removable overdenture or a fixed bridge is best.

Implant placement is typically completed in one appointment using local anesthesia. Most patients describe mild to moderate soreness for a few days. A healing period of several weeks to a few months allows the bone to integrate with the implants. During this time, a temporary denture is used so you are not without teeth.

Once the implants are stable, connectors called abutments and the attachment system are placed. A removable overdenture may use locator attachments or a bar, while a fixed option is secured with screws. Your final denture or bridge is then fitted and adjusted for comfort, bite, and appearance.

Your Experience and What to Expect

After surgery, expect minor swelling and tenderness that improve within a few days. A soft diet is common at first, then you can gradually return to regular foods as advised. Good home care is essential. Clean around implants daily using a soft brush, floss aids or a water flosser, and follow recommended rinses. If your denture is removable, take it out at night unless advised otherwise and keep it clean.

Follow-up visits help confirm proper healing and bite alignment. With consistent care, implants can last many years, while the denture or bridge may eventually need maintenance such as replacing attachments or relining the base. People who smoke or have active gum disease may need additional support to reduce the risk of complications. Our dentist will review factors like bone grafting needs, healing time, and the differences between fixed versus removable implant dentures so you understand your treatment choices.
