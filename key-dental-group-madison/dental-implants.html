What Is a Dental Implant?

A dental implant is a small titanium post. The dentist places it into your jawbone, right where your missing tooth was. Over time, the implant fuses with your bone and becomes a strong, stable base — just like a natural tooth root. This is called osseointegration. After that, we place a connector piece called an abutment on top of the implant. Then, we put a dental crown on the abutment. The crown is the part you can see. It looks just like your real tooth.

Think of it this way: the implant is the root, the abutment is the connector, and the crown is the tooth. All three parts work together to give you a full, healthy-looking smile.

Who Is an Ideal Candidate?

You may be a good candidate for dental implants in Madison, MS, if:

Have a fully grown jawbone (usually adults 18 and older)

Have enough jawbone to support the implant, or can get a bone graft to build it up

Have healthy gums with no signs of periodontal disease (gum disease)

Do not smoke, or are willing to quit — smoking slows healing and increases the risk of implant failure

Have good overall health and no medical conditions that stop normal bone healing

Are committed to good oral hygiene and regular dental check-ups

You may need to be evaluated more carefully if you:

Are going through cancer treatment (radiation to the head or neck)

Take certain medications that affect bone health

Do not worry if you are unsure. Our team at Key Dental Group will do a full exam and review your dental and medical history. We will let you know if implants are right for you — and if not, we will suggest the best alternative.

The Dental Implant Process

Getting a dental implant takes a few months. You can expect the following steps during the process:

Step 1 — Consultation and Exam

Your journey starts with a visit to our office. We take X-rays and may use a 3D scan to look at your jawbone. We check your gum health and talk about your goals. This helps us make a personalized treatment plan just for you.

Step 2 — Tooth Extraction (If Needed)

If your damaged tooth is still in place, we remove it first. This helps create a clean, healthy site for implant placement.

Step 3 — Bone Grafting (If Needed)

Some patients do not have enough jawbone to hold an implant. In this case, we do a bone graft. We add bone material to the area and let it heal. This usually takes a few months before we can place the implant.

Step 4 — Implant Placement

Our dentist near you makes a small incision in your gum and places the titanium implant into your jawbone. The area is numbed, so you will not feel pain during the procedure. You may feel some pressure, but it is manageable.

Step 5 — Osseointegration (Healing Period)

Over the next 3 to 6 months, your bone slowly grows around the implant and holds it firmly in place. This is the most important step. It is what makes implants so strong and stable.

Step 6 — Abutment Placement

Once the implant has bonded with your bone, we attach a small connector called an abutment. This is a quick procedure done right in our office.

Step 7 — Crown Placement

Finally, we place your custom-made dental crown on the abutment. The crown is designed to look just like your natural tooth. Once it is in place, your smile is complete.

Advantages of Dental Implants

Dental implants come with many great benefits. Here is why so many patients choose our clinic for dental implants in Madison:

The crown is custom-made to match the color and shape of your other teeth. Most people cannot even tell the difference.

With good oral care, dental implants can last many years — even a lifetime for most patients. That makes them a smart long-term investment.

When you lose a tooth, your jawbone slowly starts to shrink in that area. This is called bone resorption. An implant stops this process by acting like a tooth root and keeping the bone strong.

Unlike dental bridges, dental implants do not require support from the adjacent teeth. Your healthy teeth stay untouched.

You need to brush and floss them just like your natural teeth. No special cleaners or adhesives needed.

Dentures can slip when you eat. Implants stay fixed in place so that you can eat your favorite foods comfortably.
