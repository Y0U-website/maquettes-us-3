Bone Grafting

Bone grafting is a surgical procedure in which new bone material is added to the jawbone to support dental implants or to help repair a damaged jawbone. This procedure is necessary when there is not enough healthy jawbone present to support dental implants or when the jawbone has been damaged due to injury, gum disease, or other factors.

Bone Regeneration

If th e patient has severe bone loss surrounding existing teeth, Dr. Howard may recommend a regenerative procedure called Periodontal Regeneration Grafting. These procedures can reverse some of the damage by regenerating lost bone and tissue.

During this procedure, Dr. Howard accesses the deeper disease and removes the disease‐causing bacteria in a procedure called osseous surgery. Barrier membranes, bone grafts or tissue‐stimulating proteins can then be used to encourage your body’s natural ability to regenerate bone and tissue. The bone grafting material is placed in the bony defects around the teeth. A membrane may be used to cover and protect the bone graft. When healed, new bone will grow and attach to the teeth.

Sinus Lift

A sinus augmentation graft procedure makes it possible for many patients to have dental implants when years ago there was no other option besides wearing loose dentures.

The maxillary sinuses are located on either side of the nose, directly above the upper molars. When a person loses upper molars, the sinuses can enlarge and take up some of the space where the jawbone used to be. A sinus lift procedure can add bone to the sinus floor, increasing the height of the jawbone and making it possible to place dental implants in this area.

Cosmetic Crown Lengthening

If you have a gummy smile, or you feel like your teeth look too short, you might be a candidate for crown lengthening.

Using cutting-edge techniques and advanced materials, Dr. Howard will reshape your gums at the base of the teeth to expose additional tooth surface and create greater symmetry at your gum line.

Osseous Surgery

Gum disease often causes deep pockets to form around your teeth. These pockets are the perfect hiding spot for bacteria and are difficult to clean on your own, this results in further bone and tissue loss.

Dr. Howard offers osseous surgery, also known as pocket depth reduction, to restore your health.

This procedure is usually recommended after other therapy options have proved unsuccessful or as a treatment for advanced stages of gum disease.

Gum Recession Therapy

Gum recession is caused by many different factors including genetics, aggressive tooth brushing, gum disease and stress. This can make your teeth quite sensitive and can increase your risk of tooth decay and even tooth loss.

Dr. Howard offers a combination of traditional treatment and state-of-the-art techniques to give you personalized care and superior results traditional gum grafts, tunneling and the cutting-edge Chao Pinhole technique.

Scaling and Root Planing (SRP)

Scaling and root planing (SRP) is one of the most common treatments for early to advanced periodontal disease. Commonly referred to as a deep cleaning, this non-surgical procedure involves reaching beyond the gum line to remove plaque buildup and promote healing.

By catching signs of gum disease early on and taking preventive action, Dr. Howard will help you maintain a clean and healthy mouth for life.

Gum Grafting

When recession of the gum tissue occurs, the body loses a natural defense against both bacterial penetration and trauma. When gum recession is a problem, gum reconstruction using grafting techniques is an excellent option.

Dr. Howard has trained in the latest minimally invasive techniques of gum grafting and can often perform the entire procedure through a pinhole incision.
