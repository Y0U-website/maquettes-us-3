The Importance of Orthodontic Treatment

You only get one set of permanent teeth to last you for the rest of your life. When your permanent teeth are not in the proper position, it can negatively affect your dental health and your overall health.

When your teeth don’t line up properly or if they are crowded together, at the very least it can make it difficult to keep your teeth clean and allow plaque to build up in the spaces between the teeth that are pushed together. Teeth that are turned the wrong way or pushed up against other teeth can cause damage to the teeth and roots. It can cause your teeth to wear down prematurely and lead to jaw problems like TMJ disorder.

Dental health is closely linked with overall health and wellness. If your teeth are not lined up properly you may have problems chewing certain foods that are important for balanced nutrition. A buildup of plaque on your teeth may also lead to gum disease, which has been unmistakably linked to diabetes and heart disease. Not to be forgotten, straight teeth can also positively impact our self-esteem and confidence.

Frequently Asked Questions About Metal Braces

How much do metal braces cost?

The cost of metal braces varies due to a number of factors, including the extent of the orthodontic corrections you need, the length of your treatment time, and your insurance. On average, the cost ranges from $3,000 to $7,000.

Does insurance cover metal braces?

If your dental insurance includes orthodontic coverage, it will likely include metal braces. Orthodontic coverage typically comes in a one time lump sum that can be put toward braces or other orthodontic treatment. Contact your insurance provider to determine what your plan covers.

Are metal braces painful?

Some discomfort is possible in the first few days in braces. As the teeth are shifted, it can cause the gums and jaw to feel tender, which usually subsides in 4-5 days. The tenderness may return for a day or two after each adjustment. The metal brackets and wires can sometimes cause irritation to the inside of the cheeks and lips, but orthodontic wax can be used to smooth out rough places until your mouth gets used to them.
