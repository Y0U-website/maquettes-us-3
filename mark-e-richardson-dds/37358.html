Frequently Asked Questions

How long do dental implants last?

Dental implants are the most reliable and durable tooth-replacement option available today. In most cases, implants can last decades, often 20 years or more, with many patients enjoying them for life! Their longevity comes from the way the titanium post integrates with your jawbone, via osseointegration , creating a stable, natural-feeling foundation for chewing and speaking.

Maximizing Dental Implant Lifespan

Daily brushing and flossing, avoiding tobacco, and protecting your teeth from grinding also support implant longevity. With proper care, the crown attached to the implant may eventually have to be replaced due to normal wear, but the implant itself can remain stable and functional for many years.

Ultimately, though, long-term implant success depends on good oral hygiene and consistent professional care. While implants don’t decay, the gums and bone surrounding them must remain healthy. That’s why regular dental visits are essential. Professional cleanings, x-rays, and evaluations help Dr. Mark Richardson and our skilled Kalamazoo dental team identify early signs of inflammation or bite issues that could compromise the implant over time.

What are the main components of a dental implant?

A dental implant is a long-lasting tooth replacement solution designed to look, feel, and function exactly like a natural tooth. It consists of three main components that work together to restore your smile.

The Anatomy of a Dental Implant

  • Implant Post The implant post is a small titanium screw that serves as the foundation for your new tooth. Your dentist places it into the jawbone where the missing tooth once was, and as it heals, the bone naturally bonds with the implant to create a secure, long-lasting base for your replacement tooth or teeth.
  • Abutment The abutment is a connector that attaches to the top of the implant post. The abutment securely holds the replacement tooth in place.
  • Tooth replacement The tooth replacement (artificial tooth or teeth) is the visible portion of the restoration. This can be a dental crown, bridge, or denture. We customize each tooth replacement to match the size, shape and color of your surrounding teeth. Our goal is to make your new smile look and feel seamless and natural.

The Dental Implant Restoration Process

The journey to revitalizing your smile with dental implants typically involves several key steps:

  • Consultation Dr. Mark will assess your oral health and discuss your goals and concerns. He will then develop a treatment plan tailored to your needs.
  • Implant Placement An oral surgeon or periodontist will surgically place the implant fixture into your jawbone. This process typically takes several months as the implant fuses with the bone.
  • Abutment Placement Once the implant has integrated with the bone, the oral surgeon will attach the abutment, which connects the implant and the prosthetic tooth.
  • Prosthetic Creation Dr. Mark meticulously designs and crafts the custom prosthetic tooth or teeth securely attached to the abutment.
  • Final Restoration Once the prosthetic is ready, Dr. Mark expertly affixes it to the abutment, ensuring a perfect fit, natural appearance, and optimal function.
  • Ongoing Maintenance Routine checkups and proper oral hygiene maintain the longevity and vitality of your dental implant.

How to Care for Your Dental Implants in Kalamazoo

While dental implants offer a long-lasting solution for tooth replacement, proper care is essential to maintain their longevity and ensure oral health.

Here’s how to care for your dental implants:

  • Maintain Good Oral Hygiene Brushing and flossing regularly are crucial for keeping dental implants clean and preventing gum disease.
  • Get Regular Dental Checkups Schedule routine dental checkups and professional cleanings with your dentist to monitor the health of your implants and surrounding gums.
  • Avoid Damaging Habits Avoid biting on hard objects (e.g., ice, pens). Using a nightguard can protect oral health if you have habits like grinding your teeth.
