Implant-Supported Dentures

This treatment option offers the best of both worlds—full tooth replacement that feels secure and functions almost like natural teeth.

How Implant-Supported Dentures Work Unlike traditional dentures that rest directly on your gums and rely on suction for stability, implant-supported dentures attach to dental implants surgically placed in your jawbone. Think of the implants as artificial tooth roots that provide permanent anchors for your denture.

We typically place four to six implants in your upper or lower jaw—sometimes both, depending on your needs. These titanium posts fuse with your jawbone over several months through a process called osseointegration. Once healing completes, your custom denture snaps onto the implants using special attachments that hold everything securely in place.

The implants distribute chewing forces directly into your jawbone, just like natural tooth roots do. This stimulation helps keep your bones healthy and prevents the deterioration that typically occurs after tooth loss. It’s one of the biggest advantages implant-supported dentures have over traditional options.

Types of Implant-Supported Dentures

These dentures snap onto the implants, but they can be removed for cleaning. The attachments—either ball-and-socket connectors or bars with clips—hold the denture firmly during normal use but release when you apply the right amount of pressure. Most patients find them easy to remove for daily cleaning and replace without any hassle.

Removable implant-supported dentures provide the stability you need while eating and talking, while still offering the convenience of removal for thorough cleaning and allowing your gums to rest at night.

Fixed implant-supported dentures are screwed directly onto the implants and stay in your mouth permanently. Only Dr. Sidhu or Dr. Kraja can remove them during your regular checkup appointments for professional cleaning. These feel the most like natural teeth because they never come out—you brush and care for them just like you would real teeth.

Fixed options typically require more implants for support, usually six to eight per arch, but they offer maximum stability and the closest experience to having your natural teeth back.

Benefits That Change Everything

Traditional dentures can shift when you eat certain foods or speak enthusiastically, causing embarrassing clicking sounds or uncomfortable movement. Implant-supported dentures eliminate this problem. The implants hold your denture steady no matter what you’re doing, so you can bite into an apple, enjoy a steak, or laugh without any worries.

Many people with traditional dentures avoid certain foods because they’re difficult to chew or might dislodge the denture. Implant-supported dentures restore your full chewing power. You can enjoy crunchy vegetables, chewy meats, and sticky foods that would be problematic with conventional dentures. Your diet expands dramatically, which means better nutrition and more enjoyment at mealtimes.

When you lose teeth, the jawbone underneath starts shrinking because it no longer receives stimulation from chewing. This bone loss changes your facial structure over time, creating that sunken appearance and making your face look older. Implant-supported dentures prevent this by stimulating the bone just like natural tooth roots do. Your facial structure stays intact, and you maintain a more youthful appearance.

Traditional dentures rely solely on your gums, sitting on top of them. This can cause sore spots and require periodic adjustments as your mouth changes shape. Implant-supported dentures don’t put pressure on your gums—the implants bear all the force. This means greater comfort and fewer adjustment appointments over time.

The psychological benefits can’t be overstated. Patients tell us they feel liberated after getting implant-supported dentures. They stop worrying about their teeth during important meetings, social events, or romantic moments. The security these dentures provide translates directly into greater confidence in every area of life.

The Treatment Process

Getting implant-supported dentures happens in stages to allow proper healing between procedures. During your initial consultation, we examine your mouth, take detailed 3D images using CBCT scanning technology, and evaluate your jawbone density. This planning phase ensures we place implants in optimal positions for maximum support and longevity.

If you have remaining teeth that need extraction, that happens first. We can sometimes place implants immediately after extractions, which reduces your overall treatment time. For patients who’ve been without teeth for a while, the implant placement is straightforward.

The surgical procedure involves making small incisions in your gums and placing the titanium implants precisely into your jawbone. You’ll wear a temporary denture during the healing period, which typically lasts three to six months. This gives the implants time to integrate fully with your bone.

Once osseointegration completes, you return for the attachment procedure. We connect special abutments to the implants that will hold your denture. We take final impressions and create your custom implant-supported denture designed specifically for your mouth and facial features.

When your permanent denture is ready, we attach it to the implants and make sure everything fits perfectly and functions comfortably. You’ll leave our Silver Spring office able to eat and smile with complete confidence.

Who Makes a Good Candidate

Most healthy adults qualify as candidates for implant-supported dentures. You need adequate jawbone density to support the implants, though bone grafting can build up deficient areas if necessary. Good overall health matters too, since healing after surgery depends on your body’s ability to recover effectively.

Conditions like uncontrolled diabetes or active gum disease need management before implant placement. Heavy smoking can interfere with healing and implant success, so we might recommend reducing or quitting before treatment. Age isn’t typically a barrier—we’ve successfully placed implants in patients from their 20s to their 90s.
