What Causes Airway Problems?

Our team looks for structural and developmental issues including:

We collaborate with ENT doctors and sleep physicians when medical care is needed to support breathing and wellness.

  • Mouth breathing that dries tissues and alters facial development
  • Sleep-disordered breathing including snoring
  • Narrow palates, crossbites, or crowding that limit tongue room
  • Low tongue posture that restricts airflow
  • High palate or enlarged tonsils/adenoids that block the airway

What We Check During an Airway Evaluation

A full airway orthodontic exam is comfortable and detailed. We look at:

You’ll leave with real insight into whether mouth breathing is structural and what can be done to help.

  • Breathing habits and sleep history
  • Facial growth, posture, and symmetry
  • Tongue position, tonsils, palate shape, and dental crowding
  • Nose vs. mouth breathing during rest
  • Imaging (CEPH or CBCT) to measure the airway space internally
  • ENT collaboration if tissues block airflow

How Orthodontic Treatment Helps Improve the Airway

Breathing should not be a struggle. Airway orthodontics helps make space where the body needs it most.

Using rapid palatal expansion , our RPE appliance orthodontics widens the upper jaw to increase nasal airflow and tongue space. This supports airway expansion and nasal breathing, which is especially effective while children are still growing.

Improving alignment and bite helps the tongue rest properly and improves airway support.

For teens and adults with significant jaw restriction, surgery can increase airway volume and breathing comfort.

If enlarged tonsils or adenoids reduce breathing space, we coordinate with ENT specialists to ensure complete care.
