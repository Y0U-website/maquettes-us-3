What Are Dental Implants?

A dental implant is made of three parts: an implant post, an abutment, and the final restoration. The implant post is placed directly into the jaw to mimic the roots of the missing teeth. This provides an incredibly reliable and long-lasting foundation for your new tooth or teeth, which are attached via the abutment. Dental implants are widely considered as the gold standard of tooth replacement, and it’s easy to see why. When you rebuild your smile from the roots up, your new pearly whites will look, feel, and function just like natural teeth.

4 Step Dental Implant Process

To provide their unique benefits, dental implants require a multistep process. Unlike other tooth replacement options like dentures or dental bridges, the dental implant process involves several stages over the course of several months. However, the time and effort are well-worth the stunning results! While every patients’ dental implant journey will be slightly different, here are the basic steps you can expect.

Initial Dental Implant Consultation

You’ll first need an initial consultation to determine your candidacy for dental implants. Besides a visual examination, you’ll also need a CT scan to assess your jawbone and oral structures. Based on the results of the examination, you may need additional procedures before undergoing your placement surgery, like bone grafting.

Bone grafting is necessary if your jawbone isn’t thick enough to support your dental implant. It adds a piece of bone to areas that are weakened. Although it creates another step in your treatment plan, it’s time well spent to ensure your new smile has the foundation it needs to thrive.

Dental Implant Surgery

After you’ve healed from any preliminary treatments like bone grafting pr periodontal therapy, we can schedule your dental implant surgery. While many other dental practices need to refer their patients to an outside specialist for this step, we’re proud to offer start-to-finish convenience! Dr. Michitti has completed thousands of hours of advanced training in order to personally complete every step of the dental implant process. Not only will this help save you time and money, but you’ll feel more relaxed during your treatment working with a familiar dental team.

Once we’ve ensured you’re comfortable, Dr. Michitti will make an incision in the gums to expose the jawbone. Then, he’ll insert the dental implant post directly into the bone before suturing the gums closed once again. A protective cap will be placed over the top of the implant post to help maintain the shape of the gums while your mouth heals.

Dental Implant Osseointegration/Abutment

After your surgery, your dental implant post will begin a process called osseointegration. This is where the titanium post fuses with the surrounding bone tissue, essentially becoming a part of your jaw. Typically, it will take anywhere from four to six months before your mouth has healed enough to be ready for the next step of getting your dental implants. While this may seem like quite some time, the end result is a steadfast foundation for your smile that’s well-worth the wait.

Once you’ve recovered enough, you may need to undergo a second minor surgery to have a small connector piece called an abutment attached to the top of the implant post. Then, once your gums have healed, you’ll be ready for the last step of the dental implant process.

Delivery of Dental Implant Restorations

While you recovery from your surgery, we’ll create your final restoration. This is the custom crown, bridge, or denture that will act as your new teeth. Implant surgery is one of the most taxing parts of this entire process, but with the right care and maintenance, you should be able to make a comfortable and fast recovery so you can be one step closer to having a complete and healthy smile again.

It’s important to make sure that the blood clot on your surgery site remains completely untouched so your gums can heal around the implant. To do this, be sure to avoid:

  • Instead, use tissues or swallow your saliva.
  • Drinking from straws.
  • Smoking

Common Side-Effects

If you’ve never had an oral surgery before, it can be difficult to tell what symptoms are normal and which ones you should be concerned about. As you’re healing, you should notice that any initial discomfort will gradually disappear. If it just seems to be getting worse, don’t hesitate to contact our office. Here are some normal side-effects that you can expect in the days following your procedure:

  • Gradual and intermittent bleeding, which can be remedied by placing a small amount of pressure on gauze that’s located on the surgical site.
  • Swelling that can last over a week. You can get it to go down by placing a cold compress on the cheek or jaw for 10 minutes at a time.
  • General discomfort, which can be alleviated by taking prescribed or recommended medications.

  • Mashed potatoes

Health & Oral Hygiene

It’s incredibly important to keep your mouth clean after your surgery to make sure that your dental implants in Agawam successfully integrate with your jawbone. Here are a few oral hygiene and health tips:

  • You can brush your teeth as you normally would the day after your surgery, just be gentle and careful while brushing near the surgery site.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

Once you’ve received your natural-looking and long-lasting dental implants, you’ll want to take the proper steps to preserve your results. While these titanium posts are designed to provide stability and structure for decades to come, they still require maintenance to make the most of them. From everyday teeth cleaning to certain kinds of lifestyle changes, here’s what you’ll need to consider when it comes to dental implant care so you can enjoy a brand-new, complete bite.

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

Just like with your natural pearly whites, you’ll need to clean your dental implants every day with normal brushing, flossing, and rinsing with mouthwash. Though the restorations themselves won’t get cavities or decay, the oral tissue surrounding them can still be vulnerable to bacteria. That’s why you’ll want to implement good oral hygiene so that your gums don’t become at risk of infection that could ruin the success of your results.

Eat a Healthy Diet

Another way to avoid periodontal disease and other oral health complications is to consume healthier food options. Fortunately, since dental implants help you regain 80 % or more of your natural chewing ability, you’ll be free to expand your diet, allowing you to enjoy meals that are tougher in texture. Stick with nutritious foods like fruits, veggies, lean meats, and low-fat dairy to promote healthier gums and bone tissue, this way your metal posts can benefit from a more stable foundation in the long run.

Break Bad Habits

Certain oral habits may seem harmless at first, such as chewing on ice or holding your keys with your teeth, but one wrong bite can lead to potential damage to your teeth, including your dental implants. Smoking tobacco products can also be detrimental to your treatment, as it can delay healing and increase the risk of failure later on. To avoid costly complications, it’s best to break bad oral habits so that you can enjoy a full and healthy bite again. Our team will be glad to provide you with the support and resources necessary to help you with these changes.

Protect Your Dental Implants

If you tend to participate in contact sports, or you simply clench or grind your teeth during sleep, then you’ll want to speak with our team about custom mouthguards or nightguards for bruxism. In either case, these oral appliances can help protect your dental implants and natural teeth from minor or severe damage, and they can also minimize wear and tear over time.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

Other than cleaning your teeth every day with regular oral hygiene techniques, you’ll want to make sure to visit your dentist every six months for checkups and cleanings. On top of polishing your pearly whites, they’ll also monitor your oral health and dental implants to ensure there aren’t any underlying issues that could risk failure later on. If they detect anything, they can address them early with the proper treatment.
