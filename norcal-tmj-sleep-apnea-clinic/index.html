Understanding Pain Begins With Understanding Function.

A thoughtful approach to headaches, TMJ disorders, and sleep — built on one idea: compression creates compensation.

The practice

Compression Creates Compensation The head is made of many plates of bone joined together by sutures. Together, they protect the brain while helping absorb and distribute the forces of everyday life.

The jaw joint (TMJ) and airway are also designed to function with adequate space and balance.

When any part of this system becomes compressed, the body adapts to keep you functioning.

The place where you feel pain is not always where the story began.

Our method focuses on three primary areas where compression tends to begin — and evaluates them together.

Restrictions within the tissues and sutures of the skull may create strain throughout the head and neck.

When the jaw joint loses space, the disc, muscles, and surrounding tissues must compensate to keep the jaw functioning.

When the airway becomes narrowed, the body may compensate by moving the head forward, clenching the jaw, or recruiting additional muscles to help maintain airflow—especially during sleep.
