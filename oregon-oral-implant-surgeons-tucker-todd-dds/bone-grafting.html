Bone Grafting in Eugene & Roseburg OR

Why Bone Grafting is Necessary

Major Bone Grafting Procedures

Major bone grafting is often needed when implant sites have inadequate bone structure due to trauma, tooth extraction, gum disease, or congenital conditions. Bone used for these procedures can come from a tissue bank or be harvested from your own body—commonly the hip (iliac crest), tibia (below the knee), or jawbone.

  • Hip (iliac crest)
  • Tibia (lateral knee)
  • Skull (cranium)

Sinus Lift Procedure

The sinus lift , or sinus graft, is a common and effective procedure for increasing available bone in the upper jaw. This is especially important when the maxillary sinus lies too close to the jawbone as a result of tooth loss.

  • The surgeon enters the sinus from where the upper teeth used to be
  • The sinus membrane is gently lifted
  • Donor bone is inserted into the floor of the sinus (roof of the upper jaw)

Ridge Expansion

When tooth loss leads to an overly narrow or short jaw ridge, simple implant placement may be impossible. Our ridge expansion technique enables us to widen or elevate the ridge to make way for conventional implants.

  • Mechanically expanding the existing jawbone ridge
  • Placing bone graft material
  • Allowing healing and maturation of the graft area before implant placement

Nerve Repositioning

Sometimes, implant placement in the lower jaw requires moving the inferior alveolar nerve , which supplies feeling to the lower lip and chin. This complex procedure is typically reserved for cases where space is limited in areas of the back molars and second premolars.

  • Our surgeon accesses the nerve canal by removing a portion of the jawbone
  • The nerve bundle is gently retracted to the side
  • Implants are placed
  • The nerve bundle is repositioned over the implants and secured

Bone Graft Materials and Techniques

Depending on your condition, we may use your own bone or safe alternatives such as:

  • Allografts – Human donor bone from tissue banks, effective and safe
  • Synthetic materials – Used to stimulate bone growth
  • Platelet-derived growth factors – Obtained from your own blood to promote faster healing
