Treatments All Ages Kids The American Association of Orthodontists (AAO) recommends that children visit an orthodontist around age seven. By this age, most kids have a mix of baby and adult teeth, making it easier to identify and address tooth and jaw issues early, sometimes avoiding more extensive treatment later.

Early orthodontic care can guide jaw growth, create space for crowded teeth, correct habits like thumb sucking, and improve minor speech issues. Early intervention can set your child on the path to a healthy, confident smile.

Palatal Expanders (Hyrax) A palatal expander expands (or widens) your upper jaw by putting gentle pressure outward on your upper molars. This can correct crossbites and/or create more space to resolve crowding in children. Typically, an expander is turned daily for 2-4 weeks and then maintained in place for 6-9 months. However, this can vary, so please always follow the advice of Dr. Meinhardt and Dr. Jacobson.

Orthognathic Surgery

For some patients, orthodontic treatment alone isn’t enough to achieve proper alignment or balance between the upper and lower jaws. In these cases, Dr. Meinhardt and Dr. Jacobson may recommend orthognathic (jaw) surgery in coordination with an experienced oral and maxillofacial surgeon. This combined approach can correct significant bite discrepancies, improve facial balance, and enhance function for long-term oral health.

Orthognathic surgery is carefully planned using digital imaging and models to ensure precise results. While the idea of surgery may sound intimidating, our team will guide you through every step, from preparation to recovery, to make the process as smooth and comfortable as possible. The outcome is not only a healthier bite but also a smile that feels as good as it looks.

Debonding & Retainers The final stage of your orthodontic journey! Your braces have been removed, and your smile is beautiful, straight, and best of all, metal-free! However, your orthodontic journey isn’t quite completed. To keep your smile looking its best, you’ll have to wear a retainer to preserve and stabilize your results. Retainers are needed to control or limit potential changes in tooth position. They are used after braces treatment to hold teeth in their correct alignment while the surrounding gums, bone, and muscle adjust to the new positioning of your teeth.
