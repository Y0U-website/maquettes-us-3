The Scaling and Root Planing Process

The visit begins with a thorough evaluation by our dentist, including periodontal probing and X-rays as indicated. Local anesthesia is used to keep you comfortable. Ultrasonic and hand instruments remove hardened deposits from the root surfaces (scaling). Then the roots are smoothed (root planing) to remove contaminated cementum and create a surface that discourages future buildup. In some cases, an antimicrobial rinse or localized antibiotic may be placed in deeper pockets to aid healing.

Treatment is often scheduled by quadrant or by half of the mouth to allow careful, detailed cleaning. A home care plan is reviewed so you know how to brush and clean between teeth while the gums heal.

Your Visit: What to Expect Before and After

Comfort And Safety

Local anesthesia prevents pain during the procedure. You may feel vibration and gentle pressure while the instruments work. Most patients report mild soreness for a day or two afterward, which can usually be managed with over-the-counter pain relief and saltwater rinses.

Healing And Maintenance

Slight gum tenderness and temporary sensitivity to cold are common after deep cleaning. Soft foods, a gentle brushing technique, and daily interdental cleaning help the tissues heal. A re-evaluation is typically scheduled 4 to 6 weeks later to check pocket depths and bleeding. Many patients then begin periodontal maintenance visits every 3 to 4 months to sustain results.

Understanding Your Gum Care Options

Preparing For Success At Home

  • Use a soft-bristled brush with small, circular motions along the gumline.
  • Clean between teeth daily with floss or interdental brushes sized to your spacing.
  • Consider an antimicrobial mouthrinse if recommended.
  • Limit tobacco use, which slows gum healing and increases disease risk.
  • Choose a balanced diet that supports immune health and tissue repair.
