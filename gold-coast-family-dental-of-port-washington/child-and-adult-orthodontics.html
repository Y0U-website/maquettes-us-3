More on Orthodontic Treatment

  • Crowded teeth: Teeth that are overlapped or the ones that are too close.
  • Gaps between teeth: Spaces between teeth.
  • Overbite: Upper teeth protruding significantly over the lower teeth.
  • Underbite: Lower teeth protruding over the upper teeth.
  • Crossbite: Upper teeth that bite inside the lower teeth.
  • Open bite: A gap that can be seen between the upper and lower front teeth when the back teeth are closed.
  • Temporomandibular joint (TMJ) disorders: Issues related to the joint of the jaw.

The Process Behind Orthodontic Treatment

Orthodontic treatment typically involves devices that apply gradual and consistent pressure to the teeth and jaws to correct misalignment. This steady pressure allows the teeth to slowly shift into their desired position over time.

Common devices used in orthodontics include:

  • Braces: Traditional braces are a staple in orthodontics, including metal braces, lingual braces (placed on the inside of the teeth), and ceramic braces. These braces consist of brackets attached to the teeth, with wires connecting them. Dentists adjust the wires periodically to guide tooth movement.
  • Clear Aligners (e.g., Invisalign): An innovative alternative to traditional braces, clear aligners are removable, plastic, and virtually invisible. These aligners are worn in a series, with each set being replaced periodically to continue shifting the teeth gradually.
  • Retainers: After completing treatment with braces or aligners, retainers are typically recommended to help maintain the newly aligned teeth.
