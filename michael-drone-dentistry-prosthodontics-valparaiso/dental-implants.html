What are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are the new standard of care for tooth replacement. A dental implant is a small titanium or ceramic (zirconia) post that bonds with healthy bone and acts as an artificial tooth root. It is firmly anchored into the jawbone and provides a secure foundation to attach the natural-looking porcelain crown, bridge, or denture. Dental implants look, feel, and function like real teeth and will restore your confidence to speak, eat, and smile. A dental implant can be used to replace a single missing tooth, multiple missing teeth, or all missing teeth.

The Dental Implant Procedure

Tooth loss results in deterioration of the jawbone, and many times patients will need an additional procedure called a bone graft to provide better support for the dental implant. This procedure is very common, safe, and is needed for a multitude of reasons. If your bone is too narrow or fragile, our team of oral surgeons may recommend a bone graft prior to implant placement. Bone grafting stimulates your healthy bone to regenerate. The healing process takes about 3 to 4 months to ensure a stable foundation. It is a routine and kind procedure when planning for dental implants.

After the treatment plan is finalized, you will have the oral surgeon or periodontist place the implant into the jawbone. The surgeon will make a small incision in your gums and insert the surgical guide onto the existing teeth. The guide is used for the precise placement of the implant into the jawbone. Your implant will be ready to restore after approximately 3-4 months of healing.

Proper oral hygiene care is essential in maintaining the health of your implant restoration. Inflammation can occur without proper oral hygiene, routine annual examinations, and professional cleanings, so we can keep your mouth healthy. Every patient will be provided with implant care instructions, and our team will demonstrate all the necessary hygiene techniques for you to maintain your new implant restorations.

Dental Implants Consist of 3 Parts

  • The Titanium Post: this is the prosthetic device that acts as an artificial tooth root and is placed into the jawbone to provide a secure foundation for the next two pieces.
  • The Abutment: This is the “middle” piece that sits on top of the titanium post and underneath the actual crown. It can be called a coupler as well and can be made of titanium, gold, or zirconia.
  • The Replacement Tooth: This is the piece that will be securely connected to the abutment. The replacement tooth can be a crown, bridge, or denture.

The Benefits of Dental Implants

There are many benefits to having dental implants, and they are specific to each patient, but three main benefits for all patients are improvement in eating, speaking, and smiling with confidence. Some of the more specific benefits of dental implants are as follows:

  • Dental implants prevent jawbone deterioration. When a tooth is lost, the jawbone begins to shrink from lack of stimulation and becomes brittle. A dental implant mimics the normal function of a tooth. It causes stimulation to the jawbone, and the bone bonds to the implant. This feature prevents bone loss and premature aging.
  • Dental implants look, feel, and function like natural teeth and have the highest success rate of any dental restoration.
  • Dental implants are resistant to bacteria that cause decay and periodontal disease.
  • Dental implants can eliminate the sticky denture adhesives for those patients with dentures and removable partial dentures.
  • Dental implants can restore proper chewing ability up to 100 %, allowing a better diet, improved health, and quality of life.
  • Dental implants can be a lifetime solution.

Frequently Asked Questions

Q. What are dental implants? A. Dental implants are the standard of care for replacing a missing tooth, multiple missing teeth, or a complete set of teeth

Q. What are the main consequences of tooth loss? A. Your jawbone will begin to deteriorate from the lack of bone stimulation. Losing a tooth can affect your smile and alter the shape of your face, causing you to look prematurely aged.

Q. Who is a candidate for dental implants? A. Dental implants can be placed in most adults.

Q. What if there is not enough bone or gum tissue for the dental implant? A. A bone or gum tissue graft may be needed to provide support for your dental implant.

Q. Is having an implant placed into your jawbone painful? A. No, dental implants are routinely placed using local anesthesia and are a very safe procedure.

Q. How long does it take to place a dental implant? A. Dental implants can be placed in about 30-60 minutes and vary depending on the number of implants.
