Ideal Candidates

Most people who are good candidates for implant surgery include :

  • Individuals with good overall health
  • People with food oral health (i.e. no other oral issues or gum disease)
  • Patients who regularly practice good oral hygiene
  • Those with sufficient jawbone density to support the implant
  • Non-smokers
  • Individuals committed to maintaining oral health and regular dental check-ups after implantation

Pros of Dental Implants

There are many advantages to this procedure, including:

  • Permanent Solution: Dental implants offer a long-term (often lifelong) solution to tooth loss
  • Natural Look and Feel: Implants look, feel, and function remarkably like real teeth
  • Preservation of Jawbone: The implant stimulates the bones of the jaw, which helps prevent bone loss over time
  • No Dietary Restrictions: Patients can enjoy their favorite foods without fear of dislodging the prosthetic once fully healed

Cons of Dental Implants

A few drawbacks to teeth implants include:

  • Cost: Dental implants tend to have a higher upfront cost compared to other solutions, like dentures. Most patients can expect to pay between $3,000 and $4,500 per tooth .
  • Time-Consuming: The entire process (including osseointegration) requires several months to complete
  • Surgical Procedure: Some individuals may be hesitant about the surgical aspect of implant placement

Traditional Dentures: Time-Tested Convenience

Traditional dentures are a great way for patients to replace missing teeth rapidly. These removable, custom-made appliances require careful planning to ensure optimal fit and functionality. While dentures are more cost-effective than dental implants, these devices may require frequent repairs or replacement, making them an ongoing financial concern for some patients.

What are Traditional Dentures?

Traditional dentures look like a full set of real teeth . Rather than staying permanently in the mouth like dental implants, they are removable. This means that they typically require the use of adhesives combined with natural suction to maintain their position in the mouth.

Procedure Details

The process of obtaining dentures is less invasive than dental implants . It usually involves:

  • Taking impressions of the patient’s mouth
  • Using the impressions to create a custom-fitted set of dentures
  • Making adjustments to ensure a comfortable fit

Duration of the Procedure

The timeline for traditional dentures is much shorter than that of dental implants. Once a dentist gets impressions of the patient’s mouth, the fabrication and fitting process typically takes a few weeks .

Ideal Candidates

Suitable candidates for dentures may include:

  • Individuals looking for a non-invasive, reversible solution
  • Those with compromised jawbone density or health concerns that make surgery less feasible
  • Individuals seeking a more budget-friendly option

Pros of Traditional Dentures

The perks of conventional dentures may include:

  • Affordability: Dentures are generally more economical than dental implants
  • Non-Invasive: The process of obtaining dentures involves minimal discomfort and no surgery
  • Quick Results : The entire process, from impressions to fitting, is relatively quick compared to dental implants

Cons of Traditional Dentures

  • Stability Issues: Dentures may shift or slip, affecting speech and nutrition
  • Bone Resorption: Unlike implants, dentures do not stimulate the jawbone, leading to potential bone loss
  • Repairs and Replacements: Dentures may need adjustment or replacement over time due to changes in the mouth’s structure
