What to Expect During the Process

Personalized consultation and planning. Your visit begins with a thorough exam and discussions about goals, health history, and lifestyle. Candidacy is based on gum health, bone support, and your expectations for comfort and appearance. This is also the right time to ask questions about removable versus fixed options.

Implant placement with comfort in mind. Implants are placed in the jaw under local anesthesia, and most patients describe the experience as more comfortable than expected. Careful technique supports accurate positioning and helps promote smooth healing.

Healing and temporary solutions. As the area heals, the bone bonds to the implants in a process known as osseointegration. Many patients can wear a modified existing denture or a provisional restoration during this phase so daily life stays on track.

Designing your new smile. The denture is crafted to complement your facial features, lip support, and bite. Attention to tooth color and shape helps achieve a natural look that fits your personality.

Attachment and fine-tuning. Once healing is complete, your denture is attached to the implants. Small adjustments enhance comfort and function, and you receive guidance on daily care to protect your investment.

Good candidates for implant-supported dentures have healthy gums and adequate bone support, do not smoke or are willing to reduce tobacco use, and wish to improve chewing, speech, and confidence. Frequently Asked Questions About Implant-Supported Dentures

Are implant-supported dentures removable or fixed?

Both options exist. Removable overdentures “snap” onto implants for easier cleaning, while fixed dentures are secured in place for a more permanent feel. The right choice depends on your goals, home care preferences, and oral health.

How long does the process take? Timelines vary by case. After initial planning and implant placement, healing typically takes several months before the final denture is attached. Many patients wear a temporary solution during healing to maintain comfort and appearance.

What is the difference between implant-supported dentures and traditional dentures? Traditional dentures rest on the gums and may move, especially in the lower arch. Implant-supported dentures attach to implants in the jaw for added stability, improved chewing, and better support of facial contours.

How do I care for implant-supported dentures? Daily cleaning and regular checkups are essential. Removable overdentures should be cleaned each day, and fixed options require brushing around the implants and under the denture as directed. Professional visits help maintain comfort and long-term success.

Am I a candidate if I have bone loss? Possibly. An evaluation can determine whether your current bone support is adequate for implants or if alternative approaches are more suitable. Personalized planning helps match the solution to your needs.
