Coordinated Orthodontic and Surgical Care

Treating an impacted canine is a team effort. We work directly with your orthodontist to coordinate every phase of treatment, from creating space in the arch to exposing the tooth and guiding it into position. Clear communication between providers means a smoother experience for you.

Precise 3D Imaging

We use advanced 3D cone beam imaging to locate the exact position of your impacted tooth before treatment begins. This allows us to plan the procedure with accuracy and reduces guesswork during surgery.

The Benefits of Treating an Impacted Canine

Preserve Your Tooth Exposure and bracketing allows us to save the impacted tooth and bring it into its natural position, which is almost always the better long-term outcome for your bite and bone health.

Protect Your Smile An untreated impacted canine can press against the roots of adjacent teeth, causing damage that may not be visible until it becomes serious. Early intervention prevents that from happening.

Complete, Functional Smile Once your canine is in position, your bite functions the way it should. Canines are your hardest-working teeth, and having them properly placed makes a meaningful difference in how you chew, speak, and smile.

The Impacted Canine Treatment Process

Evaluation and Treatment Planning

Evaluation and Treatment Planning

We start with a thorough exam and, in most cases, 3D imaging to determine exactly where your impacted canine is positioned. We&#x27;ll review your orthodontic history and coordinate with your orthodontist to develop a treatment plan. Most cases require braces or an arch wire to be placed first, to open up space in the dental arch before surgery.

Exposure and Bracketing

Exposure and Bracketing

Once space has been created in the arch, we perform a minor in-office surgical procedure to expose the impacted tooth. We lift the gum tissue to uncover the tooth, remove any obstructions, and bond a small orthodontic bracket with a miniature chain or a spring to the tooth. The chain or spring is connected to your arch wire, and the gum is gently stitched back into place. The procedure typically takes just over an hour and is performed under general anesthesia, with sedation available if needed.

Orthodontic Guidance and Follow-Up

Orthodontic Guidance and Follow-Up

After surgery, your orthodontist takes over, using the chain and bracket to apply gentle pressure that gradually guides the canine into its proper position. This process typically takes several months to a year. We&#x27;ll follow up to monitor healing and coordinate with your orthodontic team throughout. Once the tooth is fully in place, the bracket and chain are removed and treatment is complete.

FAQ&#x27;s

Frequently Asked Questions

What does it mean when a canine tooth is impacted?

An impacted tooth is one that is stuck in the jaw and unable to erupt into its normal position in the mouth. Upper canines are the second most frequently impacted teeth after wisdom teeth. The tooth may be blocked by a baby tooth that hasn&#x27;t fallen out, crowding in the arch, or simply an unusual angle of development.

How do I know if I or my child has an impacted canine?

Impacted canines are often identified during a routine dental exam or on X-rays. If a canine hasn&#x27;t appeared by around age 13, or if there&#x27;s an obvious gap where it should be, that&#x27;s typically a signal to investigate further. Early screening around age 7 allows us to spot potential issues before they become harder to treat.

Is the surgery painful?

Most patients experience minimal discomfort during and after the procedure. We use both sedation and local anesthesia to help keep you comfortable throughout your visit. After surgery, any soreness is typically mild and manageable with over-the-counter or prescribed pain medication for the first few days.

How long does the full treatment take?

The surgical procedure itself usually takes just over an hour. The orthodontic phase that follows, where the tooth is gradually guided into position, typically takes several months to a year depending on where the tooth is positioned and how the treatment progresses.

Can an impacted canine just be left alone?

In most cases, leaving an impacted canine untreated is not recommended. Over time, the impacted tooth can put pressure on the roots of neighboring teeth, contribute to crowding, and create bite problems that become more difficult to correct. Early treatment tends to produce better outcomes with less complexity.
