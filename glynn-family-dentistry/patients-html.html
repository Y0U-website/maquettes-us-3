A Note About Dental Insurance

Dental insurance is not like medical insurance. Most plans were designed in the 1960s and 70s, and annual maximums haven’t meaningfully changed since — typically $1,000 to $2,000 per year. That covered a lot more fifty years ago than it does today.

Dental insurance is best understood as a partial benefit. It helps reduce the cost of some services, but it was never designed to cover the full scope of what you may need. Most plans cover preventive care well, basic procedures partially, and major work at reduced rates — often with waiting periods, frequency limits, and exclusions.

We believe in doing what’s right for every patient. That means our recommendations are based on your health — not on what a plan happens to allow. Limiting your care to only what insurance covers can mean deferring treatment that’s needed now, which often leads to bigger, more costly problems down the road.

We’ll always explain your benefits, give you clear cost estimates, and involve you in every decision. Our job is to show you what’s right, help you understand why, and work with you to meet your individual needs.
