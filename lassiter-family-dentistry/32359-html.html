How long do dental implants last?

Dental implants are designed to be a long-term tooth replacement solution. In fact, with proper care, they can last a lifetime!

Here are a few reasons for dental implants’ impressive durability and longevity and why they are widely considered the preferred tooth replacement solution:

While the average and typical lifespan of dental implants is quite impressive, it’s important to note that individual experiences can vary. It’s thus important to consult with a dental professional who is familiar with your oral and overall health before moving forward with a dental implant procedure.

  • Implants are typically made of titanium, a biocompatible material that fuses with the jawbone through a process called osseointegration , ensuring stability and longevity.
  • Jawbone stimulation By replicating natural tooth roots, implants stimulate the jawbone, preventing the loss of bone density that can follow tooth loss and help to maintain facial structure.
  • Dental implants require the same care as natural teeth: regular brushing, flossing, and dental check-ups. With strong oral hygiene practices, implants can last a lifetime.
  • Dental implant procedures have a very high success rate and don’t often require follow-up procedures.

Is dental implant surgery painful?

In some cases, dental implant surgery can involve some moderate discomfort. Generally speaking, however, the placement of dental implants is not a painful procedure. Most patients receiving dental implants experience little to no discomfort is due to three key reasons:

Simply put, thanks to anesthesia, proper postoperative pain management, and advancements that have reduced the procedure’s invasiveness, any discomfort felt in the wake of dental implant surgery is generally both mild and far outweighed by the benefits of strong, stable biting and chewing functions, a healthy and robust jawbone, and a natural-looking smile.

  • Anesthesia The use of local anesthesia typically ensures a pain-free experience for the patient.
  • The procedure is minimally invasive The placement of a dental implant in the jawbone only requires a small incision in the gum, which is generally neither time-consuming nor painful.
  • Post-procedure care After dental implant surgery, discomfort or swelling is usually manageable with ice packs, salt water rinses, and over-the-counter pain relievers.
