The Dental Implant Process

Dental implant treatment is completed in stages. Your exact timeline depends on your oral health, bone support, number of missing teeth, and whether additional procedures are needed.

1. Consultation and Diagnostic Planning

Your first visit includes a detailed evaluation of your teeth, gums, bite, jawbone, and overall dental health. Digital imaging may be used to assess bone volume and determine whether implants can be placed safely and predictably.

During this visit, our team will discuss your treatment options, explain the expected timeline, and review whether additional care such as bone grafting may be needed.

2. Bone Grafting, If Needed

Some patients do not have enough bone to support an implant immediately. This can happen when a tooth has been missing for a long time, after infection, or following trauma. In these cases, bone grafting may be recommended to help create a stronger foundation for implant placement.

Patients who need additional support before implant placement can learn more about bone grafting in Abington, PA .

3. Dental Implant Placement

The implant is surgically placed into the jawbone. Over time, the bone heals around the implant through a process called osseointegration. This healing process helps create the stable foundation needed for the final restoration.

4. Healing and Temporary Restoration

Healing time varies by patient. Some cases may involve a temporary restoration while the implant heals. Your dentist will provide instructions for eating, cleaning the area, and protecting the implant during this stage.

5. Final Implant Restoration

After healing is complete, the implant is restored with a custom crown, bridge, denture, or full-arch prosthesis. The final restoration is designed to fit your bite, support chewing function, and complement your smile.

Patients who already have implants placed and need the visible tooth replacement portion can learn more about dental implant restoration .

Are Dental Implants Right for You?

Dental implants may be a good option if you are missing one or more teeth and want a stable, long-term replacement. A consultation is needed to determine whether implants are appropriate for your health and anatomy.

You may be a candidate for dental implants if you:

Dental implants require healthy surrounding tissues and careful maintenance. Your dentist will explain what is realistic based on your exam, imaging, and treatment goals.

  • Are missing one tooth, several teeth, or a full arch of teeth
  • Have healthy gums or are able to complete periodontal care first
  • Have enough jawbone or are a candidate for bone grafting
  • Want a fixed or more stable alternative to removable dentures
  • Do not smoke, or are willing to discuss how smoking may affect healing
  • Are committed to good home care and routine dental visits

Who May Not Be a Good Candidate for Dental Implants?

Dental implants may not be recommended right away for every patient. In some cases, other treatment may be needed first.

You may not be an immediate candidate if you:

If implants are not the best starting point, our team can review alternatives and explain how to restore function in a way that fits your oral health needs.

  • Have untreated gum disease
  • Have active dental infection
  • Have inadequate bone without grafting
  • Have uncontrolled medical conditions that may affect healing
  • Smoke heavily or use nicotine products that increase implant risks
  • Are unable to maintain consistent oral hygiene
  • Need a faster or more affordable short-term tooth replacement option

Dental Implants vs. Dentures and Bridges

Dental implants, dentures, and bridges can all replace missing teeth, but they work differently.

Dental implants replace the missing root and support a restoration anchored in the jaw. This can provide strong chewing support and help preserve bone. Traditional bridges replace a missing tooth by using neighboring teeth for support. Dentures replace multiple teeth or full arches with a removable appliance.

Each option has benefits and limitations:

The right option depends on your goals, budget, oral health, bone support, and timeline.

  • Dental implants may offer strong stability and long-term support, but they require surgery, healing time, and adequate bone.
  • Dental bridges can replace a missing tooth without implant surgery, but they may require reshaping adjacent teeth.
  • Dentures can replace many teeth at once and may be more affordable upfront, but they may move during eating or speaking.
  • All-on-4 may help replace a full arch with implant support, but candidacy depends on bone, bite, and overall health.
