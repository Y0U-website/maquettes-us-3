What Are Dental Implants?

Dental implants are titanium or zirconia posts that are surgically planted below the gumline and into the jawbone. Because titanium and zirconia are biocompatible, the implants can fuse with the bone through a process called osseointegration, allowing them to function like your natural tooth roots. After this process, Dr. Mackie can restore any number of missing teeth by securing custom-crafted crowns, bridges, or dentures to the implant posts to complete your new smile.

The 4-Step Dental Implant Process

  • Initial Consultation: The first step of every dental implant treatment is a consultation with Dr. Mackie. During this appointment, our team will examine your mouth and help you determine whether dental implants are right for you. Before you commit to moving forward with the treatment, we’ll answer any questions you may have and provide you with a timeline and detailed cost estimate.
  • Dental Implant Surgery: Dr. Mackie partners with leading specialists in Corinth for this part of the process, ensuring that your implants are placed precisely to maximize their lifespan.
  • Osseointegration: For the following three to six months after your surgery, your implants will fuse with your jawbone through a process called osseointegration. This allows them to act as your tooth roots, supplying ample blood flow and stimulation to the bone to keep it from eroding.
  • Placing the Final Prosthetics: After you’ve healed from your surgery, our team will place small metal connector pieces on the tops of your implants. These will anchor your dental crown, bridge, or denture securely in place to resemble natural teeth.

Preliminary Treatments & Dental Implant Surgery

Preliminary treatments and dental implant surgery are separate procedures, and each will be associated with their own costs. Not everyone needs preliminary treatments, though. If you have enough jawbone density to properly support dental implants, and don’t have gum disease, cavities, infection, or other oral health problems, then they likely won’t be included in your restoration plan.

The Parts of Your Dental Implant

A dental implant restoration involves two main parts: biocompatible implant posts and replacement teeth. The number of implants necessary and the type of replacement you choose will influence how your expenses break down. A singular implant crown, for example, is less expensive than an implant bridge, which is less expensive than an implant denture.

Maintaining & Caring for Your Dental Implants

If you take excellent care of them, your new dental implants may be able to keep your smile gorgeous, strong, and healthy for several decades or even a lifetime. To help you get the most out of your investment by preventing infections and unnecessary wear, here’s a brief list of a few excellent habits that will help you keep your dental implants in great shape. If you’d like to learn more, please contact the friendly team at Loretto Family Dentistry .

Make Oral Hygiene a Priority

While dental implants cannot get cavities, the soft tissues and jawbone structures supporting them can still be compromised by destructive infections. To prevent dental implant failure, practice excellent oral hygiene by brushing at least twice and flossing and rinsing with antibacterial mouthwash every day.

Eat a Healthy Diet

Dental implants are strong enough to chew any foods you like, but eating too many sweet, acidic, chewy, or crunchy items can lead to oral infections or gradually loosen your prosthetic devices. To prevent this, be sure to enjoy a balanced diet that is rich with protein, nutrients, and minerals essential to your oral health. It’s also best to be mindful when chewing with your implant to prevent unnecessary wear.

Break Bad Habits

Tobacco use and excessive alcohol consumption are harsh on your gums and may make you more vulnerable to destructive oral infections. Similarly, grinding, using your teeth to open packages or bottles, and chewing on inedible materials like ice, wood, plastic, metal, and fingernails can break or dislodge your real and artificial teeth. It’s best to kick these bad oral habits, and we’ll be happy to fit you with a customized nightguard to protect your smile from grinding.

Protect Your Dental Implants

While dental implants are made from incredibly strong materials like ceramics and titanium, they are not indestructible and can be broken if exposed to too much pressure. When playing sports or riding a bike, it’s best to wear an athletic mouthguard to protect your smile. If you grind your teeth in your sleep, take care to wear a nightguard designed to protect your enamel and implants. Our team can supply you with customized appliances that deliver superior protection.

Schedule Regular Dental Checkups

The American Dental Association recommends seeing your dentist for routine exams and cleanings every six months, and it’s still necessary to keep up with these appointments even if all your teeth have been replaced with dental implants. These visits allow our team in Corinth to carefully monitor the health of your natural and artificial teeth and address any problems in their early stages.

Dental Implant FAQs

How Long Does the Dental Implant Process Take?

From consultation to restoration, the entire treatment usually requires at least 6 months up to a year to complete. This amount of time is necessary because the jawbone needs to envelop the implant post, and new growth needs a little patience. Otherwise, your new prosthetic teeth will not be strong or stable for the long term. Although this option takes a longer time than traditional dentures or bridges, believe us when we say dental implants are worth this relatively short wait!
